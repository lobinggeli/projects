How to use it: send the questions in writing to the BTI (the intercommunal technical office that handles permits for Corseaux), with a sketch of the openings, and keep the written answer. The questions on imputed rent and insurance go to the ACI and the ECA.

Questions for the commune

1

Garage turned into a living room: habitable area

For · BTI · ACI · ECA
  1. Does turning the garage (about 28 m² inside, 4.98 × 5.60 m) into a living room count towards any index or limit of the regulation (PEP footprint ratio, PACom ISB), or only the footprint, which does not change?
  2. Is it a change of use that needs a permit? Which habitability rules apply (minimum height 2.40 m, glazing at least 1/8 of the floor)? Is a height of about 2.35–2.40 m after floor insulation accepted?
  3. Does the declared habitable area change anything for the commune (taxes, connections)?
  4. ACI and ECA: by how much do the imputed rent, the tax valuation and the insured value rise?
What we knowThe PEP ratio and the PACom ISB count footprint, not floor area: in principle no capacity is used.Habitable area would grow from about 192 to 220 m². The heated area grows too: the new floor, walls and glazing must meet current energy rules.
2

Two enclosed parking spaces elsewhere on the parcel

For · BTI
  1. If the garage becomes a living room, can we build an enclosed two-car garage (or a carport) elsewhere on the parcel, for instance near today’s access at the top? Where exactly, and how big?
  2. Is it accepted as a minor outbuilding (RLATC art. 39, about 40 m²) within the boundary distances, or up to the boundary with the neighbour’s written consent (PACom art. 3.3.4)? What maximum height? Is a flat roof allowed (PACom art. 8.6.2)?
  3. How many spaces does the commune require for this house (VSS norms, PACom art. 5.2)? Are the existing outdoor spaces enough, or is there a compensatory tax without covered spaces?
  4. Can we convert the garage now and build the covered spaces later, with a separate permit?
What we knowUnder both regulations a detached garage of about 40 m² is plausible, but it is a Municipality authorisation, not a right.Limits to keep in mind: at least 50% of open soil (PACom), the road building limit at the south corner, the LLavaux sector.
3

Permits: which ones, and which procedure

For · BTI
  1. One permit for everything (façades, change of use of the garage, interior bearing walls, external insulation, heat pump, solar panels, terrace), or several applications?
  2. Public inquiry or exemption (RLATC art. 72d)? Does the project go to the Commission consultative de Lavaux (LLavaux art. 5a)?
  3. Solar panels: simple notice (LAT art. 18a) or permit, in the LLavaux sector? Air-water heat pump: which noise proof is needed?
  4. Which documents are needed: architect’s plans, asbestos survey (building before 1991), CECB or energy proof, engineer’s opinion? How long should we count?
  5. Can interior work (kitchen, bathrooms, floors) start without a permit while the application is processed?
What we knowDefault track is the public inquiry: about 6–9 months, 12–18 if contested.Subsidies (Programme Bâtiments) must be requested before works start.
4

Openings: can we do them as we like?

For · BTI · Lavaux commission
  1. Are there rules on the number, size or proportions of new façade openings (PEP, PACom art. 3.9 on integration)? In practice, does the commune accept:
    • two French doors down to the floor in the living space (2.85 and 2.9 m), cutting the concrete band’s sill;
    • a French door in the main bedroom;
    • a glass bay in place of the garage door, in the existing opening;
    • glass guards, including in place of the upper terrace’s parapet?
    • an alternative for the living space: instead of the French doors and the guard, windows glazed down to the floor, with a fixed laminated-glass lower part (up to about 1 m) and a tilt-and-turn sash at the top? From outside the façade would look almost the same, with a transom at about 1 m.
  2. Are there requirements on frame materials and colours, and on reflective glazing (seen from the lake and the vineyards)?
  3. West and south-west façades, 4.0–4.8 m from the boundaries (less than the regulation’s 6 m): can openings be enlarged there within the existing volume (LATC art. 80)?
What we knowNo rule found bans these openings. The risks are the integration clause and the Lavaux commission, which may want the horizontal band kept as a continuous line.Structurally, the band may be load-bearing: probe it before the application.

Points to check

Base · no garage

  • The old garage becomes a living room: the two covered spaces are gone until a parking option is built. The commune sets the number of spaces (VSS norms, PACom art. 5.2); if the existing outdoor spaces are not enough, it can ask for a compensatory tax.

Parking at the bottom option

  • The garage sits on the edge of the Chemin de Galatry, where the road building line (LRou) runs: an underground structure there needs the commune’s approval (often with a precarity agreement).
  • The road climbs about 20 %% in front of the garage: the paving in front of the doors takes up about ±0.5 m between the two doors, with a drainage grate in front of the garage.
  • At the back, the soil on the slab stands about 1 m above natural ground: within the rules’ 1.50 m fill limit (PEP art. 12).
  • Terrace ready for a pool: lay ducts, drain and power now; the pool will need its own permit later.

Parking at the top option

  • Access at about 21.2 %: steeper than the usual 12–15 % for a private drive (today’s ramp is already about 25 %); a heated ramp or a longer route to study.
  • The garage slab carries up to about 2 m of soil: a heavier structure, with careful waterproofing and drainage.
  • About 3.3 m of cut at the door; the north-east corner is about 1 m from the boundary: rules for underground structures near boundaries to check with the commune.

Glazed south façade

  • The concrete band (−0.64 to +0.85) runs in front of the living and dining room, above the lower-floor openings and the garage: it is probably a deep spandrel beam. Cutting it under the French doors needs a probe and the engineer’s opinion; if it is load-bearing, it needs strengthening (steel beam under the slab, posts or carbon fibre).
  • The upper terrace’s parapet (about 9.5 m) may stiffen the slab edge, which rests on a single pillar: check before demolishing it. It also holds the waterproofing upstand of a terrace that sits over the old garage, which becomes a habitable room: the waterproofing is redone with the new edge.
  • Guards: 1.00 m minimum (SIA 358, drop of about 2.7 m), laminated safety glass, anchored in a slab edge that can take the load. Today’s parapet (0.85 m) is below the current standard.
  • Cheaper alternative for the living space: windows with a fixed laminated-glass lower part and a tilt-and-turn sash at the top, instead of the French doors and the guard. The room has no way out (drop of about 2.7 m), so the result is the same. It drops the 6 m guard (5–11 k) and the thresholds; the estimated saving, to be confirmed by quotes, is about 8–15 k, since cutting and strengthening the band are still needed. To check with the joiner: the fixed part acts as the guard (SIA 358: sized laminated glass, manufacturer’s certificate); 2.85 and 2.9 m widths mean 2 to 3 units per opening; the outer face of the fixed glass cannot be cleaned from inside.
  • With the glazing in the garage opening, almost the whole south band changes: the engineer studies the whole façade at once, including the lower storey’s bracing (earthquake).
  • Permit for the façade change. The parcel is in the LLavaux sector: the commune sends the project to the Commission consultative de Lavaux before the public inquiry (LLavaux art. 5a). Keeping the slab edge as a continuous horizontal line helps the integration (PACom art. 3.9).
  • On the east side, the glass opens the terrace onto the neighbour at n° 5: frosted glass is possible on that side.
  • Main bedroom, kitchen upstairs: the south-west bedroom is in the west wing, outside the band, in a masonry wall; only the sill is lowered, the lintel stays. This corner of the house is about 4.8 m from the south-west neighbour, closer than the regulation’s 6 m: a larger opening inside the existing volume does not worsen the non-conformity (LATC art. 80), but it looks more towards that neighbour.

Roof and solar

  • No roof windows: the roof stays whole, which leaves the whole south slope for the solar panels.
  • For the CHF 100/m² bonus, the panels must cover at least half of the well-exposed roof area.

Pool option

  • The pool is about 4 m from the boundary: possible as a minor outbuilding (art. 39 RLATC) if the commune accepts it and its rules do not forbid it.
  • The pool will need its own permit; the base terrace is already prepared (ducts, drain, power).

Fence option

  • Enclosure walls are banned (PACom): a light fence and a hedge, no wall.
  • Hedges must be mixed native shrub species (PACom art. 4.4).
  • Fence height and the hedge’s distance from the boundary: to check with the commune (rural land code for plantings).
  • Along the road, fences may stand inside the road building line if they do not affect safety (PACom art. 3.2).

What this is

  • Orientation sketches, not an architect’s design: no structure, no costing. The pool, walls and road need a permit (CAMAC). A surveyor’s measured survey is needed before going further.
