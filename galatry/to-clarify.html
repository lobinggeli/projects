Points to check on the project

Base · no garage

  • The old garage becomes a living room: the two covered spaces are gone until a parking option is built. The commune sets the number of spaces (VSS norms, PACom art. 5.2); if the existing outdoor spaces are not enough, it can ask for a compensatory tax.

Parking at the bottom option

  • Garage at the bottom: an underground structure under the terrace, about 1.4 m from the house corner and 1.4 m from the east boundary: a shored dig on both sides, a geotechnical survey, and the agreement of the neighbour at no. 5 for the shoring.
  • The mound below the pool is dug away (about 141 m³): the gravel area comes to about 1.0 m from the south-west boundary, with a wall up to about 2.1 m on that side (neighbour’s agreement and shoring during the dig). Retaining walls of about 34 m in all, up to 3.1 m. The back wall carries the terrace edge and the pool’s south wall: to design together with the pool (foundations at about −6.0).
  • The pool overflows above the gravel area: the gutter and balance tank go into the back wall. Elsewhere, a 1.00 m glass guard on the terrace edge (drop of about 2.9 m, SIA 358).
  • The glazed lobby on the terrace (about 3.8 × 2.1 m) and the terrace extension are seen from the lake: to present to the Lavaux commission. The pool plant room can go in the garage.
  • Carport: on the edge of the Chemin de Galatry, near the east corner, where the road building line (LRou) runs: a structure there needs the commune’s approval (often with a precarity agreement). About 1.5 m from the east boundary, to confirm with the commune and the neighbour at no. 5.
  • Carport: the road climbs about 18 % in front; the platform takes up about ±0.6 m from one end to the other, with a drainage grate. It is dug into the slope: retaining walls of about 19 m, up to 3.5 m at the back on the east side. Clear height about 2.35 m. The footpath runs along its west side: about 32 m to the front door.

Parking at the top option

  • With a door into the house, the garage is no longer an outbuilding but an extension of about 49 m²: under the PEP in force, the COS leaves only about 7–14 m², so it does not fit; under the PACom, the ISB margin of about +60–67 m² is enough. To ask the commune (or wait for the PACom).
  • It stays inside the 6 m zone (about 6.0 m from the boundary at the north-east corner). Without an inside door and set apart from the house, it could pass as an outbuilding (≤ 40 m², RLATC art. 39), but smaller and with an outdoor walk.
  • Access at about 21.2 %: steeper than the usual 12–15 % for a private drive (today’s ramp is already about 25 %); a heated ramp or a longer route to study.
  • The ground rises about 2.5 m behind the garage: its slab carries about 40 cm of soil and meets the slope. Forecourt wall up to about 4.5 m on the north; careful drainage and waterproofing against the house’s north façade.

Glazed south façade

  • The concrete band (−0.64 to +0.85) runs in front of the living and dining room, above the lower-floor openings and the garage: it is probably a deep spandrel beam. Cutting it under the French doors needs a probe and the engineer’s opinion; if it is load-bearing, it needs strengthening (steel beam under the slab, posts or carbon fibre).
  • The upper terrace’s parapet (about 9.5 m) may stiffen the slab edge, which rests on a single pillar: check before demolishing it. It also holds the waterproofing upstand of a terrace that sits over the old garage, which becomes a habitable room: the waterproofing is redone with the new edge.
  • Guards: 1.00 m minimum (SIA 358, drop of about 2.7 m), laminated safety glass, anchored in a slab edge that can take the load. Today’s parapet (0.85 m) is below the current standard.
  • With the glazing in the garage opening, almost the whole south band changes: the engineer studies the whole façade at once, including the lower storey’s bracing (earthquake).
  • Permit for the façade change. The parcel is in the LLavaux sector: the commune sends the project to the Commission consultative de Lavaux before the public inquiry (LLavaux art. 5a). Keeping the slab edge as a continuous horizontal line helps the integration (PACom art. 3.9).
  • On the east side, the glass opens the terrace onto the neighbour at n° 5: frosted glass is possible on that side.
  • Main bedroom, kitchen upstairs: the south-west bedroom is in the west wing, outside the band, in a masonry wall; only the sill is lowered, the lintel stays. This corner of the house is about 4.8 m from the south-west neighbour, closer than the regulation’s 6 m: a larger opening inside the existing volume does not worsen the non-conformity (LATC art. 80), but it looks more towards that neighbour.
  • Cheaper alternative for the living space: windows with a fixed laminated-glass lower part and a tilt-and-turn sash at the top, instead of the French doors and the guard. The room has no way out (drop of about 2.7 m), so the result is the same. It drops the 6 m guard (≈ 6 k) and the thresholds; the estimated saving, to be confirmed by quotes, is about 10 k, since cutting and strengthening the band are still needed. To check with the joiner: the fixed part acts as the guard (SIA 358: sized laminated glass, manufacturer’s certificate); 2.85 and 2.9 m widths mean 2 to 3 units per opening; the outer face of the fixed glass cannot be cleaned from inside.
  • 30 cm south façade wall: drill a core through it to see what is inside (reinforced concrete or masonry, rebar, insulation, air gap). Its make-up decides how the openings are cut, what strengthening is needed and how to insulate.

Roof and solar

  • No roof windows: the roof stays whole, which leaves the whole south slope for the solar panels.
  • For the CHF 100/m² bonus, the panels must cover at least half of the well-exposed roof area.

Pool option

  • The pool is about 3.2 m from the south-west boundary. The new PACom (not yet in force) allows pools down to 3 m from the boundary; the buried gutter can come closer. Under the current rules: a minor outbuilding (art. 39 RLATC), if the commune accepts it. A thin margin: to confirm with the surveyor’s measured survey.
  • The pool will need its own permit; the base terrace is already prepared (ducts, drain, power).
  • Infinity edge: an open 21 m² pool, so outside the footprint count (ISB, open pools under 40 m²). The south edge must be perfectly level; the water drops into a gutter and on to a balance tank (about 2–3 m³) under the terrace or in the plant room.
  • Beyond the edge, the ground stays almost flat for 2–3 m before it falls: for the water to merge with the lake seen from the house, that strip must be lowered or planted low. To work out with the landscape designer and the pool builder.

Fence option

  • Enclosure walls are banned (PACom): a light fence and a hedge, no wall.
  • Hedges must be mixed native shrub species (PACom art. 4.4).
  • Fence height and the hedge’s distance from the boundary: to check with the commune (rural land code for plantings).
  • Along the road, fences may stand inside the road building line if they do not affect safety (PACom art. 3.2).

What this is

  • Orientation sketches, not an architect’s design: no structure, no costing. The pool, walls and road need a permit (CAMAC). A surveyor’s measured survey is needed before going further.
