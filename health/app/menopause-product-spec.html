3. Perimenopause vs Menopause

Why Two Approaches? Perimenopause is characterized by fluctuating hormones — a single snapshot is often misleading. Menopause has stable low hormone levels — single sample sufficient.

Perimenopause Panel

Target UserStill menstruating, irregular, 40-52
Sample TimingDay 14 + Day 21
Kit Contents2 kits, 2 envelopes
Key InsightHormone fluctuation pattern
FSH InterpretationTrack variation between samples
Price€99

Menopause Panel

Target User12+ months no period, 45-58
Sample TimingAny time
Kit Contents1 kit
Key InsightBaseline menopausal levels
FSH InterpretationSingle elevated reading diagnostic
Price€69-89

Perimenopause Sample Timing Guide

Cycle Day 1 = First day of period

Day 14 (±2 days): Ovulation Sample
├── FSH should dip if ovulation occurring
├── E2 should peak
└── LH surge indicates ovulation

Day 21 (±2 days): Luteal Sample
├── Progesterone should rise if ovulation occurred
├── E2 moderately elevated
└── FSH should be lower than Day 14

If cycles are very irregular (>35 days or <21 days),
provide alternative timing guidance in quiz flow.

4. AI Insights Specifications

1. Menopause Stage Assessment

Determines where user is in transition: Early Peri → Late Peri → Early Post → Established Post

"Based on your FSH (42 mIU/mL) and estradiol (18 pg/mL), combined with your reported 14 months without a period, your results are consistent with postmenopause."
2. Symptom-Biomarker Correlation

Links logged symptoms to specific biomarker results

"Your FSH/E2 ratio of 2.3 is associated with more frequent vasomotor symptoms. Women with similar profiles report improvement with lifestyle modifications or HRT."
3. Thyroid Ruling

Confirms whether symptoms are hormonal or thyroid-related

"Your thyroid function is normal. This suggests your fatigue and mood changes are more likely related to hormonal menopause transition rather than thyroid dysfunction."
4. CV Risk Contextualization

Compares cardiovascular markers to pre-menopause baseline

"Your total cholesterol has increased from 185 to 218 mg/dL over the past year. This 18% increase is common during menopause due to declining estrogen."
5. HRT Optimization

For HRT users: assesses if therapy is effective

"Your estradiol trough level (28 pg/mL) is at the lower end of the therapeutic range. If you're still experiencing symptoms, your doctor may consider adjusting your HRT dose."
6. Lifestyle Recommendations

Personalized actionable recommendations

"Increase vitamin D intake: 2000-4000 IU daily. Your HbA1c (5.9%) suggests prediabetes. Consider reducing refined carbohydrates."
