Psychographic Profile

Experiencing hormonal changes with real impact on daily life — 87% of untreated menopausal women report symptoms (ELISA study, N=5,004). Frustrated by a healthcare system that largely ignores menopause: 44% have never discussed symptoms with a doctor, 50% feel unable to discuss it even with a partner.

The cultural taboo is breaking rapidly — a 2025 French parliamentary report proposed 25 measures, and HAS is updating menopause guidelines (first update since the 1970s) expected in 2026. Already spending on supplements: 70%+ of menopausal women in Western Europe use dietary supplements.

Looking for evidence-based tools in a market flooded with unvalidated apps (236 menopause apps exist globally, fewer than a third are evidence-based).

Understanding Your Menopause Biomarkers

Our panels measure the hormones and health markers that matter most during menopause. Here's what each biomarker tells you about your body.

FSH (Follicle-Stimulating Hormone)

Measures: Ovarian function
The primary marker for menopause. FSH rises as your ovaries produce less estrogen, signaling your brain to work harder. Levels above 30 mIU/mL typically indicate menopause.
Optimal: <10 mIU/mL (pre-menopause) | Post-menopause: >30 mIU/mL

Estradiol (E2)

Measures: Primary estrogen level
Declining estradiol causes most menopause symptoms: hot flashes, sleep issues, mood changes. Tracking E2 helps explain your symptoms and monitor HRT effectiveness.
Pre-menopause: 30-400 pg/mL | Post-menopause: <30 pg/mL

TSH (Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone)

Measures: Thyroid function
Thyroid problems can mimic menopause symptoms—fatigue, weight gain, mood changes. Testing TSH rules out thyroid issues as the cause of your symptoms.
Optimal: 0.4-4.0 mIU/L

Vitamin D

Measures: Bone health marker
30% of postmenopausal women develop osteoporosis. Vitamin D is essential for calcium absorption and bone strength. 70% of French women are deficient.
Optimal: 30-50 ng/mL | Deficient: <20 ng/mL

HbA1c

Measures: Blood sugar control (3-month average)
Insulin resistance increases during menopause due to hormonal changes. HbA1c catches metabolic risk early, before diabetes develops.
Optimal: <5.7% | Prediabetes: 5.7-6.4%

Total Cholesterol

Measures: Cardiovascular risk
Cardiovascular risk increases 50% after menopause as estrogen's protective effect declines. LDL cholesterol typically rises 10-15% during the transition.
Optimal: <200 mg/dL | High: >240 mg/dL

HRT Monitoring: Supporting Your Hormone Therapy

If you're on hormone replacement therapy, regular monitoring helps ensure you're getting the right dose. Our HRT add-on provides the biomarkers your doctor needs.

Why Monitor HRT?

Estrogen levels vary significantly between women on the same dose, especially with transdermal (patch/gel) delivery. Testing helps optimize your therapy for symptom relief while minimizing risks.

What We Test

Estradiol trough level (before your next dose), progesterone (if on combined HRT), plus liver function markers (ALT, AST) and triglycerides to monitor for rare side effects.

+€29 add-on

When to Test

3 months after starting or changing HRT, then every 6-12 months. For transdermal estrogen, test just before applying your next patch or dose for accurate trough levels.
