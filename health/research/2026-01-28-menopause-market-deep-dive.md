## 4. Clinical Studies & Guidelines Summary

### Diagnostic Criteria

**FSH Threshold:**
- FSH >30 mIU/mL = diagnostic for menopause (when combined with 12+ months amenorrhea)
- FSH 25-30 mIU/mL = late perimenopause
- FSH levels fluctuate significantly in perimenopause (single reading insufficient)

**Estradiol Levels:**
- Premenopausal: 30-400 pg/mL (cycle-dependent)
- Perimenopausal: highly variable
- Postmenopausal: <30 pg/mL

### Clinical Guidelines Position

**ACOG (American College of OB-GYN):**
- Menopause is a clinical diagnosis (12 months no period in woman >45)
- Routine hormone testing NOT recommended for diagnosis in typical presentation
- Testing useful for: atypical cases, POI, HRT monitoring

**NAMS (North American Menopause Society):**
- Symptom-based approach for diagnosis
- Testing recommended when: age <45, surgical menopause, HRT optimization

**When Testing IS Clinically Useful:**
1. **Premature Ovarian Insufficiency (POI):** Women <40 with menopause symptoms
2. **HRT Monitoring:** Especially transdermal estrogen users (blood levels vary)
3. **Differential Diagnosis:** Rule out thyroid dysfunction, PCOS
4. **Perimenopausal Uncertainty:** Women 40-45 with irregular cycles
5. **Contraception Decisions:** When to stop birth control safely

### Bone Health Connection
- 30% of postmenopausal women develop osteoporosis
- Bone loss accelerates in first 5-7 years post-menopause
- Vitamin D status critical (70% of French women insufficient)

### Cardiovascular Risk
- CV risk increases 50% post-menopause
- LDL typically rises 10-15%
- ApoB may be better predictor than LDL in menopausal transition

### Metabolic Changes
- Insulin resistance increases with estrogen decline
- HbA1c may rise 0.1-0.2% during transition
- Abdominal fat accumulation (visceral) common
