# Existing Building Modifications — What Can You Change? (Vaud / communal law)

Use this framework to check whether a planned change to an **existing** building is allowed (urbanism: communal regulation, cantonal law, overlays) and buildable (civil engineering: structure, seismic, envelope). It covers façade openings, bay windows, garage conversions, wall removals, raised sills and similar work. It does not cover new construction or extensions; for those, start from the zone's capacity rules in [Corseaux Planning & Zoning References](../real-estate/corseaux-planning-zoning-references.md).

## Core Concept

Every change to an existing building has to pass **two independent filters**. A "yes" on one says nothing about the other:

```
                     ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  Planned change ──► │ 1. URBANISM: is it allowed?                  │
                     │    zone rules · existing-building status ·   │
                     │    aesthetics/overlays · use · permit track  │
                     └──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                     ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                ──►  │ 2. CIVIL ENGINEERING: can it be built safely? │
                     │    load path · seismic bracing · envelope ·  │
                     │    habitability · hazardous materials        │
                     └──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                                  ▼
          Verdict per item: YES / YES IF (conditions) / UNLIKELY / NO
```

Most façade and interior changes **do not use any building capacity** (COS/ISB, height, distances), because the volume doesn't change. The real constraints are then: the **existing-building regime** (if the building is non-conforming), **aesthetics and overlays** (Lavaux, heritage, municipal integration clause), **change of use** (garage → habitable room) and **structure**.

## When to Apply

- Before an offer on a house you plan to transform: test each planned change on the list
- When a renovation sketch adds or widens openings, removes walls or converts a non-habitable room
- When deciding whether a change needs a full public inquiry, a dispensed permit or nothing at all
- When briefing an architect or engineer: hand them the open questions this framework produces

## Step 1 — Identify the Rules That Apply to the Parcel

| Layer | What to get | Where (Vaud) |
|---|---|---|
| Communal zoning in force | Zone, regulation articles on existing buildings, architecture, openings, roofs, parking | RDPPF extract (rdppf.vd.ch), commune / technical office (e.g. BTI for Corseaux) |
| Pending revision | Draft PACom zone + articles; check whether "effet anticipé" applies | Commune website, public inquiry files |
| Cantonal law | LATC / RLATC (permit, existing buildings, habitability), LVLEne (energy) | BLV (prestations.vd.ch/pub/blv-publication) |
| Overlays | LLavaux, heritage inventory (recensement architectural note), ISOS, natural hazards | RDPPF, geo.vd.ch, the regulation's plans |
| Private law | Servitudes, PPE rules, neighbours' rights | Land registry extract |

**Rule of thumb**: when the commune is mid-revision, test each change against **both** the regulation in force and the draft, and keep the stricter answer until the new one is approved.

## Step 2 — Classify the Building: Conforming or Not?

Measure the existing building against the current zone rules (distances, height, footprint ratio, number of storeys).

- **Conforming**: transformations are allowed within the zone rules.
- **Non-conforming** (e.g. too close to a boundary under today's rules): in Vaud, **LATC art. 80** applies. The building may be maintained and transformed, and moderately extended, **as long as the non-conformity is not aggravated**. Work that stays inside the existing volume (new openings, interior changes) is normally fine. Work that adds height or volume on the non-conforming side usually isn't.
- **Protected object** (recensement architectural note 1–3, inventory, classified): heritage authorisation or préavis from the canton is needed, and changes to façades are hard.

## Step 3 — Run Each Planned Change Through the Urbanism Checklist

| Question | Typical rule (Vaud) | What triggers a "no" |
|---|---|---|
| Does it change footprint, height, volume or distances? | Zone capacity (COS / ISB), h/H, d/D | Any increase on a non-conforming side, or above the cap |
| Does it change the **façade**? | Permit required (LATC art. 103). Municipality may dispense the public inquiry for minor work (RLATC art. 72d) | — (it's a procedure question, not a ban) |
| Is the result **well integrated**? | Communal architecture clause (e.g. PACom Corseaux art. 3.9), LATC aesthetics clause | Form, proportions or materials that "compromise the harmony of the place" |
| Is the parcel in an **overlay**? | LLavaux art. 5a al. 3: projects go to the **Commission consultative de Lavaux** before the public inquiry, except minor work that doesn't alter the site. LLavaux art. 22: works must fit the site | Strong visual change in a protected landscape |
| Does the **use** change (garage, cellar, attic → habitable)? | Habitability: room height (RLATC: 2.40 m minimum, check the current wording), daylight (window area ≥ 1/8 of floor area, check), ventilation | Ceiling too low, no daylight, buried room |
| Does a **parking space** disappear? | Commune sets needs on VSS norms (e.g. PACom Corseaux art. 5.2); may waive against a compensatory tax | Loss of required spaces with no replacement |
| Does it touch the **energy envelope**? | LVLEne / MoPEC: new or renewed elements meet current U-values; a newly heated room joins the envelope | Can't be insulated to the required standard |
| Are there **private-law** limits? | Servitudes (view, building height), PPE rules | Servitude forbidding the change |

## Step 4 — Run Each Change Through the Civil Engineering Checklist

| Topic | Question for the engineer | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Load path** | What does the wall or band above the opening carry (slab, roof, interior bearing wall)? | Widening an opening means a new lintel or beam, temporary propping and possibly new posts and footings |
| **Existing lintel** | Is the existing opening already spanned by a beam? | Glazing **inside an existing opening** is light work. **Widening** or **heightening** it is structural work |
| **Seismic bracing** | How much bracing wall length remains in each direction, per storey? (SIA 261 / SIA 269/8 for existing buildings) | Removing façade piers and interior walls on the same storey can create a **soft storey**, especially on a lower floor that's buried on one side and glazed on the other |
| **Deflection** | Is the new beam stiff enough for large glass (sliding panels need very small deflection)? | Glass and sliding tracks jam or crack if the lintel sags |
| **Edge bands and parapets** | Is the concrete band or parapet only cladding, or is it a structural (deep) beam? | Cutting a structural band to lower a sill can remove the beam carrying the floor above |
| **Fall protection** | Is the new sill below 1.00 m with a drop behind it? (SIA 358) | Floor-to-ceiling glass on an upper floor needs a guardrail (glass or railing) |
| **Envelope** | Thermal bridge at the slab edge, threshold waterproofing at garden level, drainage in front of the opening | A garden-level opening without a raised threshold and drain lets water in |
| **Summer comfort** | Exterior solar protection on large south glazing (SIA 180) | Overheating; the blinds are also visible on the façade (aesthetics) |
| **Hazardous materials** | Asbestos (and PCB) diagnostic for buildings before 1991 | Required in Vaud for permit-subject work on pre-1991 buildings; affects cost and planning |
| **Original drawings** | Get the original permit file and, if possible, the engineer's reinforcement plans | Avoids destructive investigation; otherwise plan probe openings (sondages) |

## Step 5 — Permit Track and Sequencing

1. **Ask the commune first**: a short written question with a sketch, sent to the technical office before the purchase or the project. It's cheap and gives a written position.
2. **Overlay review** (if any): in the Lavaux perimeter, the Municipality sends the project to the Commission consultative de Lavaux before the public inquiry.
3. **CAMAC filing**: full public inquiry, or dispensed if the Municipality judges it minor (see [Renovation Permits & Costs in Vaud](../real-estate/renovation-permits-costs-vaud.md)).
4. **Engineer's verification** before execution: load path, seismic check, lintel design, propping plan.
5. **Subsidies**: ask the Programme Bâtiments **before** starting, because a request filed after work begins is refused.

## Output Format (per planned change)

| Change | Urbanism | Civil engineering | Verdict | Open questions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| e.g. glazing in the garage opening | Permit (façade + change of use), no capacity used | Existing lintel kept, light work | **YES IF** room height ≥ 2.40 m and parking replaced | Measure height under slab; ask commune about parking |

## Common Pitfalls

- **Assuming "inside the existing volume" means "no permit"**: any façade change needs a permit in Vaud, even if it's dispensed from the public inquiry.
- **Forgetting the change of use**: turning a garage into a living room is a change of use. It brings in habitability rules, energy rules and parking needs, not only the façade.
- **Treating each opening alone**: the engineer looks at the whole storey. Three "small" wall removals on the same floor can add up to a seismic problem.
- **Cutting a concrete band to lower a sill** without knowing whether it's structural.
- **Forgetting the guardrail** on floor-to-ceiling glass above a drop.
- **Ignoring the visual review** in Lavaux: the commission judges proportions, reflections and how the façade reads from the lake and the vineyards.

## Limitations

This is a method, not legal advice or a structural verification. Article numbers for LATC/RLATC habitability rules (room height, daylight) and RLATC art. 72d come from general knowledge and should be checked against the current BLV text. Structural statements are rules of thumb: only a civil engineer (SIA 260 series, SIA 269 for existing structures) can confirm what a wall or band carries.
