# Hormone Balance Assessment Framework

Use this framework to evaluate hormone relationships, ratios, and overall endocrine balance rather than isolated biomarker values.

## Core Concept

Hormones function as an interconnected network, not independent variables. A single hormone "out of range" is less informative than understanding the balance and relationships between hormones. This framework focuses on ratios, feedback loops, and system-level patterns to identify root causes and prioritize interventions.

### The Hormone Orchestra Principle

Think of hormones as an orchestra:
- Individual instruments (hormones) can be "in tune" (normal range)
- But the orchestra can still sound off if balance is wrong
- Ratios reveal the harmony or discord between hormones
- One section too loud drowns out others (dominance patterns)
- The conductor (hypothalamus/pituitary) coordinates everything

## When to Apply

- When interpreting comprehensive hormone panels
- When isolated values are "normal" but symptoms persist
- To identify root causes (vs. just describing symptoms)
- Before hormone replacement or supplementation decisions
- To track response to interventions over time
- When multiple hormonal systems seem involved

## Key Hormone Relationships

### Cortisol : DHEA-S Ratio (Stress-Recovery Balance)

**What it reveals:** Catabolic (breakdown) vs. Anabolic (building) balance

**Calculation:**
```
Cortisol:DHEA-S Ratio = [Morning Cortisol (ng/mL)] / [DHEA-S (ng/mL)]
```

**Optimal range:** 5:1 to 6:1 (some sources: 5-6)

**Example:**
- Cortisol: 15 ng/mL
- DHEA-S: 3 ng/mL
- Ratio: 15/3 = 5:1 ✓ Optimal

#### High Ratio (Cortisol Dominant)

**Ratio > 6:1**

**Interpretation:**
- Chronic stress physiology dominates
- Tissue breakdown exceeds rebuilding
- "Running on stress hormones"
- Accelerated aging physiology

**Symptoms:**
- Muscle loss despite exercise
- Central weight gain (visceral fat)
- Poor recovery from workouts
- Immune suppression (frequent colds)
- Cognitive decline (brain fog, memory issues)
- Mood disorders (depression, anxiety)
- Bone density loss
- Insulin resistance

**Common causes:**
- Prolonged psychological stress
- Chronic inflammation
- Overtraining without recovery
- Sleep deprivation
- Caloric restriction + high stress
- Chronic illness

**Intervention priorities:**
1. Stress reduction (non-negotiable)
2. Sleep optimization
3. DHEA support (if DHEA-S is low)
4. Reduce cortisol if elevated (adaptogens, phosphatidylserine)
5. Anti-inflammatory diet and lifestyle

#### Low Ratio (DHEA Dominant or Low Cortisol)

**Ratio < 5:1**

**Interpretation:**
- DHEA relatively high OR cortisol depleted
- If cortisol is low: HPA axis exhaustion
- If DHEA high: Possible PCOS or DHEA supplementation

**Symptoms (if low cortisol driven):**
- Morning fatigue, can't get going
- Crashes after stress
- Salt cravings
- Lightheadedness when standing
- Hypoglycemia symptoms

**Symptoms (if high DHEA driven):**
- Oily skin, acne
- Increased body hair (women)
- Anxiety or irritability

**Intervention priorities:**
- If low cortisol: Gentle HPA axis support (see Cortisol Analysis framework)
- If high DHEA: Investigate PCOS, stop DHEA supplements

### Progesterone : Estrogen Ratio (P:E Ratio)

**What it reveals:** Estrogen dominance vs. progesterone sufficiency

**Calculation (saliva or serum):**
```
P:E Ratio = [Progesterone (pg/mL)] / [Estradiol (pg/mL)]
```

**Optimal range:**
- Luteal phase: 100:1 to 500:1 (saliva)
- Blood: Different ranges; follow lab-specific guidelines

**Note:** Must be measured in luteal phase (days 19-21 of cycle) for women

#### Low P:E Ratio (Estrogen Dominance)

**Interpretation:**
- Progesterone insufficient to balance estrogen
- Very common in perimenopause
- Can occur with normal estrogen if progesterone is low

**Symptoms:**
- Heavy menstrual bleeding
- Breast tenderness
- PMS symptoms (irritability, mood swings)
- Fibrocystic breasts
- Uterine fibroids
- Weight gain (hips, thighs)
- Water retention
- Anxiety
- Insomnia (especially mid-cycle)

**Common causes:**
- Anovulatory cycles (no ovulation = no progesterone)
- Perimenopause
- Chronic stress (cortisol steals progesterone precursors)
- Xenoestrogen exposure (plastics, pesticides)
- Low body fat / overtraining (amenorrhea)
- Thyroid dysfunction

**Intervention priorities:**
1. Support ovulation (if anovulatory)
2. Stress reduction (cortisol robs progesterone pathways)
3. Reduce xenoestrogen exposure
4. Support estrogen metabolism (DIM, cruciferous vegetables)
5. Consider bioidentical progesterone (medical supervision)

#### High P:E Ratio (Estrogen Deficiency or Progesterone Excess)

**Interpretation:**
- Estrogen too low OR progesterone too high
- Common in menopause or over-supplementation

**Symptoms (if low estrogen):**
- Hot flashes
- Vaginal dryness
- Mood changes, depression
- Low libido
- Bone density loss
- Dry skin, hair loss

**Symptoms (if excess progesterone):**
- Drowsiness, fatigue
- Depression
- Bloating
- Reduced libido

---

### Free T3 : Reverse T3 Ratio (Thyroid Function)

**What it reveals:** Active thyroid hormone vs. inactive form

**Calculation:**
```
FT3:RT3 Ratio = [Free T3 (pg/mL)] / [Reverse T3 (ng/dL)] × 10
```

**Optimal range:** > 20 (some sources > 0.2 depending on units)

**Note:** Requires both Free T3 and Reverse T3 testing (not always included in standard thyroid panel)

#### Low Ratio (Thyroid Resistance or Low T3 Syndrome)

**Interpretation:**
- Too much T4 converting to inactive RT3 instead of active T3
- "Euthyroid sick syndrome" in chronic illness
- Tissue-level hypothyroidism despite "normal" TSH

**Symptoms:**
- Fatigue despite normal TSH
- Cold intolerance
- Weight gain or difficulty losing weight
- Hair loss
- Depression
- Constipation
- Low basal body temperature

**Common causes:**
- Chronic stress (high cortisol)
- Chronic inflammation
- Caloric restriction / low-carb diets
- Leptin resistance
- Iron, selenium, or zinc deficiency
- Chronic illness
- Heavy metal toxicity

**Intervention priorities:**
1. Address underlying inflammation or stress
2. Optimize micronutrients (selenium, zinc, iron)
3. Moderate calorie/carb intake (not extreme restriction)
4. Consider T3 replacement if persistent (medical supervision)

---

### Testosterone : Estrogen Balance (Men)

**What it reveals:** Aromatization and androgen balance in men

**Typical pattern in aging men:**
- Testosterone declines
- Estrogen increases (from aromatization of testosterone)
- Double negative effect

**Symptoms of imbalance:**
- Low libido
- Erectile dysfunction
- Gynecomastia (breast tissue)
- Abdominal weight gain
- Fatigue
- Depression
- Reduced muscle mass

**Common causes:**
- Obesity (fat tissue produces aromatase enzyme)
- Zinc deficiency
- Excess alcohol
- Age-related testosterone decline
- Insulin resistance

**Intervention priorities:**
1. Weight loss (reduces aromatase activity)
2. Zinc supplementation
3. Reduce alcohol
4. Resistance training
5. Consider aromatase inhibitors or testosterone replacement (medical supervision)

---

### Estrogen Metabolite Ratios (Women)

**What it reveals:** How estrogen is being metabolized (protective vs. harmful pathways)

**Key metabolites:**
- **2-OH Estrogen**: Protective, anti-cancer
- **4-OH Estrogen**: Potentially harmful, pro-cancer
- **16-OH Estrogen**: Proliferative, pro-cancer

**Optimal:**
- High 2-OH:16-OH ratio (>2:1)
- Low 4-OH estrogen

**Testing:** Requires urine hormone metabolite testing (DUTCH test or similar)

**To optimize metabolism:**
- Cruciferous vegetables (DIM, I3C)
- Flaxseeds
- Exercise
- Avoid alcohol (impairs metabolism)
- Support methylation (B vitamins, folate)
- Reduce toxin exposure

## Limitations

- Hormones fluctuate; single test = snapshot, not full picture
- Ratios are guides, not absolute thresholds
- Optimal ranges vary by individual, age, and life stage
- Cannot replace clinical diagnosis or medical evaluation
- Root causes may be outside hormonal system (structural, infectious, etc.)
- Interventions affect multiple hormones; complex to predict all effects
- Reference ranges and ratio targets vary by source and lab

**Remember**: Hormone balance is dynamic and individualized. This framework supports pattern recognition and informed discussions with healthcare providers, not self-diagnosis or self-treatment. Complex cases require endocrinology or functional medicine expertise.
