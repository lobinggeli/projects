# Saliva Testing Guide

Use this guide to determine when saliva testing is appropriate, how to prepare for accurate results, and how to interpret what saliva tests reveal.

## Core Concept

Saliva testing measures **free, bioactive hormones** - the fraction that's unbound to carrier proteins and directly available to your cells. Unlike blood tests that measure total hormones (bound + free), saliva reflects what's actually affecting your tissues at the moment of collection.

### Why Saliva Testing

**Key Advantages:**
- Measures bioavailable hormones (free fraction)
- Non-invasive, painless collection
- Easy repeated sampling for pattern tracking
- Can be done at home at specific times
- Ideal for diurnal rhythm assessment
- No "white coat" stress effect
- Stable samples (can be mailed)

**What Makes It Different:**
- Blood ≈ Total hormone pool (mostly bound, some free)
- Saliva ≈ Free hormones (actively affecting cells)
- Urine ≈ Metabolic breakdown products (what's been processed)

## When to Use Saliva Testing

### Ideal Use Cases

✅ **Cortisol Pattern Assessment**
- Best method for diurnal cortisol rhythm (4-point testing)
- Cortisol Awakening Response (CAR) evaluation
- HPA axis function assessment
- Stress and burnout evaluation

✅ **Sex Hormone Monitoring**
- Testosterone (free, bioactive)
- Estradiol (E2)
- Progesterone
- DHEA
- Especially useful during hormone replacement therapy

✅ **Circadian Rhythm Evaluation**
- Melatonin production patterns
- Sleep disorder investigation
- Shift work impact assessment

✅ **Multiple Time-Point Tracking**
- Any hormone that fluctuates throughout the day
- Before/after intervention comparisons
- Monthly cycle tracking (estrogen, progesterone)

### When Blood Testing May Be Better

🩸 **Use blood instead for:**
- Thyroid hormones (TSH, Free T3, Free T4, antibodies)
- Vitamin D, B12, minerals
- Cholesterol, triglycerides
- Complete blood count (CBC)
- Liver and kidney function
- Insulin, glucose (though urine/CGM also useful)
- When diagnostic criteria require serum values

### When Urine Testing May Be Better

💧 **Use urine instead for:**
- Hormone metabolites (how hormones are broken down)
- 24-hour total hormone output
- Estrogen metabolism pathways
- Kidney function markers
- Glucose and ketones
- Toxic metal exposure

### Combination Testing Strategy

🔬 **Use multiple modalities for complete picture:**
- Saliva: Real-time free hormone snapshot + patterns
- Blood: Total hormone pool + non-hormonal markers
- Urine: Metabolic processing + 24-hour averages

## Biomarkers Measurable in Saliva

### Validated & Reliable
- **Cortisol** ⭐ (Gold standard method)
- **DHEA & DHEA-S**
- **Testosterone** (total and free)
- **Estradiol (E2)**
- **Progesterone**
- **Estriol (E3)**
- **Melatonin**
- **17-OH Progesterone**

### Emerging/Research
- **Secretory IgA** (immune function)
- **Alpha-amylase** (stress marker)
- **Inflammatory markers** (CRP, IL-6)
- **Insulin** (experimental)

### Not Recommended
- ❌ Thyroid hormones (insufficient validation)
- ❌ Vitamin levels (not reliable in saliva)
- ❌ Blood sugar (use blood/urine/CGM)

## Pre-Test Preparation Protocol

### 24-48 Hours Before Test

**Maintain normal routine:**
- Don't change diet, exercise, or sleep significantly
- Continue regular medications and supplements (note any hormonal ones)
- Avoid starting new supplements
- Document any unusual stress or events

**Note these factors (they affect results):**
- Quality and duration of sleep
- Major stressors in past 24-48 hours
- Menstrual cycle day (if applicable)
- Recent illness or inflammation
- Intense exercise timing

### Day of Test

**Morning of test day:**
- Set alarm if needed for first sample (waking cortisol is critical)
- Prepare collection tubes and labels night before
- Have water ready (plain water only)

**General rules (all samples unless specified otherwise):**

❌ **Do NOT**:
- Eat or drink (except water) 30 min before collection
- Brush teeth or floss before morning sample
- Use mouthwash or whitening products
- Smoke or use tobacco
- Consume caffeine, alcohol (affects cortisol)
- Exercise vigorously before collection
- Get blood drawn the same day (stress affects cortisol)
- Have dental work within 24 hours (bleeding risk)

✅ **Do**:
- Rinse mouth with water if needed
- Wait 30 min after rinsing before collecting
- Collect at precise times (especially for cortisol)
- Use passive drool method (don't force saliva)
- Fill to the line indicated on tube

### Timing Guidelines by Hormone

#### Cortisol (4-Point Testing)
**Critical: Timing precision matters**

- **Point 1**: Within 30 min of waking, BEFORE getting out of bed
  - Set alarm if needed
  - Have tube ready on nightstand
  - Don't snooze, don't check phone first

- **Point 2**: 30-45 min after Point 1 (CAR measurement)
  - Can be out of bed, but no food/caffeine yet

- **Point 3**: Midday (typically 12-1 PM)
  - Before lunch or 2+ hours after

- **Point 4**: Before bed (typically 10-11 PM)
  - As close to bedtime as possible

**Note exact collection times** - they matter for interpretation

#### Sex Hormones (Testosterone, Estrogen, Progesterone)

**For women - Timing by cycle:**
- **Follicular phase**: Days 3-5 of cycle (Day 1 = first day of period)
- **Ovulation**: Day 12-14 (for fertility assessment)
- **Luteal phase**: Days 19-21 (for progesterone peak)
- **Postmenopausal**: Any time

**For men:**
- Morning sample (testosterone peaks in AM)
- Consistent timing for tracking over time

**For HRT monitoring:**
- Time relative to dose (consult provider)
- Transdermal: 12-24 hours after application
- Sublingual: 1-2 hours after dose
- Troches: 4-6 hours after dose

#### DHEA
- Morning sample preferred
- Can be collected with cortisol Point 1 or 2

#### Melatonin
- Evening/night sample
- Typically 9-11 PM for evening levels
- Or upon waking if testing morning suppression

## Collection Technique

### Standard Saliva Collection (Passive Drool)

1. **Prepare**:
   - Rinse mouth with water
   - Wait 10 minutes
   - Sit calmly for 5 minutes (stress affects cortisol)

2. **Collect**:
   - Remove tube cap
   - Lean forward slightly
   - Let saliva pool in mouth
   - Let it drip into tube (passive drool)
   - Don't force, cough, or spit forcefully
   - Fill to the line (usually 1-2 mL)

3. **Store**:
   - Cap tube immediately
   - Label with name, date, time, sample number
   - Refrigerate until shipping
   - Ship with ice pack as directed

### Troubleshooting Low Saliva Flow

**If saliva won't flow:**
- Think about favorite foods (stimulates production)
- Gently massage salivary glands (under jaw, in front of ears)
- Don't use citrus swabs (can affect results)
- Chew-and-spit methods may be allowed (check lab protocol)
- Ensure adequate hydration day before

**If consistently dry mouth:**
- May indicate dehydration or medication side effect
- Note this on lab form
- Consider blood testing as alternative

## Common Contamination Issues

### Avoid These Contaminants

⚠️ **Blood** (most common issue):
- From aggressive brushing, flossing
- Gum disease or recent dental work
- Biting tongue or cheek
- **Solution**: Visual check for pink/red saliva; recollect if present

⚠️ **Food particles**:
- From eating too close to collection
- **Solution**: Wait 30+ min after eating

⚠️ **Beverages**:
- Coffee, tea, alcohol can affect hormones
- **Solution**: Water only 30 min before

⚠️ **Supplements/medications**:
- Some dissolve in mouth (lozenges, sublingual)
- **Solution**: Take after collection or 2+ hours before

⚠️ **Cosmetics**:
- Lip balm, lipstick can contaminate
- **Solution**: Clean lips before collection

## Interpretation Considerations

### Factors That Affect Results

**Temporary elevations in cortisol:**
- Poor sleep the night before
- Stress within hours of test
- Caffeine before collection
- Intense exercise same day
- Traffic stress driving to lab

**Temporary reductions in cortisol:**
- Alcohol consumption (24 hours)
- Corticosteroid medications
- Cannabis use

**Sex hormone fluctuations:**
- Cycle timing in women (must note)
- Sleep deprivation (lowers testosterone)
- Stress (affects all sex hormones)

### Understanding Reference Ranges

**Saliva-specific ranges:**
- Different from blood reference ranges
- Expressed in ng/mL or pg/mL (smaller amounts)
- Time-specific ranges (morning cortisol ≠ evening cortisol)
- Method-specific (ELISA vs. LC-MS/MS)

**Optimal vs. Normal:**
- "Normal" = 95% of population
- "Optimal" = Where most people feel best
- Often optimal is in upper 25% for DHEA, lower 25% for evening cortisol

### When to Retest

**Retest if:**
- Results don't match symptoms at all
- Suspected collection error
- Contamination concerns (blood visible)
- Timing was significantly off
- Major stressor occurred during test day

**Don't immediately retest if:**
- Just one value slightly out of range
- Pattern makes sense with symptoms
- No obvious collection error

**Follow-up testing timeline:**
- After interventions: 6-12 weeks minimum
- Tracking hormone therapy: per provider recommendation (often monthly initially)
- Monitoring chronic conditions: Every 3-6 months

## Limitations

- Cannot diagnose Addison's disease or Cushing's syndrome (requires blood work)
- Food, blood, timing errors can skew results significantly
- Not all hormones validated for saliva testing
- Reference ranges vary widely between labs
- Single test is snapshot; doesn't capture longer-term trends
- More variable than blood testing (requires careful collection)
- Not covered by many insurance plans
- Requires patient compliance with detailed protocol

**Remember**: Saliva testing is a powerful tool for free hormone assessment and pattern tracking when done correctly. Precision in collection timing and technique is critical for accurate, interpretable results. Work with qualified practitioners to ensure appropriate test selection and interpretation.
