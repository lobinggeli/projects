# Supplement Assessment Framework

Use this framework to systematically identify supplement needs through evidence-based assessment of symptoms, deficiencies, and testing data.

## Core Concept

The Supplement Assessment Framework employs a hierarchical approach that prioritizes objective testing over subjective symptoms, and addresses root causes before optimization. Effective supplementation starts with identifying *why* you might need a supplement, not which supplement is trendy.

### The Assessment Hierarchy

1. **Test-Based**: Confirmed deficiencies via biomarker testing (highest confidence)
2. **Symptom-Based**: Pattern recognition of deficiency symptoms (moderate confidence)
3. **Prophylactic**: Evidence-based prevention for populations at risk (lower confidence)
4. **Therapeutic**: Targeted support for specific health goals (variable confidence)

## When to Apply

- Before purchasing any new supplements
- When experiencing persistent unexplained symptoms
- After receiving biomarker test results
- To reduce unnecessary supplement expenditure
- When building or revising a supplement protocol
- Before discussing supplement needs with healthcare providers
- When evaluating supplement recommendations from others

## Assessment Process Overview

| Step | Focus | Question | Action |
|------|-------|----------|--------|
| **Step 1** | Symptoms | What am I experiencing? | Document symptom patterns |
| **Step 2** | Testing | Do I have objective evidence? | Review biomarker data |
| **Step 3** | Risk Factors | Am I in a high-risk group? | Assess population risk |
| **Step 4** | Root Causes | Is this deficiency or dysfunction? | Identify underlying issues |
| **Step 5** | Prioritization | What matters most? | Rank by impact and evidence |

## Critical Assessment Principles

### Principle 1: Testing Over Guessing
- Supplementing without testing risks wasting money on unnecessary supplements
- Some deficiencies are dangerous to over-correct (iron, vitamin A, copper)
- Symptoms can have multiple causes; testing identifies the actual problem
- Baseline testing enables tracking efficacy

### Principle 2: Fix Absorption Before Adding Supplements
- Gut dysfunction causes deficiencies despite adequate intake
- Medications block nutrient absorption (PPIs, metformin, birth control)
- If you supplement but don't absorb, you're just creating expensive urine
- Address: gut health, medication interactions, timing with inhibitors

### Principle 3: Address Root Causes
- Chronic stress depletes magnesium → supplement AND reduce stress
- Poor sleep disrupts hormone balance → supplement AND improve sleep
- Blood sugar dysregulation affects nutrient storage → fix diet first
- Supplements support, they don't replace addressing root dysfunction

### Principle 4: Foundation Before Optimization
- Cover widespread deficiencies first (Vitamin D, Magnesium, Omega-3)
- Then address targeted symptoms (sleep, energy, cognition)
- Save optimization supplements (nootropics, athletics) for last
- You can't optimize on a deficient foundation

## Deficiency Assessment by System

### Energy & Fatigue

#### Common Deficiency Patterns
| Symptom Cluster | Likely Deficiencies | Assessment Priority | Testing |
|-----------------|---------------------|---------------------|---------|
| **Morning fatigue, slow wake** | Iron, B12, Vitamin D, Magnesium | High | CBC, Ferritin, B12, 25-OH Vitamin D |
| **Afternoon crashes** | Iron, B vitamins, Blood sugar dysregulation | High | Fasting glucose, Insulin, Iron panel |
| **Exercise intolerance** | Iron, CoQ10, Magnesium, B vitamins | Medium | Ferritin, Magnesium RBC |
| **Brain fog with fatigue** | B12, Iron, Thyroid, Omega-3 | High | B12, Ferritin, TSH, Free T3/T4 |
| **Cold intolerance + fatigue** | Iron, Iodine, Selenium (thyroid) | High | Thyroid panel, Ferritin, Selenium |

#### Clinical Pearls (Highly Specific Signs)
- **Pica (ice cravings)**: Iron deficiency until proven otherwise
- **Glossitis (smooth, sore tongue)**: B12 or folate deficiency
- **Restless legs at night**: Iron deficiency (check ferritin <75)
- **"Crashes" after meals**: Blood sugar issue, consider chromium/alpha-lipoic acid
- **Fatigue improving with sun**: Likely Vitamin D deficiency

## Biomarker-Supplement Correlation Matrix

### Vitamin D (25-OH Vitamin D)

| Test Result | Status | Supplement Need | Dose |
|-------------|--------|-----------------|------|
| <20 ng/mL | Deficient | High priority | 5,000-10,000 IU daily (with K2) |
| 20-30 ng/mL | Insufficient | Moderate priority | 2,000-5,000 IU daily (with K2) |
| 30-50 ng/mL | Adequate | Maintenance | 1,000-2,000 IU daily (with K2) |
| 50-80 ng/mL | Optimal | Maintenance (optional) | 1,000-2,000 IU daily |
| >100 ng/mL | Excessive | Stop supplementation | None - reassess in 3 months |

**Symptoms Correlation**: Fatigue, frequent infections, bone pain, depression, muscle weakness

---

### Iron (Ferritin)

| Test Result | Status | Supplement Need | Dose |
|-------------|--------|-----------------|------|
| <15 ng/mL | Deficient | High priority | 25-65 mg elemental iron daily (with Vitamin C) |
| 15-30 ng/mL | Low | Moderate priority | 25-40 mg elemental iron daily |
| 30-75 ng/mL | Suboptimal | Consider if symptomatic | 15-25 mg elemental iron |
| 75-150 ng/mL | Optimal | None needed | None |
| >300 ng/mL | Excessive | STOP - medical evaluation | None - iron overload risk |

**Symptoms Correlation**: Fatigue, brain fog, restless legs, ice cravings, brittle nails, hair loss
**Caution**: Only supplement if tested. Iron overload is dangerous.

---

### Magnesium (RBC Magnesium)

| Test Result | Status | Supplement Need | Dose |
|-------------|--------|-----------------|------|
| <4.0 mg/dL | Deficient | High priority | 400-600 mg elemental (glycinate or citrate) |
| 4.0-5.0 mg/dL | Low-normal | Moderate priority | 200-400 mg elemental |
| 5.0-6.5 mg/dL | Optimal | Maintenance (optional) | 200-300 mg elemental |
| >6.5 mg/dL | High-normal | None unless symptomatic | None |

**Note**: Serum magnesium is unreliable; use RBC magnesium or intracellular testing
**Symptoms Correlation**: Muscle cramps, poor sleep, anxiety, eye twitching, constipation, migraines

---

### Vitamin B12

| Test Result | Status | Supplement Need | Dose |
|-------------|--------|-----------------|------|
| <200 pg/mL | Deficient | High priority | 1,000-2,000 mcg methylcobalamin daily |
| 200-400 pg/mL | Low-normal | Moderate priority | 500-1,000 mcg daily |
| 400-900 pg/mL | Optimal | None unless symptomatic | None |
| >900 pg/mL | High (check MMA) | Assess functional status | Depends on MMA result |

**Consider MMA test**: Elevated methylmalonic acid (MMA) indicates *functional* B12 deficiency even if serum B12 is "normal"
**Symptoms Correlation**: Fatigue, numbness/tingling, memory issues, glossitis, mood changes

---

### Omega-3 (Omega-3 Index)

| Test Result | Status | Supplement Need | Dose |
|-------------|--------|-----------------|------|
| <4% | Very Low | High priority | 2,000-3,000 mg EPA+DHA daily |
| 4-8% | Low | Moderate priority | 1,000-2,000 mg EPA+DHA daily |
| 8-12% | Optimal | Maintenance | 500-1,000 mg EPA+DHA daily |
| >12% | High | Reduce dose | 500 mg EPA+DHA daily or less |

**Symptoms Correlation**: Dry skin, poor focus, mood issues, inflammation, joint pain

---

### Zinc (Plasma or RBC)

| Test Result | Status | Supplement Need | Dose |
|-------------|--------|-----------------|------|
| <70 mcg/dL | Deficient | High priority | 30-50 mg elemental zinc daily (with copper) |
| 70-90 mcg/dL | Low-normal | Moderate priority | 15-30 mg elemental zinc |
| 90-120 mcg/dL | Optimal | None unless symptomatic | None |
| >150 mcg/dL | Excessive | Stop - can deplete copper | None |

**Symptoms Correlation**: Frequent infections, hair loss, taste/smell changes, slow wound healing
**Caution**: Long-term zinc supplementation requires copper balance

## Common Assessment Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Supplementing Without Testing
**Error**: Assuming you need something based on symptoms alone
**Risk**: Wasting money, over-supplementing dangerous nutrients (iron, copper, vitamin A)
**Solution**: Test vitamin D, ferritin, B12, magnesium at minimum before building protocol

### Mistake 2: Over-Supplementation
**Error**: Taking "everything" to cover all bases
**Risk**: Nutrient interactions, competition, expense, pill fatigue, imbalances
**Solution**: Prioritize 3-5 evidence-based supplements; add one at a time

### Mistake 3: Ignoring Absorption Issues
**Error**: Supplementing aggressively despite gut dysfunction
**Risk**: Wasting money on supplements you don't absorb
**Solution**: Address gut health, HCL, digestive enzymes, microbiome first

### Mistake 4: Treating Symptoms Instead of Root Causes
**Error**: Taking magnesium for anxiety without addressing chronic stress
**Risk**: Temporary bandaid, symptoms return, continuous supplementation needed
**Solution**: Use supplements as SUPPORT while addressing lifestyle root causes

### Mistake 5: No Timeline or Tracking
**Error**: Taking supplements indefinitely without reassessing
**Risk**: Continuing unnecessary supplements, missing ineffective ones, not optimizing
**Solution**: Set retest dates, track symptoms, evaluate efficacy at 3 months

### Mistake 6: Following Trends Instead of Needs
**Error**: Buying supplements because influencers recommend them
**Risk**: Wasting money on unnecessary supplements that don't match your needs
**Solution**: Base decisions on YOUR symptoms, YOUR testing, YOUR risk factors

## Quick Reference: High-Yield Assessments

### Most Common Deficiencies Worth Testing
1. **Vitamin D**: 40-70% of population deficient
2. **Magnesium**: 50% of population insufficient (RBC testing needed)
3. **Omega-3**: 95% of Americans have suboptimal Omega-3 Index
4. **Iron (women)**: 20-30% of menstruating women deficient
5. **B12 (age >50, vegans, PPI users)**: 10-40% depending on population

### Highest-Priority Tests If Budget Limited
1. **Vitamin D (25-OH)**: Cheap, common deficiency, clear supplementation guidelines
2. **Ferritin**: Cheap, critical to rule out before supplementing, dangerous if high
3. **Magnesium RBC**: Moderate cost, very common deficiency, explains many symptoms
4. **B12 + MMA**: Moderate cost, especially if neurological symptoms present

### Highest-Value Supplements (Evidence-Based for Most People)
1. **Vitamin D3 + K2**: If tested and deficient (very common)
2. **Magnesium glycinate**: If symptoms (cramps, sleep, anxiety) or tested low
3. **Omega-3 (EPA/DHA)**: If low fish intake, inflammation, mood issues
4. **Probiotic**: If gut issues, recent antibiotics, immune issues

## Limitations

- Symptoms overlap between deficiencies; testing is required for certainty
- "Normal" reference ranges vary by lab and may not represent optimal levels
- Some nutrients don't have reliable biomarker tests (e.g., Vitamin C, Vitamin K)
- Single-point testing doesn't capture temporal variation
- Deficiency symptoms can take weeks to months to resolve with supplementation
- Supplements are tools, not replacements for healthy diet and lifestyle
- Self-assessment has limitations; consult healthcare providers for complex cases

**Remember**: Supplement assessment is about identifying true needs, not building a cabinet full of bottles. Test, target, track. Start with evidence, not trends. Address root causes, not just symptoms. This framework supports informed decision-making, not self-diagnosis.
