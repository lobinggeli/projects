# Supplement Selection Framework

Use this framework to choose the right supplements, doses, and forms based on individual needs, evidence, and goals.

## Core Concept

The Supplement Selection Framework employs a structured decision-making process that matches supplements to verified needs while optimizing for efficacy, safety, and cost-effectiveness. Effective selection requires balancing evidence strength, bioavailability, dosing precision, and individual circumstances.

### The Selection Hierarchy

1. **Foundation**: Widespread deficiencies most people benefit from addressing
2. **Targeted Support**: Evidence-based supplements for specific symptoms or conditions
3. **Optimization**: Performance, longevity, or cognitive enhancement for those with solid foundation

## When to Apply

- After completing supplement assessment (symptoms, testing, risk factors)
- When building a new supplement protocol
- When revising an existing protocol that isn't working
- After receiving biomarker test results
- When budget requires prioritization
- When evaluating supplement recommendations from others
- Before purchasing supplements

## Foundation Supplements

These address the most common deficiencies in modern populations. Consider if you have symptoms, risk factors, or test results indicating need.

### Vitamin D3 + K2

**Who Needs It**:
- 40-70% of population deficient
- High risk: Limited sun exposure, dark skin, northern latitudes, winter months, age >50
- Symptoms: Fatigue, frequent infections, bone pain, depression, muscle weakness
- Test: 25-OH Vitamin D < 30 ng/mL

**Best Form**: Vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol) with Vitamin K2 (MK-7)
- D3 > D2 (ergocalciferol) for efficacy
- K2 ensures calcium goes to bones, not arteries
- Oil-based softgels for better absorption

**Dosing**:
| 25-OH Vitamin D Level | Dose | Duration | Retest |
|-----------------------|------|----------|--------|
| < 20 ng/mL (Deficient) | 5,000-10,000 IU daily | 8-12 weeks | 3 months |
| 20-30 ng/mL (Insufficient) | 2,000-5,000 IU daily | 8-12 weeks | 3 months |
| 30-50 ng/mL (Adequate) | 1,000-2,000 IU daily | Maintenance | 6-12 months |
| 50-80 ng/mL (Optimal) | 1,000-2,000 IU daily | Maintenance | 12 months |

**K2 Dose**: 100-200 mcg per 5,000 IU D3

**Timing**: Morning with breakfast (fat-soluble, energizing)

**Evidence Strength**: **STRONG** - Extensive research on immune, bone, mood, metabolic health

**Cost**: $10-20/month

### Magnesium

**Who Needs It**:
- 50%+ of population insufficient
- High risk: Chronic stress, poor sleep, athletes, high calcium intake, PPI use, alcohol consumption
- Symptoms: Muscle cramps, poor sleep, anxiety, eye twitching, constipation, migraines, fatigue
- Test: RBC Magnesium < 5.0 mg/dL (serum magnesium unreliable)

**Best Form**: Depends on goal
| Form | Absorption | Best For | Avoid If |
|------|------------|----------|----------|
| **Magnesium Glycinate** | Excellent | Sleep, anxiety, muscle cramps (doesn't cause diarrhea) | None |
| **Magnesium Citrate** | Good | Constipation (mild laxative effect) | Diarrhea-prone |
| **Magnesium Threonate** | Good (crosses BBB) | Cognitive function, brain health (expensive) | Budget-limited |
| **Magnesium Oxide** | Poor (~4%) | **AVOID** - cheap but poorly absorbed | Always |
| **Magnesium Chloride** | Good | Topical use (oil/spray for muscles) | None |

**Dosing**:
- **General**: 200-400 mg elemental magnesium daily
- **Deficiency**: 400-600 mg elemental magnesium daily
- **Sleep**: 300-500 mg glycinate before bed
- **Athletic**: 400-800 mg daily (split doses)

**Timing**: Evening or before bed (promotes relaxation and sleep)

**Evidence Strength**: **STRONG** - Well-researched for sleep, anxiety, muscle function, blood pressure

**Cost**: $10-20/month

---

### Omega-3 (EPA/DHA)

**Who Needs It**:
- 95% of Americans have suboptimal Omega-3 Index
- High risk: Low fish intake (<2 servings fatty fish/week), inflammation, mood disorders, cardiovascular risk
- Symptoms: Dry skin, poor focus, mood issues (depression/anxiety), joint pain, inflammation
- Test: Omega-3 Index < 8%

**Best Form**: Triglyceride or re-esterified triglyceride form > Ethyl ester
- Look for "triglyceride form" on label
- Third-party tested for purity (IFOS, USP)
- High EPA:DHA ratio (2:1 or 3:1) for mood/inflammation
- Higher DHA for cognitive/pregnancy

**Dosing**:
| Omega-3 Index | Goal | Dose (EPA+DHA combined) |
|---------------|------|-------------------------|
| < 4% (Very Low) | Therapeutic | 2,000-3,000 mg daily |
| 4-8% (Low) | Improvement | 1,000-2,000 mg daily |
| 8-12% (Optimal) | Maintenance | 500-1,000 mg daily |

**Specific Conditions**:
- **Depression/Anxiety**: 1,000-2,000 mg EPA daily (EPA > DHA)
- **Inflammation**: 2,000-3,000 mg EPA+DHA daily
- **Cardiovascular**: 1,000-2,000 mg EPA+DHA daily
- **Pregnancy**: 300-600 mg DHA daily

**Timing**: With largest meal (fat-soluble). Can take AM or PM.

**Evidence Strength**: **STRONG** - Robust evidence for cardiovascular, mood, inflammation, cognitive health

**Cost**: $20-40/month (quality matters - cheap = rancid)

---

### Probiotic

**Who Needs It**:
- High risk: Recent antibiotics, digestive issues (IBS, bloating, diarrhea), frequent infections, mood issues
- Symptoms: Bloating, gas, irregular bowel movements, frequent infections, food sensitivities, mood issues
- Test: Not typically tested; trial-based assessment

**Best Form**: Multi-strain with 10-50 billion CFUs
- Look for: Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium strains
- Shelf-stable or refrigerated (depends on formulation)
- Enteric-coated or acid-resistant capsules

**Key Strains by Goal**:
| Goal | Strains to Look For |
|------|---------------------|
| **General gut health** | Multi-strain with L. acidophilus, L. rhamnosus, B. longum, B. bifidum |
| **IBS/Bloating** | B. infantis, L. plantarum, B. coagulans |
| **Diarrhea** | S. boulardii (yeast probiotic), L. rhamnosus GG |
| **Constipation** | B. lactis, L. casei |
| **Immune support** | L. rhamnosus, L. casei, B. longum |
| **Mood/Anxiety** | L. helveticus, B. longum (psychobiotics) |

**Dosing**: 10-50 billion CFUs daily (higher after antibiotics: 50-100 billion)

**Timing**: Morning with breakfast (on empty stomach or with food - depends on product)

**Evidence Strength**: **MODERATE-STRONG** - Strong for specific conditions (antibiotic-associated diarrhea, IBS), moderate for general health

**Cost**: $20-40/month

## Targeted Support Supplements

Use these for specific symptoms or conditions after addressing foundation needs.

### For Stress & High Cortisol

| Supplement | Mechanism | Dose | Evidence | Cost/Month |
|------------|-----------|------|----------|------------|
| **Ashwagandha** (KSM-66 or Sensoril) | Adaptogen; reduces cortisol 15-30% | 300-600 mg daily | Strong | $15-25 |
| **Rhodiola rosea** | Adaptogen; enhances stress resilience | 200-400 mg daily (AM) | Moderate | $15-25 |
| **Phosphatidylserine** | Blunts cortisol response | 200-400 mg evening | Moderate | $20-30 |
| **L-Theanine** | Promotes calm without sedation | 100-200 mg as needed | Moderate | $10-20 |
| **Magnesium glycinate** | Calming, muscle relaxation | 300-400 mg evening | Strong | $10-15 |

**Selection Strategy**: Start with Magnesium + Ashwagandha. Add L-Theanine for acute stress. Add Phosphatidylserine if high evening cortisol.

| **Magnesium glycinate** | GABA modulation, muscle relaxation | 300-500 mg | 1 hour before bed | Strong | $10-15 |
| **Glycine** | Lowers core body temp, improves sleep quality | 3,000 mg (3g) | 1 hour before bed | Moderate | $10-15 |

- **Start with**: Magnesium glycinate (most versatile, addresses deficiency)

## Dosing Quick Reference

### Start Low, Go Slow
- Begin at lower end of dosing range
- Increase gradually every 1-2 weeks if needed
- Some supplements take 6-12 weeks for full effect
- Don't exceed upper tolerable limits

### Common Upper Limits (Don't Exceed Without Medical Supervision)
| Supplement | Upper Tolerable Limit | Risks if Exceeded |
|------------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Vitamin D | 10,000 IU daily | Hypercalcemia, kidney stones |
| Vitamin A | 10,000 IU daily | Liver toxicity, birth defects |
| Iron | 45 mg elemental (100-150mg in salt form) | GI distress, constipation, toxicity |
| Zinc | 40 mg daily long-term | Copper depletion, immune suppression |
| Magnesium | 350 mg from supplements (more from food OK) | Diarrhea (glycinate higher tolerance) |
| Niacin (B3) | 35 mg (for flush-free form much higher OK) | Flushing, liver toxicity (high dose) |

## Special Populations

### Pregnancy & Breastfeeding
**Essential**: Prenatal multivitamin with folate (not folic acid), Iron, DHA
**Safe**: Vitamin D, Magnesium, B vitamins, Probiotics
**Avoid**: High-dose Vitamin A, herbs (most), weight loss supplements

### Children
- Require lower doses (calculate by weight)
- Focus on: Vitamin D, Omega-3, Probiotics if needed
- Avoid: Stimulants, weight loss, hormonal supplements

### Elderly (Age >65)
- Higher risk for: B12, Vitamin D, Calcium, Protein deficiency
- Focus on: B12 (sublingual or methylcobalamin), Vitamin D, Calcium+D+K2, Protein powder if low intake
- Caution: Drug interactions increase with age; review with pharmacist

### Athletes (Intense Training)
- Higher needs: Magnesium, Iron (if deficient), B vitamins, Antioxidants
- Performance: Creatine, Beta-alanine, Citrulline
- Recovery: Protein, Omega-3, Vitamin D

## Limitations

- Bioavailability varies by form and individual absorption capacity
- Optimal doses are ranges, not exact prescriptions
- Response is individual; what works for others may not work for you
- Supplements complement, don't replace, healthy diet and lifestyle
- Evidence strength varies; some supplements lack robust human trials
- Quality varies dramatically between brands (third-party testing matters)
- Cost-effectiveness calculations don't account for individual absorption or need
- Self-selection has limitations; complex cases need professional guidance

**Remember**: Selection is a hypothesis. Implement → Track → Retest → Adjust. Start with foundation, add targeted support based on evidence, save optimization for later. Quality over quantity. Evidence over trends. This framework supports informed choices, not self-treatment.
