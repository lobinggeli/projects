# Biomarker Test Preparation Framework

Use this framework to prepare for accurate, reliable biomarker testing and avoid common errors that can invalidate results.

## Core Concept

Test accuracy depends heavily on proper preparation and collection technique. Even the best laboratory analysis can't correct for poor sample collection, wrong timing, or contamination. This framework ensures your test results accurately reflect your biology, not collection errors.

### The Three Pillars of Accurate Testing

1. **Pre-Test Preparation**: Optimize conditions 24-48 hours before
2. **Collection Technique**: Follow precise protocols during sampling
3. **Post-Collection Handling**: Proper storage and transport

## General Preparation Principles

### 48 Hours Before Any Test

**Maintain normalcy:**
- Don't change diet drastically
- Don't start new supplements
- Don't drastically alter exercise routine
- Continue prescribed medications (unless told otherwise)
- **Why**: You want results to reflect your actual state, not a temporary change

**Document context:**
- Sleep quality and duration
- Stress levels
- Recent illness or inflammation
- Menstrual cycle day (if applicable)
- Any unusual events

### 24 Hours Before

**Avoid these common interfering factors:**

❌ **Alcohol**: Affects cortisol, liver enzymes, glucose, triglycerides, hormones
- Abstain 24-48 hours before testing

❌ **Intense exercise**: Elevates cortisol, CK, may show transient protein in urine
- Avoid hard workouts 24 hours before
- Light walking is fine

❌ **Fasting (for non-fasting tests)**: Can affect hormone levels
- Only fast if test specifically requires it
- Cortisol testing: No fasting needed (in fact, don't skip meals)

❌ **Binge eating/drinking**: Spikes glucose, insulin, triglycerides
- Eat normally

### Day Before Test

**Preparation checklist:**
- [ ] Collection materials ready and labeled
- [ ] Alarms set for collection times
- [ ] Instructions re-read
- [ ] Questions for provider written down
- [ ] Refrigerator space cleared (if needed)
- [ ] Normal, consistent sleep planned

## Test-Specific Preparation

### Blood Tests (Venous Draw)

#### Fasting Blood Work
**Required for:** Fasting glucose, insulin, lipid panel, metabolic panel

**Fasting protocol:**
- 8-12 hours without food (typically 10-12 hours)
- Water is allowed and encouraged
- No coffee, tea, or other beverages
- No gum or mints
- Take medications with water unless told otherwise

**Timing:**
- Schedule morning appointment (easier to fast overnight)
- If diabetic: Coordinate with provider on medication timing

**Morning of test:**
- Drink water before going (helps with blood draw)
- Bring medications to take after draw
- Eat breakfast immediately after (especially if diabetic)

#### Non-Fasting Blood Work
**Examples:** Cortisol, some hormone panels, CBC, thyroid

**Preparation:**
- Eat normally before test
- Avoid high-fat meal immediately before (can make blood lipemic/cloudy)
- Stay hydrated

**Timing considerations:**
- Cortisol: Morning draw (8 AM ideally) to capture peak
- Testosterone: Morning draw (peaks in AM for men)
- Thyroid: Morning, fasting if possible (TSH highest in AM)
- Iron studies: Morning, fasting (diurnal variation)

#### Day of Blood Draw

**Hydration:**
- Drink 16-20 oz water before appointment
- Makes veins easier to find
- Reduces lightheadedness

**Stress management:**
- Arrive early, don't rush
- Deep breathing (stress spikes cortisol)
- Look away during needle stick if anxious

**After draw:**
- Apply pressure to site for 5 minutes
- Keep bandage on for a few hours
- Avoid heavy lifting with that arm for rest of day
- Drink fluids and eat

### Saliva Tests

#### Cortisol 4-Point Saliva Test

**Critical: Timing precision is everything**

**48 Hours before:**
- Note sleep quality (affects cortisol)
- Avoid alcohol (affects HPA axis)
- Note major stressors

**Day before:**
- Prepare collection tubes and labels
- Set alarms for each collection time
- Get good sleep (poor sleep elevates cortisol)
- Have water ready for morning

**Morning of test (Sample #1 - Waking):**
- Collect WITHIN 30 MINUTES of waking
- Collect BEFORE getting out of bed (keep tube on nightstand)
- Don't snooze alarm (affects cortisol awakening response)
- Don't check phone/email first (stress spike)
- Don't drink, eat, or brush teeth before collecting

**Sample #2 (30-45 min after Sample #1 - CAR):**
- Exactly 30-45 min after first sample
- Can be out of bed by now
- Still no food, drink (except water), caffeine, or tooth brushing

**Sample #3 (Midday - typically 12-1 PM):**
- 30+ minutes after eating (if near a meal)
- Rinse mouth with water 10 min before if you've eaten
- Record exact time

**Sample #4 (Before bed - typically 10-11 PM):**
- As close to bedtime as possible
- 30+ min after eating/drinking
- Rinse mouth with water 10 min before

**Collection technique (all samples):**
1. Rinse mouth with water (if needed)
2. Wait 10 minutes
3. Sit quietly for 5 minutes
4. Passive drool into tube (don't force)
5. Fill to line indicated
6. Cap immediately
7. Label with: name, date, time, sample #
8. Refrigerate

**Avoid contamination:**
- No blood (gentle with teeth/gums)
- No food particles (wait 30 min after eating)
- No lip balm, lipstick
- No aggressive spitting (passive drool only)

#### Sex Hormone Saliva Testing

**Timing by menstrual cycle (women):**
- **Estrogen/Progesterone balance**: Day 19-21 (luteal phase)
- **Estrogen dominant phases**: Day 3-5 (follicular)
- **Ovulation**: Day 12-14

**For men:**
- Morning sample (testosterone peaks in AM)
- Consistent timing for tracking

**For hormone replacement therapy (HRT):**
- Time relative to dose:
  - Transdermal (creams): 12-24 hours after application
  - Sublingual: 1-2 hours after dose
  - Troches: 4-6 hours after dose
- Follow provider's specific instructions

### Urine Tests

#### Spot Urine (Urinalysis, Ketones, Glucose)

**First morning urine (most concentrated):**
- Collect immediately upon waking
- Before drinking fluids
- Best for: protein, microalbumin, pregnancy tests

**Midstream clean catch:**
1. Wash hands with soap and water
2. Clean genital area:
   - Women: Wipe front to back with clean wipes
   - Men: Retract foreskin if uncircumcised, clean glans
3. Begin urinating into toilet (first part has bacteria)
4. Collect midstream urine in sterile cup
5. Finish urinating into toilet
6. Cap and label
7. Refrigerate if not testing within 1 hour

**Avoid contamination:**
- Don't touch inside of cup or lid
- Don't collect during menstrual period (blood contamination)
- Don't get toilet paper in sample
- Don't collect after sexual activity (wait 24 hours)

#### 24-Hour Urine Collection

**Preparation:**
- Get collection container from lab (may have preservative)
- Clear refrigerator space
- Plan to be home or have portable cooler

**Collection protocol:**

**Day 1, Morning (Start):**
1. Wake and urinate normally - DISCARD this sample
2. Record exact time
3. From now on, collect EVERY urination for 24 hours
4. Keep container refrigerated or on ice

**Throughout 24 hours:**
- Don't miss any voids (if you do, start over)
- Pour each void into large container
- Refrigerate between voids
- Continue normal activities, diet, hydration
- Document medications taken

**Day 2, Same time as Day 1 start (End):**
1. Urinate one final time - INCLUDE this sample
2. Record exact end time
3. Measure and record total volume
4. Mix well
5. Pour sample into lab tube
6. Deliver to lab within 1 hour or keep refrigerated

**Common errors to avoid:**
- Discarding first void of Day 2 (should be INCLUDED)
- Forgetting to collect a void (invalidates test)
- Not refrigerating throughout
- Starting collection WITH first morning void (should discard)

#### DUTCH Test (Dried Urine)

**Timing (women):**
- Day 19-21 of cycle (luteal phase) for comprehensive testing
- Count Day 1 as first day of period
- If irregular cycles, consult provider

**Collection:**
1. Urinate on filter paper strip or collection card
2. Use first morning urine
3. Saturate filter paper fully
4. Air dry for 24 hours (lay flat)
5. Place in protective sleeve
6. Mail to lab with ice pack

**Avoid:**
- Touching filter paper with hands (oils contaminate)
- Drying in direct sunlight (degrades hormones)
- Folding before completely dry
- Exposing to extreme heat

### Glucose & Ketone Testing

#### Fasting Glucose (Blood)

**Preparation:**
- 8-12 hour fast (water allowed)
- No coffee, tea, or beverages
- Schedule for morning
- Normal dinner the night before (don't carb-load or restrict)

#### Continuous Glucose Monitor (CGM)

**Sensor insertion:**
- Clean skin with alcohol, let dry completely
- Apply to back of upper arm or abdomen (depends on device)
- Avoid areas with scars, tattoos, moles
- Avoid very muscular areas (interferes with reading)

**Calibration (if required):**
- Follow manufacturer's instructions
- Use accurate finger-stick meter
- Calibrate when glucose is stable, not rising or falling

**Wearing CGM:**
- Can shower (most are waterproof)
- May need to remove for MRI or X-rays
- Keep phone/receiver within range for readings
- Change sensor every 10-14 days (per device)

**Factors affecting accuracy:**
- First 24 hours often less accurate (sensor settling in)
- Lag time (~10-15 min behind blood glucose)
- Compression (sleeping on sensor can cause false lows)
- Acetaminophen can cause false high readings (some devices)

#### Ketone Testing

**Blood ketones (BHB):**
- Test fasted in morning (lowest levels of day)
- Or pre-meal to assess metabolic state
- Clean finger, alcohol swab, let dry
- Use side of fingertip (less painful)

**Urine ketones:**
- First morning urine (most concentrated)
- Or before meals
- Don't over-interpret (shows excess being excreted, not utilization)
- Becomes less positive as keto-adapted (using ketones efficiently)

**Factors affecting ketones:**
- Exercise: Can temporarily raise or lower
- Protein intake: Excess can suppress ketones
- MCT oil: Rapidly raises ketones (exogenous)
- Hydration: Affects urine concentration

## Common Interfering Factors

### Medications & Supplements

**Always disclose to provider:**
- Prescription medications
- Over-the-counter drugs
- Supplements and herbs
- Hormone therapies

**Common interferants:**

| Substance | Affects | How |
|-----------|---------|-----|
| **Biotin (high-dose)** | Thyroid, troponin | False low TSH, false results |
| **Vitamin C** | Urine glucose | False negative |
| **Corticosteroids** | Cortisol, glucose | Elevates both |
| **Birth control** | Hormones, clotting | Alters multiple markers |
| **Diuretics** | Electrolytes | Depletes potassium |
| **Metformin** | B12, lactic acid | Depletes B12 over time |
| **Statins** | Liver enzymes, CK | Elevates |

**Timing with tests:**
- Biotin: Stop 3-7 days before testing (especially thyroid)
- Hormone supplements: Stop 48 hours before (if testing that hormone)
- Most others: Take as prescribed, but note on lab form

### Lifestyle Factors

**Exercise:**
- **Intense exercise**: Wait 24-48 hours (affects CK, cortisol, inflammatory markers)
- **Moderate exercise**: 12-24 hours
- **Light activity**: OK, may even be beneficial (stress reduction)

**Sleep:**
- **Poor sleep**: Elevates cortisol, glucose, inflammatory markers
- **Goal**: Normal, consistent sleep before testing

**Stress:**
- **Acute stress**: Spikes cortisol, glucose, blood pressure
- **Mitigation**: Arrive early, practice deep breathing, minimize rush

**Caffeine:**
- Elevates cortisol (avoid before cortisol testing)
- OK for most other tests (maintain normal routine)
- Avoid for fasting tests

**Alcohol:**
- Affects liver enzymes, triglycerides, glucose, hormones
- Abstain 24-48 hours before testing

**Smoking/Vaping:**
- Affects cortisol, inflammatory markers
- Note on lab forms, but don't quit right before test (affects baseline)

### Timing Considerations

**Time of day:**
- Cortisol: Morning (8 AM ideal)
- Testosterone: Morning (M), cycle-dependent (F)
- Thyroid: Morning, fasting (TSH highest then)
- Iron: Morning, fasting (diurnal variation)
- Lipids: Morning, fasted

**Day of week:**
- Weekday preferred (consistent routine)
- Avoid Monday after wild weekend (skewed results)

**Cycle timing (menstruating women):**
- Day 3-5: Baseline FSH, LH, estrogen
- Day 19-21: Progesterone peak (confirms ovulation)
- Avoid testing during period (for urine/saliva)

**Season (for Vitamin D):**
- Summer levels higher than winter
- Test at same time of year for comparisons

## Troubleshooting Common Issues

### "My veins are hard to find"
**Solutions:**
- Drink 20 oz water 1-2 hours before
- Stay warm (cold constricts veins)
- Let arm hang down before draw
- Squeeze stress ball to pump up veins
- Request butterfly needle or pediatric phlebotomist

### "I can't produce enough saliva"
**Solutions:**
- Think about favorite foods (stimulates saliva)
- Gently massage salivary glands (under jaw, in front of ears)
- Ensure adequate hydration day before
- Don't force; let saliva pool naturally
- May take 5-10 minutes to fill tube

### "I missed a void in my 24-hour collection"
**Solutions:**
- Unfortunately, must start over
- Set phone alarms for every 2-3 hours as reminder
- Keep collection container in visible place
- Post sticky notes on bathroom mirror

### "My urine ketone strips don't show ketosis but I feel I'm in ketosis"
**Solutions:**
- Urine ketones decrease as keto-adapted (you're using them efficiently)
- Switch to blood ketone testing for accuracy
- Check glucose (if low-normal + keto symptoms, likely in ketosis)

### "I forgot I'm not supposed to eat before my fasting test"
**Solutions:**
- Call lab/provider immediately
- May need to reschedule
- Some tests (like cortisol) don't require fasting - clarify

### "The lab lost my sample"
**Solutions:**
- Always get receipt/confirmation when dropping off
- For expensive tests (DUTCH), photograph sample before shipping
- Use tracking for mailed samples
- Confirm receipt with lab

## Limitations

- Perfect preparation can't overcome poor lab quality (use reputable labs)
- Some variability is biological, not collection error
- Absolute control of all variables is impossible
- Stress of testing itself can affect some markers (cortisol, blood pressure)
- Individual factors (genetics, medications) may override standard prep

**Remember**: Proper test preparation is as important as the analysis itself. A perfectly analyzed sample collected incorrectly provides misleading results. When in doubt, contact the lab or provider for clarification before collecting. Better to delay and do it right than waste time and money on invalid results.
