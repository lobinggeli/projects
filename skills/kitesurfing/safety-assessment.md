# Kitesurfing Safety Assessment Framework

Use this framework to systematically evaluate risks and conditions before each kitesurfing session to ensure safe practice.

## Core Concept

The Safety Assessment Framework employs a multi-layered risk evaluation approach across five critical dimensions. Safe kitesurfing requires continuous assessment of environmental, equipment, personal, location, and emergency factors.

### The Five Safety Pillars

1. **Environmental Conditions**: Wind, weather, water state
2. **Equipment Integrity**: Gear condition and appropriateness
3. **Personal Readiness**: Skill level, physical state, mental preparation
4. **Location Hazards**: Obstacles, crowds, escape routes
5. **Emergency Preparedness**: Rescue plans, communication, backup systems

## When to Apply

- Before every kitesurfing session (mandatory pre-flight check)
- When conditions change mid-session
- When evaluating unfamiliar locations
- After equipment changes or repairs
- When returning after extended breaks
- When introducing new riders to a spot

## Risk Assessment Matrix

| Risk Level | Wind Variance | Obstacles | Crowd Density | Your Action |
|------------|---------------|-----------|---------------|-------------|
| **Low** | Steady ±2 knots | None within 200m | <3 riders visible | Proceed with normal caution |
| **Moderate** | Variable ±5 knots | Some downwind | 3-8 riders | Increase vigilance, clear launch plan |
| **High** | Gusty >5 knots | Multiple hazards | >8 riders | Consider postponing, brief plan |
| **Critical** | Extreme gusts, offshore | Immediate obstacles | Overcrowded | Do not launch |

## Critical Go/No-Go Factors

### Immediate No-Go Conditions
- **Offshore wind** with no safety boat
- **Lightning** within 10 miles
- **Equipment damage** (tears, broken lines, leash failure)
- **Skill mismatch**: Conditions beyond your proven ability
- **Solo session** at unfamiliar/remote location
- **Physical impairment**: Fatigue, injury, illness, substance use

### Yellow Flags (Proceed with Caution)
- Wind at edge of kite's range
- Onshore wind with obstacles inland
- First session at new location
- New equipment not yet tested
- Tide changing significantly during session
- Limited visibility (dusk, rain, glare)

## Pre-Session Safety Checklist

### Equipment Check (5 minutes)
- [ ] Kite: No tears, valve secure, bladders inflated to correct PSI
- [ ] Lines: No tangles, equal length, no fraying (especially at knots)
- [ ] Bar: Depower system functional, safety release tested
- [ ] Harness: Hook secure, spreader bar intact, leash attached
- [ ] Board: No cracks, fins tight, footstraps/bindings secure
- [ ] Safety leash: Connected, swivel working, quick release tested

### Environmental Assessment (5 minutes)
- [ ] Wind: Direction stable, speed within kite range, gust spread acceptable
- [ ] Weather: No storms approaching, visibility good, temperature safe
- [ ] Water: Depth adequate, current strength known, tide stage noted
- [ ] Obstacles: Launch/land areas clear, downwind hazards identified

### Personal Readiness (2 minutes)
- [ ] Skill level appropriate for conditions
- [ ] Physical state: Well-rested, hydrated, no injuries
- [ ] Mental state: Focused, confident, no pressure to perform
- [ ] Someone aware of your session plan and return time

## Risk Mitigation Strategies

### Before Session
- Check multiple weather sources (not just one app)
- Observe conditions for 10+ minutes before setup
- Start with smaller kite if between sizes
- Identify and brief on emergency procedures
- Establish check-in times with shore contact

### During Session
- Continuously scan for condition changes
- Maintain awareness of position relative to hazards
- Stay within proven skill boundaries
- Take breaks to reassess conditions
- Have clear escalation plan if conditions deteriorate

### Abort Triggers (End Session Immediately)
- Wind increases >5 knots above comfort level
- Equipment failure or damage
- Physical exhaustion or injury
- Lightning, storm cells approaching
- Offshore wind shift
- Loss of situational awareness

## Limitations

- Conditions can change rapidly; static assessment becomes outdated
- Local knowledge often critical; framework cannot replace experience
- Individual risk tolerance varies; framework provides baseline only
- Some hazards (e.g., currents, marine life) may not be immediately visible
- Overconfidence can lead to checkbox mentality without genuine assessment

**Remember**: When in doubt, don't go out. No session is worth serious injury. Conservative decisions build longevity in the sport.
