# Kitesurfing Session Preparation Framework

Use this framework to systematically prepare for kitesurfing sessions from initial planning through post-session review, ensuring safety and maximizing session quality.

## Core Concept

The Session Preparation Framework structures pre-session planning, setup procedures, and post-session review across four time horizons. Proper preparation reduces equipment failure risk, prevents avoidable accidents, and optimizes time on the water.

### The Four Preparation Phases

1. **24-Hour Planning**: Forecast review, equipment selection, logistics coordination
2. **On-Site Assessment**: Real-time condition verification, setup area selection
3. **Equipment Setup**: Systematic rigging, safety checks, launch preparation
4. **Post-Session Review**: Debrief, equipment care, session logging

## When to Apply

- Before every kitesurfing session (comprehensive workflow)
- Teaching new riders proper preparation habits
- Group session organization and coordination
- Travel sessions requiring rental equipment verification
- After accidents or near-misses (review what was missed)
- Seasonal refresh after extended breaks

## Equipment Setup Procedure (20-30 minutes)

### Pre-Setup Organization

**Lay Out Gear Systematically**:
1. Place kite bag upwind, board and harness downwind
2. Position pump and tools within reach
3. Keep bar/lines in bag until kite inflated (prevent tangles)
4. Secure all loose items (bags, clothes) so they don't blow away

### Kite Inflation & Inspection

**Inflation Steps**:
- [ ] **One-pump connection**: All struts connected to manifold
- [ ] **Leading edge**: Inflate to firm (not rock-hard), typically 7-9 PSI
- [ ] **Struts**: Check all inflated evenly, no soft spots
- [ ] **Valve closure**: Caps closed tightly, tested by pressing bladder

**Visual Inspection** (critical safety check):
- [ ] **Canopy fabric**: No tears, thin spots, or UV damage
- [ ] **Seams**: Stitching intact, no separation
- [ ] **Bladders**: No wrinkles or folds (can cause wear)
- [ ] **Bridle**: All connection points secure, no twisted lines
- [ ] **Wingtips**: Reinforced areas intact (high-stress zone)

### Bar & Line Setup

**Line Attachment**:
- [ ] **Leader lines**: Connect bar to kite in correct configuration (usually color-coded)
- [ ] **Check line length**: Walk out lines, verify equal length (adjust if uneven)
- [ ] **Bridle routing**: Ensure bridles not wrapped around struts
- [ ] **Line condition**: Feel for nicks, fraying (especially near knots)

**Bar System Check**:
- [ ] **Depower system**: Pull in/out, verify trim strap function
- [ ] **Safety release**: Activate primary safety, confirm kite flags out
- [ ] **Reset**: Re-engage safety, verify positive click/connection
- [ ] **Quick release**: Test secondary safety (if equipped)
- [ ] **Center lines**: Not twisted around bar ends

### Harness & Personal Setup

**Harness Preparation**:
- [ ] **Fit check**: Snug but not restrictive, comfortable position
- [ ] **Hook**: Spreader bar secure, no cracks or wear
- [ ] **Leash**: Connected to harness, other end ready for bar
- [ ] **Leash routing**: Won't tangle in hook or cause twisting

**Personal Safety Gear**:
- [ ] **Helmet**: Secure strap, positioned correctly (not too far back)
- [ ] **Impact vest**: Snug fit, doesn't ride up when arms raised
- [ ] **Wetsuit**: Zipped fully, comfortable range of motion
- [ ] **Booties**: If needed, tight enough not to slip off
- [ ] **Sunscreen**: Applied 15+ minutes prior (water resistance activated)

### Final Pre-Launch Checks (Critical)

**The 5-Point Safety Check**:
1. **Kite**: Inflated, no damage, bridle correct
2. **Lines**: Equal length, no tangles, connected correctly
3. **Bar**: Depower works, safety releases tested and reset
4. **Harness**: Secure fit, hook functional, leash ready
5. **Conditions**: Wind stable, launch area clear, escape route identified

**Launch Assistant Briefing** (if applicable):
- Show launch signal: [Thumbs up when ready]
- Explain hold position: [Wingtips, even pressure]
- Agree on abort signal: [Drop kite immediately if...]
- Confirm wind direction: [Launch into wind, kite overhead]

## Launch Procedure

### Self-Launch (Advanced Riders)

**Secure Setup**:
- [ ] Weight or stake bar downwind (cannot blow away)
- [ ] Position kite at edge of wind window (5 or 7 o'clock)
- [ ] Sand/weight on one wingtip, leading edge facing up
- [ ] Lines laid straight upwind from kite

**Launch Sequence**:
1. **Final check**: Wind stable, area clear, kite positioned correctly
2. **Connect leash**: Attach bar to harness leash
3. **Grab bar**: Walk upwind with bar, lines tensioning
4. **Reach edge**: When lines taut, hook in
5. **Power up**: Sheet in gently, kite rises from tip
6. **Control**: Fly to 12 o'clock, stable overhead
7. **Entry**: Wade to depth, launch with board

### Assisted Launch (Standard)

**Assistant Position**:
- Holds kite at wingtips (10 & 2 o'clock position)
- Leading edge pointing skyward, wingtips level
- Stands evenly weighted, ready to release together

**Launcher Sequence**:
1. **Hook in**: Bar attached to harness, leash connected
2. **Signal ready**: Thumbs up, clear communication
3. **Assistant releases**: Kite rises, launcher sheets in gradually
4. **Overhead**: Fly to 12 o'clock, stable before water entry
5. **Clear assistant**: Walk/wade to water, assistant clear

**Abort Conditions**:
- Wind gust during launch (wait for lull)
- Kite flips or tips unevenly
- Lines tangled or bar not functioning correctly
- Assistant loses grip prematurely

## Session Execution

### In-Session Monitoring

**Continuous Assessment**:
- [ ] **Wind changes**: Strength increasing/decreasing (kite size appropriate?)
- [ ] **Weather**: Clouds building, storms approaching, visibility
- [ ] **Position**: Distance from shore, downwind drift, proximity to hazards
- [ ] **Equipment**: Any unusual sounds, handling changes, damage
- [ ] **Physical state**: Fatigue, cold, dehydration (take breaks)

**Session Limits**:
- **Time**: [Planned duration, not exceeding skill/endurance]
- **Distance**: [Maximum offshore distance based on self-rescue capability]
- **Conditions**: [Abort triggers - wind >X, <Y, direction shift, weather]

### Mid-Session Breaks

**When to Break**:
- Every 30-60 minutes (hydration, assessment)
- Kite size no longer optimal (switch kites)
- Fatigue affecting control or decision-making
- Weather changes requiring reassessment

**Break Procedure**:
- Land kite safely, secure (weight/helper)
- Remove harness, rest properly (don't rush)
- Hydrate, snack if needed
- Check equipment for damage/wear
- Reassess conditions before relaunch

## Post-Session Procedure

### Safe Conclusion

**Landing**:
- [ ] Choose landing zone: [Clear area, assistant available if possible]
- [ ] Approach upwind: [Minimize power, controlled descent]
- [ ] Depower fully**: [Trim strap out, bar pushed away]
- [ ] Touchdown: [Wingtips first, gentle contact, assistant grabs]
- [ ] Immediate safety: [Activate safety release, secure kite]

**Pack-Down**:
- [ ] Deflate kite: [Open valves, roll leading edge]
- [ ] Fold properly: [Wingtips to center, roll from leading edge]
- [ ] Disconnect lines: [Prevent tangles in transit]
- [ ] Rinse gear: [Fresh water if available, especially bar/lines]
- [ ] Secure all items: [Nothing left behind, no wind-blown trash]

### Equipment Care

**Immediate Attention**:
- [ ] **Rinse salt water**: [Spray kite, thoroughly rinse bar/lines, harness]
- [ ] **Dry before storing**: [Hang or lay flat, avoid sun exposure]
- [ ] **Check for damage**: [Note any repairs needed]

## Common Preparation Mistakes

| Mistake | Consequence | Prevention |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| **Skipping safety check** | Equipment failure mid-session | Always complete 5-point check |
| **Rushing setup** | Tangles, improper connections | Allow full 20-30 minutes |
| **Ignoring wind changes** | Wrong kite size, dangerous conditions | Reassess on-site, not just forecast |
| **Solo without plan** | No rescue if trouble | Always tell someone your plan |
| **Neglecting hydration** | Fatigue, poor decisions, cramps | Drink before, break every 30-60min |
| **Old/damaged gear** | Mid-session failure, safety risk | Inspect before every session |
| **Poor launch area** | Collision, obstacle strike | Scout thoroughly, choose wisely |

## Emergency Preparation

### Emergency Kit (Keep in Vehicle)

**First Aid**:
- [ ] Waterproof first aid kit (cuts, scrapes, major bleeding supplies)
- [ ] Emergency blanket (hypothermia)
- [ ] CPR mask
- [ ] Emergency contact card (medical info, allergies)

**Communication**:
- [ ] Fully charged phone in waterproof case
- [ ] Emergency numbers saved (local coast guard, rescue)
- [ ] Whistle (signal for help)
- [ ] VHF radio (for offshore/remote locations)

**Repair & Recovery**:
- [ ] Spare pump + patch kit (bladder repair)
- [ ] Duct tape (temporary fixes)
- [ ] Spare lines/pigtails
- [ ] Multi-tool/knife

### Self-Rescue Rehearsal

**Practice Skills** (at least annually):
- [ ] Flag kite and body drag upwind
- [ ] Wrap lines and convert to kite sled
- [ ] Tow kite on leading edge while swimming/body dragging
- [ ] Signal distress (arm waving, whistle)
- [ ] Re-launch from water (if kite drops)

## Limitations

- Weather can change unpredictably; preparation reduces but doesn't eliminate risk
- Local conditions vary; framework must adapt to specific spot characteristics
- Equipment age and history affect reliability beyond visible inspection
- Individual risk tolerance and skill level affect appropriate decision thresholds
- Group dynamics can pressure poor decisions; maintain personal safety standards

**Prime Directive**: Never let time pressure, peer influence, or "forecast says it'll die" rush preparation. Thorough setup saves lives and equipment.
