# Corseaux Planning & Zoning References (PGA / PACom / PAC Lavaux)

Use this framework to understand the actual land-use planning instruments that govern what can be built, extended, or renovated on a specific parcel in Corseaux — this is the layer beneath the market-price context in [Corseaux & Riviera Vaudoise Local Market](corseaux-riviera-vaudoise-local-market.md), and directly determines what a [Renovation Permits & Costs in Vaud](renovation-permits-costs-vaud.md) CAMAC filing will actually be allowed to do on a given lot.

## Core Concept

A Corseaux parcel sits under **up to four overlapping planning instruments simultaneously**, not one:

```
What governs a Corseaux parcel?

[1. Communal zoning — RPGA/PGA]   currently in force (1982/1993), being replaced
[2. Communal zoning — PACom]      the REVISION replacing #1, not yet adopted (2026)
[3. Cantonal overlay — PAC Lavaux] vineyard/agricultural protection, not yet in force
[4. Emerging — tree heritage reg.] mandated by 2022 cantonal law, still being drafted
        +
[Natural hazard overlay — flood risk] mapped for part of the buildable zone
```

**The practical consequence**: the zoning rules quoted below (from the current RPGA) are what's legally binding *today*, but Corseaux is mid-revision on nearly every one of these layers. A parcel's buildable rights could look different once the PACom is adopted (expected 2026) — so any purchase or renovation decision should be checked against **both** the current instrument and the pending revision, not just the one that's technically in force this week.

## When to Apply

- You are evaluating buildable rights, extension potential, or renovation limits on a **specific Corseaux parcel**
- You want to know whether a target property might be affected by the commune's in-progress rezoning (PACom)
- You are considering a property near the lakeshore or near vineyard/agricultural land and want to know what protective overlays apply
- You want to know who to contact and what document to request before making an offer

## Instrument 1: The Current Communal Zoning (RPGA / PGA)

**What's in force today**: Corseaux's **Règlement du Plan Général d'Affectation (RPGA)** — the commune-level zoning regulation, originally adopted by the commune (session of **15 March 1982**), with the overall Plan Général d'Affectation approved by the **Conseil d'État vaudois on 25 June 1993** (this later date is documented specifically for zones "to be developed by special plans," suggesting the instrument has been amended in layers over time — treat the exact adoption history as something to confirm parcel-by-parcel rather than a single clean date).

**Known zone categories** (from the regulation's own zone map legend):
| Zone type | Marked as | What it generally means |
|---|---|---|
| Zones ruled by extension/development plans | Red | Built-up/village-type areas |
| Zones to be developed by special plan (plan spécial) | Grey | Development contingent on a separate, parcel-specific special plan being adopted first |
| Zones de verdure (green/landscape zones) | Green | Restricted or non-buildable landscape zones |
| Zone de villas | — | Residential low-density zone, rules below |

**Zone de villas — the specific building-rights numbers found in the regulation**:
- **Minimum parcel size**: a dwelling may not be built on a parcel smaller than **800 m²**
- **Coefficient d'occupation du sol (COS — building footprint ratio)**: **0.20** if the building has a single visible storey below the cornice, or **0.15** if it has two visible storeys below the cornice (i.e., the ratio tightens as the building gets taller)
- **Maximum façade length**: **22 metres**

**Why this matters for renovation/extension planning**: if a target property is in the zone de villas, the COS numbers above directly cap how much additional footprint an extension can add — a 1,000 m² parcel at COS 0.15 (two-storey) caps total building footprint at 150 m², *including* the existing structure. Check the existing building's footprint against this cap before assuming an extension is feasible.

**Who administers this today**: the **BTI (Bureau Technique Intercommunal)** — the shared technical/building office serving Corseaux and neighboring communes — handles permit applications and zone certificates under this regulation, feeding into the cantonal CAMAC process described in [Renovation Permits & Costs in Vaud](renovation-permits-costs-vaud.md).

## Instrument 2: The PACom Revision (In Progress — Not Yet Adopted)

Vaud's cantonal spatial-planning reform requires every commune to replace its old PGA with a modernized **Plan d'affectation communal (PACom)**, reconciling cantonal densification targets with the commune's wish to preserve a village character. Corseaux's revision is **actively underway but not yet adopted**:

| Milestone | Date |
|---|---|
| First public inquiry | 9 November – 9 December 2024 (info session 11 Nov 2024) |
| Conciliation with opponents | January 2025 |
| Municipality revises proposal based on valid objections; bureau Plarel drafts supplementary Art. 47 OAT report | Early-mid 2025 |
| Complementary public inquiry (only on the amended points) | 27 August – 25 September 2025 |
| Municipal council adoption vote | **Préavis n° 01-2026** — on the Corseaux Conseil communal's 2026 docket |
| Cantonal final approval | After communal adoption — not yet scheduled |

**Known substantive changes in the draft PACom**:
- A **new 15-metre green/buffer zone along the lakeshore** — directly relevant to any lakefront or near-lakefront parcel; this would constrain construction/rebuilding closer to the shore than may currently be assumed.
- Per municipal council discussion, **the large majority of parcels are reportedly NOT affected** by the rezoning changes — but the exceptions matter, and "not affected" should be confirmed for the specific parcel, not assumed.
- The PACom revision has real teeth in the meantime: as of April 2025, an EMS (care home) and crèche redevelopment project in Corseaux was **frozen pending PACom validation**, with the matter separately stuck at the Federal Tribunal for 22 months — illustrating that affected projects can be blocked for years while the revision is pending.

**Practical implication**: if a target parcel might fall inside the new 15m lakeshore buffer or any other amended zone, get written confirmation from the BTI/commune of how the **draft** PACom treats that specific parcel, not just its current RPGA zone — a purchase decision or renovation plan timed against the *current* rules could be overtaken by the revision before a permit is filed.

## Instrument 3: PAC Lavaux (Cantonal Overlay)

The **Plan d'affectation cantonal Lavaux (PAC Lavaux)** implements the **Loi sur le plan de protection de Lavaux ("Loi Lavaux")**, approved by Vaud popular vote in 2014, protecting agricultural and viticultural land **outside building zones** across ten Lavaux communes, including **Corseaux**.

**Status (September 2026)**: still **not in force**. Timeline: first Grand Conseil debate began March 2024; the Conseil d'État made modifications in June 2024, triggering a complementary public inquiry on those specific changes; the cantonal parliamentary commission was set to resume review of new oppositions/observations in **early 2026**, ahead of a second Grand Conseil debate. No confirmed entry-into-force date as of this writing — treat this as an active, unresolved legislative process to re-check before relying on it.

**Relevance to a Corseaux purchase**: PAC Lavaux governs land **outside** the commune's building zone (za bâtir) — i.e., vineyard and agricultural terraces. It's most relevant if a target property **borders or includes** vineyard/agricultural land (common on the Corseaux hillside toward Mont-Pèlerin), since it can constrain what happens on the non-built portion of a larger parcel, even when the house itself sits in an ordinary residential zone.

## Instrument 4 (Emerging): Tree Heritage Protection Regulation

Following the revised **Loi sur la protection du patrimoine naturel et paysager (LPrPNP)** (amended 30 August 2022) and its implementing ordinance (in force **1 July 2024**), Vaud communes are now required to adopt a specific regulation protecting significant/heritage trees on their territory.

**Status**: as of the most recent council record available (April 2025), Corseaux and three neighboring communes ("le cercle") had formed a joint working group, working from a cantonal model regulation, with the draft still under municipal review before going to the cantonal department for preliminary examination, followed by a communal council commission and adoption vote. **Not yet adopted** as of the latest confirmed information — check current status before relying on it.

**Relevance**: once adopted, this regulation will require authorization before removing or significantly impacting protected trees — relevant to any renovation, extension, or landscaping plan on a wooded or mature-tree parcel.

## Natural Hazard Overlay: Flood Risk

A dedicated technical document ("transcription des dangers naturels") flags that **part of Corseaux's buildable zone is exposed to flood risk**, prepared as supporting material for the PACom revision. This is a parcel-specific overlay, not a blanket commune-wide designation — a specific address needs to be checked against the hazard map rather than assumed either way.

## Practical Due-Diligence Checklist (Before Making an Offer)

For any specific Corseaux parcel under consideration:

1. **Request a zone certificate / extrait du plan et règlement d'affectation** from the BTI or the commune, confirming the parcel's current RPGA zone (village / villas / special-plan / verdure) and applicable COS, height, and setback rules.
2. **Ask explicitly whether the parcel is among those affected by the draft PACom** — don't rely on the "large majority unaffected" statistic; get parcel-specific confirmation, especially for anything near the lakeshore (15m buffer) or near a zone boundary.
3. **If the parcel borders vineyard/agricultural land**, check its position relative to the PAC Lavaux perimeter — this can matter even when the house itself sits inside the building zone.
4. **Check the flood-risk/natural-hazard map layer** for the specific parcel.
5. **Note any mature/heritage trees** on or near the parcel as a forward-looking risk once the tree protection regulation is adopted.
6. **Cross-check via CartoRiviera** (the regional cadastral/GIS portal covering Corseaux and neighboring communes) for cadastral boundaries, zoning layers, slope/hazard data, and any architectural inventory notes on the building itself.
7. **Track live PACom status** via the commune's own published council minutes (procès-verbaux) and préavis list on corseaux.ch — Préavis n° 01-2026 is the one to watch for the adoption vote.

## Common Pitfalls

**Treating the current RPGA numbers (800 m² minimum, COS 0.15–0.20) as permanent.** They're what's legally binding today, but the PACom revision could change them for the specific parcel — verify against the draft, not just the 1982/1993 text.

**Assuming a lakefront or near-lakefront property is unaffected by the PACom.** The proposed 15m shoreline buffer zone is exactly the kind of change that hits lakefront parcels specifically while leaving most of the commune untouched.

**Ignoring the PAC Lavaux perimeter because the house itself isn't in a vineyard.** A parcel that includes or borders agricultural/vineyard land can still be constrained on that portion even if the residential building sits in an ordinary zone.

**Not asking about flood-risk exposure.** It's a specific, mapped overlay for part of the buildable zone — not something to assume away based on the property "not looking like it's near water."

**Assuming BTI/commune records are as current as a canton-wide search suggests.** Planning documents on communal websites (b-t-i.ch, corseaux.ch) can be intermittently unreachable or out of date — confirm directly with the commune's technical office (BTI) or a local notary before relying on any single source, including this one.

## Limitations

This is an educational overview assembled from public search results, cantonal (État de Vaud) announcements, and Corseaux's own published council minutes (procès-verbal of 7 April 2025) — it is **not** a substitute for an official zone certificate or a direct BTI/commune inquiry on a specific parcel. Some primary planning documents (the full RPGA text, the flood-risk overlay map) were not directly retrievable during this research session due to a connectivity issue reaching b-t-i.ch — the zoning figures above were captured via search-engine summaries of that document rather than the full source text, so **verify the exact current wording directly with the BTI or commune before relying on the COS/parcel-size figures for a real transaction**. The PACom, PAC Lavaux, and tree-heritage regulation are all live, moving processes — re-check status before acting on any date or provision here.
