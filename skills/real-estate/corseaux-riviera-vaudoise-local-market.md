# Corseaux & Riviera Vaudoise Local Market

Use this framework when evaluating properties specifically in **Corseaux** or the surrounding Riviera vaudoise communes (Vevey, La Tour-de-Peilz, Corsier-sur-Vevey, Chardonne, Blonay, Montreux).

## Core Concept

Corseaux is a **small, dense, lakefront-adjacent commune** with a limited property stock — its entire territory is ~106 hectares. That combination (small supply + Lake Geneva frontage + Riviera vaudoise desirability) means prices sit at the high end of the canton and per-listing comparables move the average significantly; treat any single "average price/m²" figure as a rough anchor, not a precise valuation, and always pull live listings for the specific street/lake-proximity.

## When to Apply

- You are evaluating a specific property in Corseaux against market norms
- You are deciding between Corseaux and a neighboring Riviera vaudoise commune (Vevey, Corsier-sur-Vevey, La Tour-de-Peilz, Chardonne)
- You want context on what drives price variation within this small market (lake view vs. no view, vineyard-adjacent, hillside vs. shoreline)

## About Corseaux

- **Location**: Riviera-Pays-d'Enhaut district, Canton de Vaud, on the shore of Lake Geneva, at the foot of Mont-Pèlerin, between Vevey and Corsier-sur-Vevey.
- **Size**: ~106 hectares — one of the smallest communal territories in the canton.
- **Population**: ~2,300 residents ("Corsalins"/"Corsalines").
- **Character**: Historically agricultural/vineyard village, transformed over the 20th century into a residential lakeside community while retaining vineyard terraces. Extends ~2 km along the lakeshore.
- **Notable heritage**: Villa "Le Lac" — a Le Corbusier-designed house (1923) built for his parents, now a museum, sits directly on the Corseaux lakeshore. This gives the commune an architectural pedigree that shows up in marketing of higher-end listings nearby.

Corsier-sur-Vevey's steep 12-month house appreciation (+17.7%) is worth flagging when comparing: it suggests that commune has repriced sharply upward recently, which could mean either momentum (buy before it moves further) or a temporarily inflated comp set (a few high sales skewing a small-sample average) — check transaction count, not just the average, before treating that figure as representative.

**Reading these numbers**: all three communes sit in a tight CHF 11,000–12,700/m² band — meaningfully above the Vaud cantonal average — reflecting Riviera vaudoise / lakefront demand generally, not something unique to any one of the three. Differentiation between them is more about specific property (view, plot, condition) than commune-level pricing.

## What to Know Before Buying in Corseaux Specifically

- **Thin market**: with ~2,300 residents and 106 hectares, the number of listings at any given time is small. Be ready to move fast on a good match and expect fewer "similar" comparables than in a larger commune — get a local agent's read on recent off-market or pocket-listing activity, not just portal listings.
- **Vineyard and heritage constraints**: land near the Lavaux/Chablais vineyard terraces and near heritage sites (e.g., around Villa Le Lac) may carry stricter building/renovation constraints (protected views, facade rules) — factor this into renovation planning (see [Renovation Permits & Costs in Vaud](renovation-permits-costs-vaud.md)) before assuming a free hand on structural changes.
- **Lakefront premium is steep and non-linear**: direct shoreline or unobstructed lake-view properties command a premium well above the commune average — don't anchor on the average CHF/m² figure for a lakefront-specific property.
- **Cross-check against transaction data, not just asking prices**: portal listings (Homegate, Comparis, ImmoStreet, Properstar) show asking prices; RealAdvisor/Neho-style averages blend recent transactions — use both, since asking-to-sale gaps can be material in a thin market.

## Limitations

Price/m² figures here are point-in-time snapshots (September 2026) drawn from real estate market aggregators (RealAdvisor, Neho) and will drift — re-pull current figures before making an offer, and always get a formal appraisal or comparative market analysis from a local agent for any specific property rather than relying on commune-level averages.
