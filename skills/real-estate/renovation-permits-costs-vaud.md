# Renovation Permits & Costs in Vaud

Use this framework once a property is under consideration or purchased and renovation planning starts — covering the Vaud permit process (CAMAC) and realistic Swiss renovation cost ranges.

## Core Concept

In Vaud, almost every renovation touching a building's structure, envelope, or footprint needs to go through the cantonal **CAMAC** clearinghouse — the question is not "permit or no permit" but **which procedure** (full public inquiry vs. exempted) and **how long it takes**. Budget both money and calendar time before committing to a renovation-dependent purchase or move-in date.

## When to Apply

- You are budgeting renovation costs as part of a purchase decision (see the [house purchase & renovation tracker](../../personal/real-estate/house-purchase-renovation.md))
- You are planning structural work (extensions, facade changes, insulation, window changes) and need to know if/how a permit applies
- You are sequencing a purchase-to-move-in timeline and need realistic permit lead times
- You are deciding whether to renovate before or after a tax-relevant deadline (e.g., the 2029 valeur locative abolition)

## The CAMAC Process

**CAMAC** (Centrale des autorisations en matière de construction) is the canton's single intake point for building permit applications: it receives the application, circulates it across relevant cantonal services for authorizations, and returns a consolidated response to the commune, which issues the permit.

**2026 platform change**: applications are filed via the **ACTIS-CAMAC** online platform. From **1 September 2026**, new users (or anyone renewing their electronic ID) must use an **AGOV.ch** identifier instead of the prior login method — if you or your architect haven't used the portal recently, expect to set up AGOV access before filing.

### Two Tracks

| Track | When it applies | Typical timeline |
|---|---|---|
| **Full public inquiry (mise à l'enquête publique)** | Default for most permit-requiring work — structural changes, extensions, new construction, significant facade/volume changes | ~6–9 months if uncontested; **12–18 months if there are objections** during the public notice period |
| **Exempted from public inquiry (dispensé d'enquête publique)** | Minor works meeting Art. 72d RLATC criteria — e.g., an overhang/balcony, exterior insulation, an access ramp, a small non-habitable structure (garage, pool, cabin) — **if** it causes no material impact on neighbors, public interest, or the environment | Faster — commune circulates without the full public notice period |

**Critical distinction**: exemption from **public inquiry** is not exemption from the **permit itself**. Every dispensed project still goes through CAMAC filing and cantonal service checks (Art. 111 LATC) — "dispensed" only skips the neighbor-notice/objection window, not the authorization process.

### Practical Sequencing

1. Engage an architect or specialized permit consultant early — they determine which track applies and prepare the CAMAC file.
2. File via ACTIS-CAMAC (AGOV login from Sept 2026).
3. CAMAC circulates to cantonal services (heritage/environment/roads, etc. as relevant) and, for public-inquiry cases, opens the neighbor notice period.
4. Commune issues the permit once CAMAC's consolidated response comes back clean (or objections are resolved).
5. Only then can construction legally start.

**Budget the long end of the range** (9–18 months) for anything beyond cosmetic interior work, especially in heritage-sensitive or vineyard-adjacent zones like parts of the Riviera vaudoise/Corseaux area — see [Corseaux & Riviera Vaudoise Local Market](corseaux-riviera-vaudoise-local-market.md) for that local context.

## Typical Renovation Costs (Switzerland, 2026)

Order-of-magnitude figures — always get 2–3 local contractor quotes; regional labor costs and property condition swing these significantly.

| Item | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Kitchen renovation | CHF 20,000–50,000 (CHF ~6,000 basic layout refresh to CHF ~25,000+ for premium appliances/natural stone) |
| Bathroom renovation | CHF 15,000–30,000 per bathroom (CHF 2,500–7,500/m² depending on finish standard) |
| Whole-house renovation | Highly variable by scope (cosmetic vs. gut renovation vs. structural/insulation work) — get a per-project quote; don't rely on a single blended CHF/m² figure without specifying scope |

**Interior/cosmetic-only work** generally does not need a CAMAC permit at all (repainting, non-structural interior finishes, like-for-like kitchen/bathroom swaps without moving plumbing/walls) — confirm with the commune's building department for the specific scope, since the line between "cosmetic" and "permit-triggering" depends on whether plumbing, electrical routing, or load-bearing elements move.

## Timing Consideration: Valeur Locative Abolition (2029)

Mortgage interest and maintenance-cost deductions are currently offset against the **imputed rental value (valeur locative)** added to taxable income — see [Vaud Property Market & Buying Guide](vaud-property-market-buying-guide.md). This regime is confirmed through **end-2028**, with abolition from **1 January 2029**.

**Implication for renovation timing**: maintenance-cost deductibility against valeur locative is a "use it or lose it" benefit only while the regime exists. If a renovation is discretionary and its tax deductibility matters to your financing plan, completing it (and claiming the deduction) **before the 2029 change** captures a benefit that disappears afterward. This is a secondary factor — condition of the property and livability should still drive the primary timing decision — but it's worth factoring into a multi-year renovation phasing plan.

## Common Pitfalls

**Assuming "dispensed from public inquiry" means "no permit needed."** It doesn't — it only skips the neighbor notification window. CAMAC filing is still required.

**Underbudgeting the calendar, not just the cost.** A structural renovation with any objection risk can run 12–18 months from filing to permit — before construction even starts. If a purchase is contingent on renovating before move-in, build this into the overall project timeline in the [house purchase & renovation tracker](../../personal/real-estate/house-purchase-renovation.md).

**Treating a single CHF/m² renovation figure as a quote.** Kitchen, bathroom, and whole-house ranges vary 3–5x by finish standard — get scoped local quotes rather than budgeting off a blended average.

**Not checking heritage/vineyard-zone constraints before budgeting.** Properties near protected sites or AOC vineyard terraces (relevant on the Riviera vaudoise, including around Corseaux) may face stricter facade/volume rules that push a project onto the full public-inquiry track even for moderate changes.

## Limitations

This is an educational overview, not a substitute for a commune building department consultation or a licensed architect's assessment of a specific project. Permit track classification (Art. 72d RLATC), exact CAMAC/AGOV login requirements, and cost estimates should be verified for the specific property and scope before budgeting or committing to a purchase-renovation timeline.
