# Renovation Tax Deductions in Switzerland

Use this framework to plan *which* renovation spending is tax-deductible, when, and how — the single most consequential distinction is **value-preserving vs. value-enhancing** work, with an important carve-out for energy/environmental investments. This complements [Renovation Permits & Costs in Vaud](renovation-permits-costs-vaud.md) (the physical/permit side) and [Vaud Property Market & Buying Guide](vaud-property-market-buying-guide.md) (the valeur locative context this all sits inside).

## Core Concept

Swiss federal tax law (**Art. 32 al. 2 LIFD/DBG**, mirrored by cantonal law incl. Vaud) draws one line that decides deductibility:

```
Is the work "value-preserving" (entretien) or "value-enhancing" (plus-value)?

[Value-preserving / werterhaltend]        [Value-enhancing / wertvermehrend]
Repairs, like-for-like replacement,         Extensions, new living space,
recurring maintenance                        luxury fittings, land servicing
        ↓                                            ↓
  DEDUCTIBLE from taxable income        NOT deductible (added to cost basis,
  in the year incurred                   reduces future capital gains tax instead)

        EXCEPTION — always deductible regardless of which side of the line:
        • Energy-saving / environmental protection investments
        • Demolition costs for a replacement building (since 2020)
        • Historic monument protection measures (special conditions)
```

Getting this distinction right — and documenting it — is the single highest-leverage tax decision in a renovation project.

## When to Apply

- You are budgeting a renovation and want to know how much of the cost is tax-deductible
- You are deciding whether to phase a renovation across multiple tax years
- You are considering energy-efficiency upgrades (insulation, heat pump, solar, windows) and want to understand the tax treatment
- You are weighing a full demolition-and-rebuild against a renovation
- You are deciding between the **effective-cost** deduction and the **flat-rate (forfait)** deduction for a given tax year
- You are timing renovation work relative to the **2029 abolition of valeur locative** for owner-occupied homes

## The Core Distinction: Value-Preserving vs. Value-Enhancing

| Category | Examples | Tax treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Value-preserving (entretien)** — deductible | Repainting, re-roofing (like-for-like), replacing a heating system with an equivalent one, repairing plumbing/electrical, routine facade/garden maintenance | Fully deductible from taxable income in the year paid |
| **Value-enhancing (plus-value)** — not deductible as income | Building an extension, converting an attic to new living space, land servicing/viabilisation (raccordement), luxury installations beyond standard equipment | Not deductible against income; instead added to the property's cost basis, which **reduces the taxable gain** when the property is eventually sold (relevant to the Vaud real estate gains tax — see [Vaud Real Estate Succession Framework](../swiss-succession/vaud-real-estate-succession.md)) |

**The test in practice**: does the work restore/maintain the property's existing condition and function, or does it change/expand what the property *is* (more space, higher standard, new capability)? Replacing a mid-range kitchen with an equivalent mid-range kitchen is maintenance; replacing it with a significantly upgraded one may be partially maintenance (the replacement-in-kind portion) and partially value-enhancing (the upgrade portion) — mixed-scope projects often get split by the tax authority, so itemized contractor invoices matter.

## The Exception That Matters Most: Energy & Environmental Investments

**Since energy-saving and environmental-protection investments are explicitly deductible even when they would otherwise count as value-enhancing.** This is the one place the value-preserving/value-enhancing line doesn't decide the outcome.

**Covered examples**:
- Photovoltaic (solar) installations
- Building insulation upgrades (facade, roof, windows) — even where this improves the building beyond its original standard
- Heat pump or other efficient heating system installations, including switching *away* from a fossil system (not just like-for-like replacement)

**Since 2020, this exception was extended to demolition-for-reconstruction**: demolition costs are deductible **when a new building is constructed on the same land as the old one** — and notably, the replacement building does **not** need to match the old one's external appearance or volume to qualify. This makes a full teardown-and-rebuild materially more tax-efficient than it might appear at first glance, if the land is retained.

## Carry-Forward: Spreading Deductions Across Tax Periods

**Since 2020**, energy-saving/environmental investment costs (and the demolition-for-reconstruction costs above) that exceed your taxable income in the year the work is paid for can be **carried forward across a maximum of three consecutive fiscal periods** (the year of investment plus the two following years) — previously, any excess above that year's income was simply lost.

**Why this matters for planning**: a large energy retrofit (e.g., CHF 80,000 of insulation + heat pump work in one year) no longer needs to be timed against a single high-income year to capture the full deduction — spread the *claim* (not necessarily the payment) across up to three years if your income in the investment year doesn't fully absorb it. Cantons apply their own procedural rules for how this carry-forward is claimed on the cantonal/communal return even though the federal framework is uniform — confirm the Vaud mechanics with a tax advisor or the cantonal tax administration when the situation arises.

**This carry-forward provision does not extend to ordinary (non-energy) value-preserving maintenance costs** — those are simply deducted in the year paid, with no carry-forward if they exceed that year's income.

## Effective Costs vs. Flat-Rate (Forfait) Deduction — Vaud

Each tax period, **for each property**, a Vaud taxpayer chooses between two methods for the maintenance-cost deduction:

| Method | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Effective costs (frais effectifs)** | Deduct actual documented value-preserving expenses for the year — requires invoices |
| **Flat-rate deduction (forfait)** | Deduct a fixed percentage of the property's rental value/income instead of itemizing |

**Vaud flat-rate percentages**:

| Property type | Building < 20 years old | Building ≥ 20 years old (at start of tax period) |
|---|---|---|
| Owner-occupied (valeur locative basis) | 20% of valeur locative | 30% of valeur locative |
| Other private real estate (rental income basis) | 10% of gross rental income | 20% of gross rental income |

- The flat-rate option is **excluded once gross rental income exceeds CHF 150,000** — effective-cost documentation becomes mandatory above that threshold.
- The choice is made **per property, per tax period** — you can use the flat rate one year and switch to effective costs the next (e.g., the year of a major renovation), which is the standard strategy: use the flat rate in quiet years, switch to effective-cost itemization in the year(s) with real maintenance spending.

**Documentation reminder**: as covered in the [Vaud Property Market & Buying Guide](vaud-property-market-buying-guide.md), Vaud tightened documentation rules from 1 January 2026 — for the separate *gains tax on sale* calculation, only actual invoices/notary statements count (the old flat-rate fallback was abolished). Keep every renovation invoice regardless of which annual deduction method you use, since the underlying documentation need resurfaces at sale.

## Timing Consideration: The 2029 Valeur Locative Abolition

For owner-occupied homes, imputed rental value (valeur locative) — the mechanism this entire maintenance-deduction system is built to offset — is **confirmed through end-2028** and **abolished from 1 January 2029**.

**This means: 2026, 2027, and 2028 are the last three tax years in which ordinary value-preserving renovation costs are deductible for an owner-occupied home**, because after 2029 there is no more imputed rental income to deduct them against under the current design.

**Planning implication**: if you're weighing whether to do value-preserving maintenance work (roof, heating replacement, facade) sooner or spread it out over a longer horizon, doing it **before end-2028** captures a deduction that the 2029 reform removes for owner-occupied property. This is a secondary factor relative to genuine maintenance need and livability, but it is a real, dated planning window — not a vague future consideration.

**What's still unsettled**: whether a substitute incentive mechanism (e.g., a targeted energy-renovation credit or deduction outside the valeur-locative framework) survives the 2029 reform is an active policy question at the time of writing (September 2026) — several Swiss cantons and banks (e.g., BCV) are actively publishing "renovate now vs. later" guidance on this exact question. **Verify the confirmed post-2029 regime closer to the time** rather than assuming either full continuity or full loss of energy-investment deductibility.

## Other Deductible Items

Beyond direct renovation work, Vaud/federal practice also allows deduction of:
- Building insurance premiums
- Property management/administration fees
- Mortgage interest (see [Swiss Mortgage Law & Financing](swiss-mortgage-law-financing.md) — subject to its own limits, separate from the maintenance-cost question)

These sit alongside, not instead of, the maintenance-cost deduction choice above.

## Common Pitfalls

**Assuming a full renovation is 100% deductible.** Mixed-scope projects (e.g., a kitchen remodel that both replaces worn fixtures *and* expands the footprint) typically get split between deductible (replacement-in-kind) and non-deductible (the expansion) — request itemized contractor invoices that separate these, or the tax authority will make its own (less favorable) apportionment.

**Missing the flat-rate vs. effective-cost election in the right year.** Choosing the flat rate in a year with a major renovation forfeits the real deduction — check which method is better *before* filing, not after.

**Not knowing about the 3-year carry-forward for energy investments.** A large solar/insulation/heat-pump project paid for in one year doesn't need to be "wasted" against a low-income year — the excess can roll forward up to two additional years.

**Treating demolition-and-rebuild as automatically non-deductible.** Since 2020, demolition costs for a replacement building on the same land are deductible under the energy/environmental exception — this can materially change the economics of "renovate vs. rebuild" on an older property.

**Ignoring the 2026–2028 window for owner-occupied maintenance deductions.** Discretionary maintenance timing decisions should account for the fact that this deduction mechanism disappears for owner-occupied homes after 2028 under current law.

**Losing invoices.** Both the annual maintenance deduction (if using effective costs) and the eventual gains-tax calculation on sale depend on documented, itemized invoices — store every renovation invoice in the project's [Documents folder](../../personal/real-estate/house-purchase-renovation.md) indefinitely.

## Limitations

This is an educational overview of federal (LIFD/DBG) and Vaud cantonal tax treatment of renovation costs, not tax advice. The value-preserving/value-enhancing classification of a specific project, the exact carry-forward filing mechanics, and especially the **post-2029 treatment of energy-investment deductions** are all matters to confirm with a Vaud tax advisor or the cantonal tax administration (Administration cantonale des impôts) before relying on them for a specific renovation's financial planning.
