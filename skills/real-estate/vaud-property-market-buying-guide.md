# Canton de Vaud Property Market & Buying Guide

Use this framework for the Vaud-specific costs, taxes, and market context that sit on top of the general [Swiss Property Purchase Fundamentals](swiss-property-purchase-fundamentals.md).

## Core Concept

Vaud is a **high-transaction-cost, high-tax canton** relative to Swiss norms: buyer-side transfer duties and notary fees run close to 5% of price, and Vaud layers both an inheritance/gift tax and a real estate gains tax on top (see the companion succession skill). Budgeting for Vaud property should always add a ~5% cash buffer on top of price and equity, and factor cantonal tax exposure into any resale or transfer plan.

## When to Apply

- You are buying property physically located in Canton de Vaud (any commune — Lausanne, La Côte, Riviera vaudoise, etc.)
- You are budgeting notary/transfer costs for an offer
- You are comparing Vaud to a neighboring canton (Geneva, Fribourg) for a purchase decision
- You are estimating ongoing property tax exposure post-purchase
- You later plan to sell, gift, or pass the property to heirs (see [Vaud Real Estate Succession Framework](../swiss-succession/vaud-real-estate-succession.md) for the tax mechanics)

## Buyer-Side Transaction Costs

Vaud notary/transfer costs total **approximately 4–5% of the purchase price**, paid by the buyer (who therefore also chooses the notary):

| Component | Rate | Goes to |
|---|---|---|
| Droits de mutation (transfer duty) — cantonal share | 2.2% | Canton |
| Droits de mutation — communal share | up to 1.1% | Commune |
| **Transfer duty subtotal** | **~3.3%** | |
| Land registry registration | ~0.3% | Registre foncier |
| Notary's own fee | ~0.5–0.7% | Notary |
| **Total** | **~4–5%** | |

**Practical rule of thumb**: on a CHF 1,500,000 property, budget **CHF 60,000–75,000** in closing costs, in cash, on top of your 20% equity.

**Who picks the notary**: since the buyer pays, the buyer chooses the notary in Vaud (unlike some other cantons/situations). Shop notary fees — the ~0.5–0.7% notary component has some room to negotiate, even though transfer duty and registration fees are fixed by law.

## Real Estate Gains Tax (Impôt sur les gains immobiliers)

Separate from transfer duty — this applies **on future sale**, not on purchase, taxing the appreciation between purchase price and sale price. Rate is progressive and depends on **gain size** and **holding period** (shorter holds are taxed much more heavily; the rate steps down the longer you hold). This does not affect your purchase budget directly, but it should factor into any "buy now vs. wait," "flip vs. hold," or renovation-then-sell decision — a quick resale after a heavily renovated purchase can trigger a high effective rate on the uplift.

Full mechanics, rates, and planning strategies (including the inheritance exemption) are covered in [Vaud Real Estate Succession Framework](../swiss-succession/vaud-real-estate-succession.md).

## Ongoing Ownership Tax: Valeur Locative & Property Tax

- **Valeur locative (imputed rental value)**: added to taxable income for owner-occupiers; offset by deducting mortgage interest and maintenance costs. Confirmed in force through **end-2028**, with **abolition scheduled 1 January 2029**.
- **2026 revaluation**: the cantonal revaluation coefficient for built/unbuilt property tax bases is **1.008** (i.e., +0.8%) for 2026, based on the November 2025 Swiss CPI reading. This is a routine annual indexation, not a policy change.
- **Deductions available to Vaud owners**: valeur locative offset, mortgage interest, and maintenance costs (actual documented costs, or a flat-rate deduction — whichever is higher) are all deductible in the cantonal/federal tax return.
- **2026 documentation change on resale**: starting 1 January 2026, Vaud abolished the flat 4.5% deemed-cost deduction that used to apply when original notary/purchase invoices couldn't be found. Sellers now need the **actual purchase deed and notary fee statement** to claim transfer-cost deductions when calculating gains tax — **keep every notary invoice and the acte de vente indefinitely**; this documentation matters again whenever you eventually sell.

## Regional Market Context

Vaud's residential market varies sharply by district. Rough orientation (order of magnitude, not precise — always pull current comparables for the specific commune):

| Region | Character | Relative price level |
|---|---|---|
| Lausanne & agglomeration | Urban, dense, strong rental/investment demand | High |
| La Côte (Nyon–Morges, Geneva-facing) | Cross-border commuter belt, international schools, Geneva proximity premium | High to very high |
| Riviera vaudoise (Vevey–Montreux–Corseaux–Chardonne) | Lakefront, vineyard terraces (Lavaux UNESCO backdrop), strong lifestyle/second-home demand | High, lakefront and view properties command a significant premium |
| Gros-de-Vaud, Broye, Jura-Nord vaudois | More rural, commuter-distance to Lausanne | Moderate |

See [Corseaux & Riviera Vaudoise Local Market](corseaux-riviera-vaudoise-local-market.md) for commune-level detail on the Riviera vaudoise.

## Renovation & Building Permits

Any structural renovation in Vaud runs through the cantonal **CAMAC** (Centrale des autorisations en matière de construction) process, regardless of commune. See [Renovation Permits & Costs in Vaud](renovation-permits-costs-vaud.md) for the procedure, timelines, and what qualifies for a lighter-touch exemption.

## Common Pitfalls

**Budgeting only the 3.3% transfer duty and forgetting notary + land registry.** The full buyer-side cost is closer to 4–5%, not 3.3%.

**Not keeping notary/purchase documentation.** Since 1 January 2026, Vaud requires the actual deed and notary statement to deduct transfer costs on a future sale — the old flat-rate fallback is gone. Store these documents from day one (ideally alongside the [house purchase & renovation tracker](../../personal/real-estate/house-purchase-renovation.md)).

**Treating Vaud communes as a single market.** Price per m² can differ by 30%+ between neighboring communes on the Riviera vaudoise alone (lakefront vs. hillside, vineyard-adjacent vs. not) — always benchmark at the commune level, not the cantonal average.

**Ignoring the 2029 valeur locative abolition when timing renovations.** Maintenance/interest deductions against valeur locative disappear in the reform — renovations planned to offset several years of imputed income should be weighed against that horizon (see renovation skill).

## Limitations

This is an educational overview of Vaud-specific costs and process, not tax or legal advice. Transfer duty rates, revaluation coefficients, and the valeur locative reform timeline are all subject to change by cantonal/federal decision — verify current figures with the [État de Vaud tax administration](https://www.vd.ch/etat-droit-finances/impots/) or a Vaud notary before committing to a purchase.
