# Business Succession Planning Framework

Use this framework to plan succession for Swiss family businesses (AG, GmbH, partnerships), balancing ownership transfer, management transition, business continuity, and tax relief qualification.

## Core Concept

The Business Succession Planning Framework applies a dual-track approach that separates ownership succession (who owns shares) from management succession (who runs operations). Effective business succession ensures continuity, preserves value, qualifies for cantonal tax relief, and balances family and business interests.

### The Four Succession Dimensions

1. **Ownership Transfer**: Share transfer to next generation (gradual vs. immediate, equal vs. capability-based)
2. **Management Transition**: Leadership handoff (family vs. professional CEO, capability assessment, mentorship)
3. **Value Preservation**: Business continuity and growth during transition (avoid disruption, maintain relationships)
4. **Tax Optimization**: Qualification for cantonal business relief (80% reduction) and succession timing

### Succession Architecture

```
[Current Owner/Leader]
         ↓
    ┌────┴────┐
    │         │
[Ownership] [Management]
    │         │
    ├─ Shares   ├─ CEO/Executive
    ├─ Voting   ├─ Operations
    ├─ Economic ├─ Strategy
    │         │
    ↓         ↓
[Next Gen] [Successor or Professional]
```

## When to Apply

- Family business owner approaching retirement (age 55-70)
- Business value represents majority of estate wealth (succession planning critical for wealth transfer)
- Multiple heirs with different capabilities (some business-capable, some not)
- Desire to keep business in family vs. sell to external buyer
- Qualifying for cantonal business tax relief (80% reduction strategies)
- Business performance/continuity concerns during transition
- No clear successor identified yet (need systematic selection process)

## Swiss Business Structures

### 1. AG (Aktiengesellschaft) - Stock Corporation

**Characteristics:**
- **Shares**: Freely transferable unless articles of association restrict
- **Minimum capital**: CHF 100,000 (CHF 50,000 paid-in at formation)
- **Governance**: Board of directors (Verwaltungsrat), shareholders' meeting, executive management
- **Liability**: Limited to company assets (shareholders not personally liable)
- **Publicity**: Commercial register entries, annual filings public

**Succession advantages:**
- Shares easily divisible among multiple heirs (each receives X%)
- Can separate voting shares (management control) from non-voting economic shares (passive heirs)
- Board structure allows gradual transition (add successor to board, increase responsibilities)
- Professional management easier to integrate

**Succession challenges:**
- Minority shareholders can block certain decisions (need supermajority provisions in articles)
- Shareholder disputes if family conflict
- More formal structure (less flexibility than GmbH)

### 2. GmbH (Gesellschaft mit beschränkter Haftung) - Limited Liability Company

**Characteristics:**
- **Shares** (Stammanteile): Registered, transfer requires notarization (less liquid than AG)
- **Minimum capital**: CHF 20,000 (fully paid-in at formation)
- **Governance**: Managers (Geschäftsführer), shareholders' meeting
- **Liability**: Limited to company assets
- **Publicity**: Less formal requirements than AG

**Succession advantages:**
- Lower capital requirement than AG
- More private/confidential (fewer disclosure requirements)
- Flexible governance (manager can be non-shareholder)

**Succession challenges:**
- Share transfer requires notarization (more cumbersome for gradual transfers)
- Harder to create voting vs. non-voting classes
- Less suitable for many shareholders (becomes unwieldy with 10+ owners)

### 3. Einzelfirma (Sole Proprietorship)

**Characteristics:**
- **Ownership**: Single owner (sole trader)
- **Liability**: **Unlimited personal liability** (owner personally liable for business debts)
- **Simple**: No corporate structure, minimal formalities

**Succession:**
- **Cannot transfer**: Einzelfirma cannot be "inherited" as legal entity
- **Must convert**: Before succession, convert to AG or GmbH, then transfer shares
- **Or liquidate**: Close Einzelfirma, sell assets to successor

**Recommendation**: If succession planned, convert to AG/GmbH 2-5 years before to establish track record and qualify for tax relief

### 4. Partnerships (Kollektivgesellschaft, Kommanditgesellschaft)

**Less common for family succession** (complex partner dynamics, liability issues)

**Recommendation**: Convert to AG/GmbH for cleaner succession

## Ownership vs. Management Succession

### Separation Rationale

**Why separate:**
- **Equal ownership** (all heirs receive shares = equal economic value)
- **Unequal management capability** (only one/some heirs capable of running business)
- **Fair to all**: Non-business heirs receive economic benefit without operational burden

**Example:**
- 3 children, CHF 3M business
- Child A (MBA, 10 years business experience): Receives 1/3 ownership + CEO role
- Child B (doctor, no business interest): Receives 1/3 ownership, passive
- Child C (artist, no business capability): Receives 1/3 ownership, passive
- **Result**: Equal ownership (economic fairness), capable management (business continuity)

### Ownership Succession Strategies

#### Strategy 1: Equal Ownership, Capable Management

**Ownership**: All heirs receive equal shares (33% each)

**Management**: Only capable heir becomes CEO/board member

**Governance protections:**
- Shareholder agreement grants CEO broad operational authority (non-active siblings can't micromanage)
- Board composition: Majority independent directors + CEO (family minority on board to avoid sibling conflicts)
- Buyout option: Non-active heirs can sell shares to active heir at fair market value (FMV) if they want liquidity

**Advantages**: Fair to all heirs, capable management

**Challenges**: Minority shareholders (siblings) can still create conflicts (require strong shareholder agreement)

#### Strategy 2: Ownership to Capable Heir, Cash to Others

**Ownership**: Capable heir receives 100% of business shares

**Compensation**: Non-business heirs receive equivalent value in other assets (real estate, cash, investments)

**Example**:
- CHF 3M estate: CHF 1.5M business + CHF 1.5M other assets
- Child A: CHF 1M business shares
- Child B: CHF 1M real estate + cash
- Child C: CHF 1M stocks + bonds
- (Total slightly more to account for liquidity difference)

**Advantages**: Clean ownership (no minority shareholder conflicts), business preserved

**Challenges**: Requires sufficient non-business assets for equalization; valuation disputes ("Business is worth CHF 2M not CHF 1M!")

#### Strategy 3: Gradual Transfer During Life

**Timing**: Transfer shares over 10-20 years during life (not all at death)

**Structure**:
- Year 1: Transfer 10% to successor
- Year 5: Transfer additional 20% (now 30% total)
- Year 10: Transfer additional 30% (now 60% total, successor has control)
- Year 15: Transfer final 40% (successor has 100%)

**Advantages**:
- Successor proves capability gradually
- Tax optimization (gifts spaced to optimize cumulative gift tax)
- Founder maintains involvement and mentors
- Business continuity (no sudden change)

**Challenges**: Long timeline, founder must relinquish control gradually (difficult psychologically)

### Management Succession Options

#### Option 1: Family CEO

**When appropriate:**
- Capable family member exists (education, experience, desire)
- Family member has worked in business 5-10+ years (proven track record)
- Family member has external experience (credibility)

**Selection process:**
1. Formal qualification criteria (MBA/equivalent, X years external + internal experience)
2. Assessment by board (can be independent directors + family council)
3. Trial period (COO or division head first, then promote to CEO if successful)
4. Performance metrics (revenue growth, profit, employee retention)

**Advantages**: Family continuity, long-term commitment, alignment with family values

**Challenges**: Nepotism perception, may not be best qualified, family dynamics complicate performance management

#### Option 2: Professional (Non-Family) CEO

**When appropriate:**
- No capable family member (or family member not interested)
- Business complexity requires specialized expertise family lacks
- Family conflict makes family CEO untenable

**Structure:**
- Hire external CEO (market salary and compensation)
- Family remains owners (board of directors)
- Board oversees CEO (strategy, performance, compensation)

**Advantages**: Best qualified leader, merit-based, avoids family conflicts in operations

**Challenges**: Family must accept non-family running "family business," potential misalignment of incentives (CEO vs. owners)

#### Option 3: Co-CEOs or Hybrid (Family + Professional)

**Structure:** Family member + professional co-manage (President + COO, Co-CEOs, etc.)

**When appropriate:** Family member has some capability but needs support; transition period

**Challenges**: Dual leadership can be confusing, requires clear division of responsibilities

## Business Continuity & Stakeholder Management

### Stakeholder Communication

**Who needs to know:**
1. **Employees**: Reassurance (jobs safe, business continues)
2. **Customers**: Confidence (quality, service, relationships maintained)
3. **Suppliers**: Stability (contracts honored, payment terms unchanged)
4. **Lenders**: Risk assessment (debt obligations secure)
5. **Business partners**: Continuity (joint ventures, partnerships remain)

**Communication timeline:**

| Stakeholder | When to Inform | Message | Method |
|------------|---------------|---------|---------|
| **Key employees** | 12-18 months before | "Succession plan underway, business stable, jobs secure" | In-person meeting (senior staff), company-wide announcement |
| **Customers** | 6-12 months before | "New leadership transition, commitment to quality unchanged" | Letter from founder + successor, personal visits to major customers |
| **Suppliers** | 6 months before | "Ownership transition, contracts honored, terms unchanged" | Letter, phone calls to major suppliers |
| **Lenders** | 12 months before | "Succession plan, financial stability, debt service unaffected" | Formal presentation with financials |

**Key message**: Continuity, stability, commitment (not "everything will change")

### Business Continuity Planning

**Before succession:**
- [ ] Document key processes (operations manual, client relationships, supplier agreements)
- [ ] Cross-train employees (reduce dependency on founder's knowledge)
- [ ] Formalize client relationships (not just personal relationships of founder)
- [ ] Strengthen management team (capable team supports successor)
- [ ] Financial stability (clean up balance sheet, reduce debt, build reserves)

**During transition:**
- [ ] Founder available for mentorship (phone calls, advice, customer introductions)
- [ ] Gradual responsibility transfer (not all at once)
- [ ] Monitor key metrics (revenue, profit, customer retention, employee turnover)
- [ ] Address issues quickly (if problems arise, founder steps back in temporarily)

**After transition:**
- [ ] Founder step back (let successor lead, don't micromanage)
- [ ] Regular check-ins (quarterly board meetings, not daily operations)
- [ ] Celebrate milestones (public announcement, customer event, employee recognition of new leader)

## Valuation & Buyout Mechanisms

### Business Valuation Methods

**1. Asset-Based Valuation**
- Formula: Assets - Liabilities = Net Asset Value
- **Use when**: Holding company, real estate-heavy, liquidation scenario
- **Limitation**: Ignores ongoing business value (customers, brand, cash flow)

**2. Revenue Multiple**
- Formula: Annual Revenue × Industry Multiple
- Example: CHF 5M revenue × 0.5-1.5 multiple = CHF 2.5-7.5M
- **Use when**: Standard industry, comparable sales data available
- **Limitation**: Ignores profitability (high revenue, low profit over-valued)

**3. EBITDA Multiple**
- Formula: EBITDA × Industry Multiple
- Example: CHF 1M EBITDA × 4-6 multiple = CHF 4-6M
- **Most common** for profitable SMEs
- **Use when**: Profitable business, industry comparable data available

**4. Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)**
- Formula: Future cash flows discounted to present value
- **Most accurate** but complex
- **Use when**: Established cash flows, long-term projections reliable

**Professional valuation required**: CHF 5,000-20,000 for independent business appraisal

### Buyout Financing Mechanisms

**Scenario**: Capable heir receives business, must compensate siblings

**Example**: CHF 3M business, 3 heirs
- Heir A receives business (CHF 3M value)
- Owes siblings CHF 1M each (equal distribution = CHF 1M per heir)

**How to finance CHF 2M buyout:**

#### Mechanism 1: Life Insurance

**Structure**: Founder purchases CHF 2M life insurance, beneficiaries are non-business children

**At death**:
- Heir A inherits business (CHF 3M)
- Heirs B & C receive CHF 1M each from life insurance (total CHF 2M)
- **Result**: Heir A gets business, siblings get cash, equal value

**Advantages**: Clean, immediate liquidity, tax-free death benefit (most cantons)

**Cost**: Annual premiums (CHF 15,000-40,000/year depending on age, health)

#### Mechanism 2: Business Cash Flow Buyout

**Structure**: Heir A inherits business, signs promissory note to pay siblings over time

**Terms**: CHF 2M total, paid over 10 years = CHF 200K/year + interest

**Funding**: Business generates CHF 500K profit/year → CHF 200K to siblings, CHF 300K to Heir A and reinvestment

**Advantages**: No upfront cash needed, payments from business cash flow

**Challenges**: Business must remain profitable (risk if business declines), siblings wait years for full payment

#### Mechanism 3: Installment Sale During Life

**Structure**: Founder sells business to Heir A during life (not inheritance)

**Terms**: CHF 3M purchase price, CHF 500K down payment, CHF 2.5M financed over 10 years

**At founder's death**: Remaining debt is forgiven (estate plan), or Heir A receives inheritance to offset debt

**Advantages**: Gradual transfer, Heir A builds ownership stake, founder receives income during life

**Challenges**: Complex tax treatment (sale vs. gift), founder must trust Heir A's payments

### Shareholder Agreements

**Essential clauses for family business:**

**1. Buy-Sell Provisions (Shotgun Clause)**
- If shareholders deadlock: Shareholder A offers to buy Shareholder B's shares at price X
- Shareholder B must either: (1) Sell at price X, or (2) Buy Shareholder A's shares at same price X
- **Prevents stalemate**: Ensures someone exits

**2. Right of First Refusal**
- If shareholder wants to sell: Must offer to other family shareholders first (before external sale)
- **Keeps business in family**

**3. Drag-Along Rights**
- If majority shareholders (>75%) want to sell business: Minority must sell too
- **Prevents minority blocking** valuable sale opportunity

**4. Tag-Along Rights**
- If majority sells to external buyer: Minority can join sale at same terms
- **Protects minority** from being stuck with unknown new majority owner

**5. Valuation Mechanism**
- Pre-agreed formula or independent appraiser for buyouts
- **Avoids disputes** over "fair price"

**6. Non-Compete**
- Exiting shareholder cannot compete with business for X years
- **Protects business** from departing family member starting competitor

## Cantonal Business Tax Relief Qualification

### 80% Reduction Overview

**What it is**: Qualifying business succession = business valued at 20% of fair market value for inheritance tax purposes

**Example impact:**
- Business FMV: CHF 2M
- Heir (sibling, not child): Normally taxed at 12% sibling rate in Zurich = CHF 240,000 tax
- With 80% relief: Tax base = CHF 400K (20% of CHF 2M) → Tax = CHF 48,000
- **Savings: CHF 192,000**

### Qualification Requirements (Varies by Canton)

**Common criteria (Zurich example):**

1. **Active Operating Business**
   - [ ] Produces goods or services (not passive holding company)
   - [ ] Generates business income (not just rental or investment income)
   - [ ] Has employees (minimum 5-20 depending on canton)

2. **Continuation Requirement**
   - [ ] Heir must **continue operating** business for minimum period (typically 5-10 years)
   - [ ] Cannot sell or liquidate shortly after inheritance
   - [ ] Must maintain substantial business activity (revenue, employment)

3. **Management Requirement**
   - [ ] Heir must take **active management role** (CEO, COO, Board Chair - not passive owner)
   - [ ] Some cantons require full-time involvement
   - [ ] Documentation of management activity required

4. **Ownership Threshold**
   - [ ] Often requires >50% ownership transfer (not minority stakes)
   - [ ] Some cantons: Relief only on shares exceeding thresholds

5. **Swiss Presence**
   - [ ] Business located and operating in Switzerland
   - [ ] Swiss legal entity (AG, GmbH)
   - [ ] Some cantons: Must be in **same canton** for full relief

### Qualification Strategy

**Timeline: 3-5 years before succession:**

**Year -5:**
- Convert Einzelfirma to AG/GmbH (if sole proprietorship)
- Hire employees to meet minimum (if below threshold)
- Document active operations (not passive)

**Year -3:**
- Identify successor and begin transition
- Successor takes active management role (COO, VP)
- Establish track record of heir's involvement

**Year -1:**
- Professional business valuation
- Verify cantonal relief qualification (consult tax advisor)
- Document succession plan for tax authorities

**At succession (death or gift):**
- Transfer >50% ownership to heir
- Heir assumes CEO or equivalent role
- File for tax relief with cantonal authorities

**Years +1 to +5/+10 (continuation period):**
- Maintain business operations (revenue, employment)
- Heir actively manages (document involvement)
- Do NOT sell or liquidate
- **Risk**: Early sale triggers clawback (full tax due retroactively)

### Clawback Risk

**If heir sells/closes business within continuation period:**
- Tax relief is **revoked retroactively**
- Full inheritance tax becomes due (as if no relief)
- Plus interest from date of original inheritance
- **Penalty**: Can be very costly

**Example:**
- Year 0: Inherit business, pay CHF 50K tax (with 80% relief)
- Year 3: Sell business (within 5-year continuation period)
- **Clawback**: CHF 250K (full tax) - CHF 50K (already paid) = **CHF 200K now due + interest**

**Mitigation:** Only plan for relief if heir is **genuinely committed** to continuing business long-term

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: No Succession Plan Until Crisis
**The mistake**: Waiting until founder is seriously ill or dies suddenly to address succession.

**Why it's a problem**: Business leadership vacuum, panic decisions, no time to train successor, employee/customer uncertainty, value destruction (business worth less in chaos).

**The fix**: Start planning 5-10 years before anticipated retirement. Gradual transition >> sudden change.

### Pitfall 2: Assuming Family Member Will Run Business
**The mistake**: Assuming child wants to/can run business without assessing capability or interest.

**Why it's a problem**: Child may not want business (has own career), lack capability (no training, no aptitude), or feel pressured (resentment). Result: Bad leadership, business declines.

**The fix**: Assess capability AND desire early. If no qualified/willing family member, hire professional CEO. Family ownership ≠ family management.

### Pitfall 3: Equal Ownership With Unequal Capability
**The mistake**: Giving all children equal shares (33% each) when only one is capable of running business.

**Why it's a problem**: Minority shareholders (siblings) second-guess CEO sibling, create conflicts, demand dividends when reinvestment needed, deadlock on major decisions.

**The fix**: Either (1) Business to capable heir, cash to others, or (2) Strong shareholder agreement limiting minority shareholder interference + independent board.

### Pitfall 4: Losing Business Tax Relief Due to Early Sale
**The mistake**: Qualifying for 80% tax relief, then heir sells business in Year 2 (within 5-year continuation period).

**Why it's a problem**: Relief clawed back retroactively → massive unexpected tax bill + interest.

**The fix**: Only plan for relief if heir is genuinely committed to long-term operation (5-10+ years). Formal continuation commitment, shareholder agreement prohibiting sale.

### Pitfall 5: No Stakeholder Communication
**The mistake**: Announcing succession to employees/customers/suppliers AFTER it happens.

**Why it's a problem**: Uncertainty creates fear. Employees seek other jobs, customers switch suppliers (worried about quality/service), suppliers tighten credit. Business value declines.

**The fix**: Proactive communication 12-18 months before. Message: Stability, continuity, commitment. Introduce successor gradually (not sudden replacement).

## Limitations

### What This Framework Cannot Do

**This framework is a planning tool, not legal or business advice:**
- Business succession is highly specific to industry, company, family
- Tax relief rules vary by canton and change frequently
- This framework cannot replace business valuation, tax optimization, or legal document professionals

### When Professional Help Is Mandatory

**You MUST consult professionals for:**

1. **Business succession consultant**: Complex transitions, family business advisory
2. **Business valuator**: Professional appraisal (CHF 5,000-20,000)
3. **Tax advisor (cantonal specialist)**: Relief qualification, tax timing
4. **Corporate lawyer**: Shareholder agreements, governance documents, AG/GmbH conversions
5. **Business broker** (if considering sale): Market valuation, buyer identification
