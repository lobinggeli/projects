# Canton de Vaud Real Estate Succession Framework

Use this framework to navigate the unique challenges and opportunities of inheriting real estate in Canton de Vaud, where property succession involves both inheritance tax and real estate gains tax considerations.

## Core Concept

The Vaud Real Estate Succession Framework addresses the dual taxation system affecting property transfers: **inheritance tax on transfer** and **real estate gains tax on disposition**. Vaud's situs rule means all property located in Vaud is taxed by Vaud regardless of owner residence, making strategic planning essential for valuable estates.

### The Dual Tax Challenge

```
Vaud Real Estate Transfer

[Property Transfer Event]
         ↓
    ┌────┴────┐
    │         │
[Inheritance Tax]  [Real Estate Gains Tax]
(Relationship-based)  (Appreciation-based)
  0-50% depending     Up to 60%+ on gains
  on heir type        over holding period
```

Both taxes can apply simultaneously, making Vaud real estate succession among Switzerland's most complex.

## When to Apply

- You own real estate located in Canton de Vaud (regardless of your residence)
- You are inheriting Vaud property from a deceased relative
- You are planning succession for a Vaud primary residence, vacation home, or investment property
- You are considering selling vs. inheriting Vaud real estate
- You are structuring lifetime gifts of Vaud property
- You are comparing real estate succession strategies across Swiss cantons
- You own multiple properties and deciding which to sell/keep before death

## Vaud Real Estate Inheritance Tax

### Situs Rule: Location is Everything

**Key principle**: Real estate is **always taxed where it is physically located**, not where the owner lived.

**Vaud situs rule application**:
- Property located in Vaud = **Vaud taxes it** (regardless of deceased's residence)
- Deceased lived in Vaud but owned Geneva property = **Geneva taxes it** (not Vaud)

#### Example 1: Non-Vaud Resident Owns Vaud Property

**Scenario**:
- Deceased: Resident of Schwyz (0% inheritance tax canton)
- Owns: Vacation chalet in Montreux, Vaud (CHF 1,200,000)
- Heir: Sister (sibling)

**Taxation**:
- Schwyz does NOT tax (property not in Schwyz)
- **Vaud taxes** under sibling rate
- Tax calculation (sibling, CHF 1,200,000):
  - CHF 0-50K @ 3% = CHF 1,500
  - CHF 50-100K @ 6% = CHF 3,000
  - CHF 100-200K @ 9% = CHF 9,000
  - CHF 200-300K @ 12% = CHF 12,000
  - CHF 300-500K @ 15% = CHF 30,000
  - CHF 500K-1M @ 18% = CHF 90,000
  - CHF 1-1.2M @ 25% = CHF 50,000
  - **Total tax: CHF 195,500** (16.3% effective rate)

**Implication**: Living in tax-free Schwyz does NOT protect Vaud real estate. Sister receives net CHF 1,004,500.

#### Example 2: Vaud Resident Owns Multi-Canton Properties

**Scenario**:
- Deceased: Resident of Vaud
- Owns:
  - Primary residence in Lausanne, Vaud (CHF 900,000)
  - Investment apartment in Geneva (CHF 600,000)
- Heir: Nephew

**Taxation**:
- **Vaud property** (CHF 900,000): Taxed by Vaud at nephew rate (~CHF 198,000)
- **Geneva property** (CHF 600,000): Taxed by Geneva at nephew rate (~CHF 140,000)
- **Total tax**: CHF 338,000 on CHF 1,500,000 total real estate (22.5% effective)

**Planning implication**: Multi-canton real estate creates multiple tax jurisdictions - each canton applies its own rates to property within its borders.

### Property Valuation for Inheritance Tax

**Valuation date**: Date of death

**Valuation method**: **Fair market value** (valeur vénale / Verkehrswert)

**How determined**:
1. **Official tax value** (valeur fiscale): Vaud maintains property valuations for wealth/property tax purposes
2. **Market appraisal**: Professional appraisal if official value is outdated or contested
3. **Recent sale price**: If property sold shortly before/after death, sale price is strong evidence

**Typical approach**:
- Vaud tax authorities use **official tax value** as starting point
- Heirs can contest if market value is lower (must provide appraisal)
- Tax authorities can contest if market value is higher (rare unless obvious undervaluation)

#### Contesting Valuation

**When to contest**:
- Official tax value significantly exceeds realistic market price (property condition deteriorated, market downturn)
- Specialized property (farm, unique building) where official value doesn't reflect marketability

**Process**:
1. Obtain professional appraisal from certified Swiss appraiser (expert immobilier agréé)
2. Submit appraisal with inheritance tax declaration
3. Vaud tax authorities review and may accept, reject, or negotiate
4. If rejected: Can appeal through administrative process

**Cost**: Professional appraisal CHF 2,000-5,000+

**Worth it if**: Valuation reduction exceeds appraisal cost + tax savings
- Example: CHF 5,000 appraisal reduces value by CHF 100,000 → Tax savings CHF 18,000-35,000 (depending on heir type) → Net benefit CHF 13,000-30,000

### Mortgage and Debt Deductions

**Fully deductible from property value**:
- Outstanding mortgages
- Property loans
- Unpaid property taxes
- Deferred maintenance costs (if documented and necessary)

#### Example: Mortgaged Property Inheritance

**Scenario**:
- Vaud property market value: CHF 1,000,000
- Outstanding mortgage: -CHF 500,000
- **Net taxable value**: CHF 500,000

**Tax (sibling rate)**:
- Gross value CHF 1M: Would be ~CHF 140,000 tax
- Net value CHF 500K: Actual tax ~CHF 55,500
- **Mortgage deduction saves**: CHF 84,500 in tax

**Heir responsibility**: Heir inherits property **subject to mortgage**
- Heir can keep mortgage (assume payments)
- Heir can refinance/pay off mortgage
- Heir can sell property and pay off mortgage from proceeds

**Important**: Mortgage deduction is automatic if properly documented. Ensure mortgage is listed in estate inventory.

## Vaud Real Estate Gains Tax (Impôt sur les gains immobiliers)

### What is Real Estate Gains Tax?

**Separate from inheritance tax**: Tax on **appreciation** of property value, levied when property is sold or transferred.

**Trigger events**:
- Sale of property (most common)
- Gift of property during life
- **Inheritance is generally EXEMPT** (see below)

**Tax rate**: Progressive, based on:
- Amount of gain (higher gain = higher rate)
- Holding period (shorter hold = higher rate)

**Typical rates**: 0% to 60%+ depending on gain and holding period

### Inheritance Exception: Generally No Gains Tax on Death Transfer

**Critical rule**: Transfer of property **by inheritance at death** is **generally exempt** from Vaud real estate gains tax.

**Why**: Inheritance is not considered a "sale" (no consideration paid); heirs receive stepped-up basis at market value on date of death.

**Implication**: Direct inheritance at death avoids gains tax - heir receives property at current market value for future gains calculation.

#### Example: Gains Tax Exemption on Inheritance

**Scenario**:
- Father purchased Vaud property in 1990 for CHF 300,000
- Property value at father's death (2026): CHF 1,200,000
- Appreciation: CHF 900,000
- Heir: Daughter (direct descendant)

**At father's death**:
- **Inheritance tax**: CHF 0 (direct descendant exempt)
- **Real estate gains tax**: CHF 0 (inheritance exempt from gains tax)
- Daughter receives property with **stepped-up basis of CHF 1,200,000**

**If daughter later sells** (e.g., 2030) for CHF 1,400,000:
- Taxable gain: CHF 1,400,000 (sale price) - CHF 1,200,000 (inherited basis) = CHF 200,000
- Gains tax on CHF 200,000 (rate depends on holding period from 2026-2030)

**Key benefit**: Father's CHF 900,000 appreciation (1990-2026) is **never taxed** due to inheritance exemption.

### Lifetime Gift: Gains Tax DOES Apply

**Critical difference**: Gifting property **during life** triggers real estate gains tax.

**Why**: Gift is treated as "deemed sale" at market value for gains tax purposes.

**Tax calculation**:
- Deemed sale price = Market value at time of gift
- Taxable gain = Market value - Original purchase price
- Gains tax rate applies to full appreciation
- Donor pays gains tax (even though no cash received)

#### Example: Gains Tax on Lifetime Gift

**Scenario**:
- Father purchased Vaud property 1990 for CHF 300,000
- Current value 2026: CHF 1,200,000
- **Gifts** property to daughter during life (not inheritance)

**Taxation**:
- Appreciation: CHF 900,000
- **Real estate gains tax** (assume 40% rate for long-held property): CHF 360,000
- **Gift tax** (daughter is direct descendant): CHF 0
- **Donor cost**: CHF 360,000 gains tax (paid by father)

**After gift**:
- Daughter receives property with basis CHF 1,200,000 (stepped up)
- Father paid CHF 360,000 out-of-pocket for gains tax

**Compare to inheritance at death**:
- Inheritance tax: CHF 0
- Gains tax: CHF 0
- **Savings from waiting until death**: CHF 360,000

**Implication**: For direct descendants, **inheritance at death is far superior** to lifetime gift of Vaud real estate (avoids gains tax).

### When Lifetime Gift Makes Sense

**Scenario where gift might be beneficial**:

**Condition 1**: Heir is **not** direct descendant (e.g., sibling, nephew, unmarried partner)
- Inheritance tax would be high (18-50% in Vaud)
- Gift tax equally high (same rates)
- But: Future appreciation removed from estate

**Condition 2**: Property expected to **appreciate significantly**
- Gift now "freezes" estate value
- Future appreciation grows outside taxable estate
- Gains tax paid now, but no inheritance tax on future appreciation

**Condition 3**: Donor has liquidity to pay gains tax
- Gains tax requires cash payment
- Donor must have funds to pay (cannot defer or pay from property value)

#### Example: Lifetime Gift to Non-Descendant

**Scenario**:
- Vaud property purchased CHF 400,000, now worth CHF 1,000,000
- Owner wants to provide for unmarried partner
- Property expected to appreciate to CHF 1,500,000 over next 10 years

**Option A: Inherit at death (10 years from now)**:
- Property value at death: CHF 1,500,000
- Inheritance tax (unmarried partner, ~37% effective): CHF 555,000
- Real estate gains tax: CHF 0 (inheritance exempt)
- **Partner receives**: CHF 945,000 net

**Option B: Gift now**:
- Gift value: CHF 1,000,000
- Real estate gains tax on CHF 600K gain (~35% rate): CHF 210,000 (donor pays)
- Gift tax (unmarried partner, ~37% effective): CHF 370,000 (partner pays, or donor pays on partner's behalf)
- **Immediate cost**: CHF 580,000
- Over 10 years: Property appreciates to CHF 1,500,000
- **At donor's death**: Property already transferred, CHF 500,000 appreciation not in estate
- **Partner avoids**: CHF 185,000 future inheritance tax on appreciation

**Net comparison**:
- Option A (inheritance): CHF 555,000 total tax
- Option B (gift): CHF 580,000 immediate tax, but saves CHF 185,000 future tax if appreciation occurs
- **Decision depends on**: Likelihood/magnitude of appreciation, liquidity to pay taxes now, donor longevity

**For most cases**: **Registered partnership** (0% tax) is far superior to either option.

## Strategic Planning for Vaud Real Estate

### Strategy 1: Sell Property Before Death and Reinvest in Financial Assets

**Concept**: Convert real estate into movable assets to shift tax jurisdiction from Vaud to residence canton.

**When beneficial**: Owner lives in lower-tax canton than Vaud, property will go to non-direct heir.

#### Example Implementation

**Scenario**:
- Owner: Resident of Schwyz (0% inheritance tax on movable assets)
- Owns: Vaud investment property worth CHF 800,000
- Intended heir: Nephew

**Option A: Keep property, inherit at death**:
- Vaud inheritance tax (nephew, CHF 800K): ~CHF 176,000
- Gains tax: CHF 0 (inheritance exempt)
- **Nephew receives**: CHF 624,000 net

**Option B: Sell property before death, reinvest in stocks/bonds**:
- Sell Vaud property: CHF 800,000 proceeds
- **Pay real estate gains tax** on sale (assume CHF 100,000 gains tax)
- Net proceeds: CHF 700,000
- Reinvest in financial assets (stocks, bonds, mutual funds)
- At death: CHF 700,000 financial assets taxed at **Schwyz rate** (0%)
- **Nephew receives**: CHF 700,000 net

**Net comparison**:
- Option A: CHF 624,000 to nephew (Vaud property inheritance)
- Option B: CHF 700,000 to nephew (financial assets after paying CHF 100K gains tax on sale)
- **Benefit**: CHF 76,000 additional to nephew

**Considerations**:
- Donor must pay real estate gains tax from liquid funds (or from sale proceeds)
- Loss of specific property (may have sentimental value)
- Transaction costs (broker fees, notary fees on sale)
- Liquidity for nephew (stocks easier to liquidate than real estate)

**Best for**:
- Investment properties (no sentimental attachment)
- Owners with residence in lower-tax cantons
- Heirs who would prefer liquidity over specific property

### Strategy 2: Usufruct (Nutzniessung) Arrangement

**Concept**: Transfer bare ownership to heir during life, retain lifetime usufruct (right to use and income).

**Tax treatment**:
- Bare ownership transfer is taxed as gift (reduced value due to usufruct discount)
- At donor's death: Usufruct expires automatically, heir gets full ownership with no additional tax event

**Vaud usufruct discount**: Based on donor's age
- Younger donor = longer expected usufruct = higher discount (lower gift tax)
- Older donor = shorter expected usufruct = lower discount (higher gift tax)

**Typical discount**: 20-50% of property value, depending on donor age

#### Example: Usufruct Transfer

**Scenario**:
- Vaud property value: CHF 1,000,000
- Donor age: 65 (life expectancy ~20 years)
- Usufruct value: ~40% of property value = CHF 400,000
- Bare ownership value: ~60% of property value = CHF 600,000
- Heir: Sister (sibling)

**At time of transfer**:
- Gift tax calculated on **bare ownership** (CHF 600,000), not full value
- Gift tax (sibling, CHF 600K): ~CHF 73,500
- **Real estate gains tax**: Triggered on transfer (donor pays on full appreciation)

**Donor retains**:
- Right to live in property (if residential)
- Right to rental income (if investment property)
- Responsibility for maintenance

**At donor's death**:
- Usufruct expires
- Sister automatically receives full ownership
- **No additional inheritance tax** (already paid gift tax on bare ownership)
- No additional gains tax (property already transferred)

**Net benefit**:
- Gift tax on CHF 600K (vs. CHF 1M if no usufruct): Saves ~CHF 40,000
- Donor keeps use of property for life
- Sister avoids future inheritance tax

**Downside**:
- Real estate gains tax paid now (not deferred until donor's death)
- Complex legal arrangement (requires notary, registration)
- Irrevocable (donor cannot reclaim bare ownership)

**Best for**:
- Donors wanting to retain use of property but transfer ownership
- Direct descendants (where gains tax is primary concern, not inheritance tax)
- Long-term planning (benefits increase with longer donor survival)

### Strategy 3: Registered Partnership (Unmarried Couples)

**Most effective strategy for unmarried couples with Vaud real estate.**

**Tax impact**:

**Before registered partnership**:
- Vaud property CHF 1,200,000 to unmarried partner
- Inheritance tax (non-relative rate, ~37% effective): CHF 444,000
- **Partner receives**: CHF 756,000 net

**After registered partnership (PACS)**:
- Same property to **registered partner**
- Inheritance tax: **CHF 0** (spouse equivalent)
- **Partner receives**: CHF 1,200,000 net
- **Savings**: CHF 444,000

**Registration process**:
- Apply at Vaud Civil Registry Office (Office de l'état civil)
- Requirements: Both partners 18+, not married, not closely related, both consent
- Cost: Minimal administrative fees (~CHF 100-200)
- Timeline: Immediate (effective date of registration)

**Immediate tax benefit**: Tax status changes instantly upon registration.

**Best for**:
- All unmarried couples with significant Vaud real estate
- Immediate ROI (saves hundreds of thousands in tax for large estates)

### Strategy 4: Property Company Structure (AG/GmbH)

**Concept**: Transfer real estate into Swiss company, inherit company shares instead of property directly.

**Potential tax treatment**:
- Company shares may be taxed as **movable assets** (residence canton rate)
- NOT taxed as Vaud real estate (situs rule may not apply to shares)

**⚠️ WARNING**: This is a **complex, specialized structure** with significant anti-avoidance scrutiny. Vaud tax authorities may challenge and "pierce the veil" to tax as real estate.

#### When It Might Work

**Scenario**: Operational real estate company (e.g., property development, rental business with multiple properties)
- **Substance**: Company has genuine business operations, employees, active management
- **Not pure holding**: Company actively manages properties, not just passive ownership
- **Commercial rationale**: Business reason for corporate structure beyond tax

**Tax treatment**:
- Shares taxed as movable assets at residence canton rate (if structure respected)
- May qualify for business succession relief (80% reduction) in some cantons (not Vaud, but relevant if owner relocates)

**Unlikely to work**: Single property held in passive holding company solely for tax avoidance
- Vaud will likely challenge and tax as real estate transfer

**Professional consultation mandatory**: Requires specialized Swiss tax lawyer and corporate structuring expert.

### Strategy 5: Life Insurance for Tax Liquidity

**Problem**: Heir inherits Vaud property but lacks cash to pay inheritance tax.

**Solution**: Life insurance policy provides liquidity for tax payment.

#### Example Implementation

**Scenario**:
- Vaud family chalet worth CHF 1,500,000 (no mortgage)
- Heir: Nephew (wants to keep chalet in family)
- Estimated inheritance tax (nephew rate): ~CHF 525,000

**Without insurance**:
- Nephew inherits chalet
- Must pay CHF 525,000 tax within 6 months
- Nephew lacks personal funds → Must **sell chalet or take large loan**
- Forced sale under pressure = poor price, family heritage lost

**With insurance**:
- Owner purchases CHF 600,000 life insurance policy
- Beneficiary: Nephew
- Annual premium: ~CHF 10,000-20,000 (age-dependent)
- **At death**: Nephew receives CHF 600,000 insurance proceeds
- Uses CHF 525,000 to pay tax, retains chalet, keeps CHF 75,000 buffer

**Insurance taxation**: Life insurance proceeds to direct descendants/spouses generally **tax-free** in Vaud. For other beneficiaries (siblings, nephews), verify current treatment with insurance advisor.

**Best for**:
- Heirs who want to keep specific property (family home, vacation property)
- Estates with high real estate value but limited liquid assets
- Avoiding forced sales to pay taxes

## Vaud Real Estate Succession Timeline

### Standard Timeline for Property Inheritance

**Month 0**: Death of owner

**Months 1-2**: Estate inventory
- Identify all properties owned
- Obtain official Vaud tax values (valeur fiscale)
- Consider professional appraisal if contesting valuation
- Document mortgages and debts

**Month 3**: Inheritance tax declaration
- File with Administration cantonale des impôts, Vaud
- Include property valuations, mortgage documentation
- Submit appraisals if contesting official values

**Months 4-6**: Tax assessment
- Vaud tax authority reviews declaration
- May accept or challenge valuations
- Issues official tax assessment (taxation)

**Month 6-12**: Tax payment due
- Standard deadline: 6 months from assessment
- Request extension if needed (liquidity issues, property must be sold)
- Pay by deadline or interest accrues (~4-5% annually)

**Concurrent process**: Property title transfer
- Notary prepares ownership transfer deed (acte de propriété)
- Register new ownership at Land Registry (Registre foncier)
- Transfer cannot be completed until inheritance tax paid

### Expedited Timeline (Liquid Estate)

If heirs have cash to pay tax immediately:
- Months 1-3: Inventory, declaration, assessment
- Month 4: Pay tax immediately
- Month 5: Complete property title transfer
- **Total**: 5 months to full ownership

### Extended Timeline (Property Must Be Sold to Pay Tax)

If heir must sell property to pay tax:
- Months 1-6: Standard process (inventory, assessment)
- Month 6: Request payment extension (6-12 months)
- Months 7-18: Market property for sale, find buyer
- Month 18: Sell property, use proceeds to pay inheritance tax
- Month 19: Complete estate settlement

**Extension rationale**: Tax authorities generally grant extensions when property must be sold to pay tax (especially if no other liquid assets).

## Common Pitfalls: Vaud Real Estate

### Pitfall 1: Assuming Inheritance Avoids All Taxes

**The mistake**: Believing inherited real estate is "tax-free" in Switzerland.

**Why it's a problem**: Confusion between **real estate gains tax** (exempt on inheritance) and **inheritance tax** (applies unless direct descendant/spouse).

**Reality**:
- **Direct descendants/spouses**: Both taxes avoided (0% inheritance tax, gains tax exempt on inheritance)
- **Others (siblings, nephews, unmarried partners)**: Inheritance tax applies at Vaud rates (up to 50%)

**Example**:
- Nephew inherits Vaud property CHF 800,000
- Assumes "no tax" because gains tax doesn't apply to inheritance
- **Reality**: Vaud inheritance tax ~CHF 176,000 (22% effective rate for nephew)
- Nephew receives unexpected tax bill

**The fix**: Understand both tax types. **Gains tax** exempt on inheritance, but **inheritance tax** applies based on relationship to deceased.

### Pitfall 2: Gifting Property to Avoid Tax (Backfires)

**The mistake**: Gifting Vaud property to direct descendant during life to "avoid taxes."

**Why it's a problem**:
- **Inheritance at death**: CHF 0 inheritance tax (direct descendant), CHF 0 gains tax (inheritance exempt)
- **Lifetime gift**: CHF 0 gift tax (direct descendant), **but triggers real estate gains tax**

**Example**:
- Father bought Vaud chalet 1995 for CHF 400,000
- Now worth CHF 1,200,000 (appreciation CHF 800,000)
- **Gifts to daughter** during life (trying to "plan ahead")
- Triggers real estate gains tax on CHF 800K appreciation: ~CHF 280,000 (35% rate)
- **If waited until death**: CHF 0 inheritance tax, CHF 0 gains tax
- **Cost of premature gift**: CHF 280,000

**The fix**: For direct descendants, **keep property until death** (both taxes avoided). Lifetime gift triggers unnecessary gains tax.

### Pitfall 3: Not Valuing Mortgage Deduction

**The mistake**: Paying inheritance tax on gross property value without claiming mortgage deduction.

**Why it's a problem**: Mortgages are fully deductible but must be **documented and claimed**.

**Example**:
- Vaud property CHF 900,000
- Mortgage CHF 400,000
- Heir (sibling) files tax declaration listing property as CHF 900,000 (forgets to claim mortgage)
- Tax on CHF 900K: ~CHF 126,000
- **Should be**: Tax on CHF 500K net value: ~CHF 55,500
- **Overpayment**: CHF 70,500

**The fix**: Always include mortgage documentation with inheritance tax declaration. List net value (market value minus mortgage).

### Pitfall 4: Ignoring Co-Ownership Complications

**The mistake**: Assuming jointly-owned property transfers smoothly to heirs.

**Why it's a problem**: Swiss property co-ownership (copropriété / Miteigentum) has specific succession rules.

**Scenario**: Deceased owned 50% of Vaud property (co-owned with business partner)
- Market value of 50% share: CHF 400,000
- Heir: Nephew

**Complications**:
- Heir inherits 50% undivided interest (not exclusive ownership)
- Must co-own with deceased's former partner (may be problematic relationship)
- **Pre-emption rights**: Co-owner may have first right to purchase inherited share
- If co-owner exercises pre-emption: Heir must sell to co-owner (cannot keep)
- Valuation disputes (co-owner may contest market value to reduce purchase price)

**The fix**:
- Review co-ownership agreement (convention de copropriété) for pre-emption clauses
- Negotiate with co-owner before inheritance
- Consider buyout arrangements in advance
- Include co-ownership considerations in estate planning (may want to sell share before death to avoid complications)

### Pitfall 5: Overlooking Vaud Real Estate Gains Tax on Donor Gift

**The mistake**: Donor gifts property without budgeting for gains tax payment.

**Why it's a problem**: Gains tax is due **immediately** upon gift transfer, even though donor receives no cash.

**Example**:
- Donor gifts Vaud property (purchased CHF 300K, now worth CHF 900K)
- Gain: CHF 600,000
- Gains tax (~35% rate): CHF 210,000
- **Due immediately** (within months of gift)
- Donor has no liquidity to pay → Must take loan or sell other assets

**The fix**: Before gifting real estate, ensure donor has **liquid funds to pay gains tax**. If not, lifetime gift is impractical regardless of inheritance tax savings.

## Limitations

### What This Framework Cannot Do

**This is an educational tool, not legal/tax advice**:
- Vaud real estate and tax law is complex and changes regularly
- Individual property situations have unique considerations
- Professional Vaud real estate and tax advice is **mandatory** for implementation

### When Professional Consultation Is Mandatory

**You MUST consult professionals for**:
- Vaud real estate appraiser: Property valuations for tax purposes (if contesting official value)
- Vaud tax specialist: Inheritance tax and gains tax optimization strategies
- Notary: Property transfers, usufruct arrangements, ownership documentation
- Swiss tax lawyer: Corporate structures, international property holdings
- Estate planning lawyer: Complex family situations, co-ownership issues

### Verify Current Vaud Law

**Before implementing any strategy**:
- Confirm current Vaud inheritance tax rates and real estate gains tax rates
- Verify co-ownership agreement terms (if applicable)
- Check mortgage deduction documentation requirements
- Consult Vaud-specialist professionals for properties >CHF 500,000
