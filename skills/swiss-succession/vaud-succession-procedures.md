# Canton de Vaud Succession Procedures & Timeline

Use this framework to navigate the administrative process, timeline, and requirements for settling an estate in Canton de Vaud from death through final distribution.

## Core Concept

The Vaud Succession Procedures Framework provides a step-by-step roadmap for estate administration, covering death notification, estate inventory, tax assessment, will execution, and asset distribution. Understanding Vaud-specific procedures, deadlines, and authorities ensures compliant, efficient estate settlement and minimizes delays, disputes, and penalties.

### The Succession Process Flow

```
[Death] → [Notification] → [Estate Inventory] → [Tax Assessment]
   ↓
[Will Execution/Intestacy] → [Asset Distribution] → [Closure]

Timeline: 6-18 months typical
Complex estates: 18-36+ months
```

## When to Apply

- A Vaud resident has died (estate subject to Vaud procedures)
- You are executor/heir of a Vaud estate
- You are planning ahead to understand post-death administrative requirements
- You need to understand Vaud-specific deadlines and authorities
- You are coordinating with Swiss professionals on estate settlement
- You are comparing cantonal procedures (Vaud vs. other cantons)

## Phase 1: Immediate Post-Death (Days 1-14)

### Step 1: Death Notification (Within 2 Days)

**Who reports**: Family member, medical professional, funeral home

**Report to**:
- **Commune civil registry office** (Office de l'état civil) where death occurred
- If death at home: Commune of residence
- If death in hospital: Commune where hospital located

**Required documents**:
- Medical death certificate (certificat de décès médical) from doctor
- Deceased's identity documents (passport, ID card)
- Family relationship documentation (for informant)

**Timeline**: Report within **2 business days** of death

**Outcome**: Official death certificate (acte de décès) issued by Civil Registry

**Copies needed**: Request 5-10 certified copies (needed for banks, insurance, real estate, tax authorities)

**Cost**: CHF 20-40 per certified copy

### Step 2: Secure Assets and Notify Key Parties (Days 1-7)

**Immediate actions**:

**Secure physical property**:
- Change locks if necessary (prevent unauthorized access)
- Secure valuables (jewelry, art, cash, important documents)
- Inventory immediately accessible assets
- Prevent asset removal by non-heirs

**Notify financial institutions** (within 1 week):
- Banks (all accounts) - freeze accounts to prevent unauthorized transactions
- Insurance companies (life insurance, property insurance)
- Investment/brokerage accounts
- Pension funds (2nd pillar, 3rd pillar)
- Employer (final salary, benefits)

**Notify authorities**:
- Vaud tax authorities (Administration cantonale des impôts)
- Swiss Federal Tax Administration (if relevant)
- Social security (AVS/IV) - stop pension payments
- Health insurance (cancel or transfer policy)

**Notify service providers**:
- Utilities (electricity, gas, water) - transfer or cancel
- Telecom (phone, internet)
- Subscriptions and memberships

### Step 3: Locate Will and Estate Planning Documents (Days 1-14)

**Where to look**:

1. **Home safe or filing system**: Check deceased's personal files
2. **Lawyer/notary office**: Contact deceased's known legal advisors
3. **Bank safe deposit box**: Request access (may require court order)
4. **Cantonal will registry** (if will was registered):
   - Contact Vaud Justice de Paix
   - Provide death certificate and relationship proof
   - Registry will confirm if will was registered and location

**Documents to locate**:
- [ ] Last will and testament (testament)
- [ ] Inheritance contract (erbvertrag / pacte successoral)
- [ ] Marital agreement (contrat de mariage)
- [ ] Power of attorney (procuration)
- [ ] Adult protection mandate (mandat de protection)
- [ ] Life insurance policies with beneficiary designations
- [ ] Business succession agreements
- [ ] Trust or foundation documents (if international estate)

**If no will found**: Proceed with **intestate succession** (statutory heirs inherit per Swiss Civil Code)

### Step 4: Arrange Funeral (Days 1-7)

**Vaud requirements**:
- Burial or cremation must occur within **5 days** of death (unless special authorization for delay)
- Funeral home handles logistics
- Commune cemetery or private cemetery (permissions required)

**Costs**: CHF 5,000-15,000+ depending on choices (burial plot, ceremony, etc.)

**Estate payment**: Funeral costs are **deductible from estate** for tax purposes (reduce taxable estate value)

## Phase 2: Estate Inventory and Valuation (Months 1-3)

### Step 5: Appoint Executor or Estate Administrator (Month 1)

**Executor designation**:
- If will names executor: That person serves (can decline)
- If no executor named: Heirs collectively act as administrators
- If dispute/complexity: Court-appointed administrator (administrateur judiciaire)

**Executor responsibilities**:
- Compile estate inventory
- Protect assets
- Pay debts and taxes
- Distribute assets to heirs
- File tax declarations
- Communicate with heirs

**Executor compensation**: Typically 1-3% of estate value (negotiable, subject to heir approval)

### Step 6: Compile Comprehensive Estate Inventory (Months 1-3)

**All assets must be listed and valued**:

#### Real Estate
- [ ] Vaud properties: Obtain official tax value (valeur fiscale) from Commune
- [ ] Properties in other cantons: Contact relevant cantonal authorities
- [ ] Foreign properties: Obtain local valuations
- **Valuation date**: Date of death
- **Market appraisals**: Consider professional appraisal if official value contested

#### Financial Accounts
- [ ] Bank accounts (Swiss and foreign): Request date-of-death balances
- [ ] Investment accounts: Market value of securities on death date
- [ ] Retirement accounts:
  - 2nd pillar (pension fund): Contact fund for vested benefits
  - 3rd pillar: Bank or insurance company value
- [ ] Life insurance: Contact insurers for death benefit amounts

#### Business Interests
- [ ] Company shares (AG, GmbH): Professional valuation required
- [ ] Sole proprietorship: Business appraisal
- [ ] Partnership interests: Refer to partnership agreement for valuation method

#### Personal Property
- [ ] Vehicles: Market value (used car valuations)
- [ ] Art and collectibles: Professional appraisal if significant value
- [ ] Jewelry and precious items: Appraisal
- [ ] Household contents: Estimated value (detailed inventory if valuable)

#### Intellectual Property and Digital Assets
- [ ] Patents, copyrights, trademarks: Valuation
- [ ] Cryptocurrency: Market value on death date (exchange statements)
- [ ] Digital accounts: Access, value any monetizable assets

#### Liabilities (Deductions)
- [ ] Mortgages: Outstanding balances from lenders
- [ ] Personal loans: Documentation from creditors
- [ ] Credit card debt: Final statements
- [ ] Unpaid taxes: Prior year liabilities
- [ ] Medical/funeral expenses: Receipts

**Inventory documentation**:
- Spreadsheet with all assets and values
- Supporting documents (bank statements, appraisals, property records)
- Total gross estate value
- Total liabilities
- **Net estate value** = Gross - Liabilities

### Step 7: Obtain Professional Valuations (Months 1-3)

**When required**:
- Significant real estate (CHF 500K+): Independent appraisal
- Family business: Certified business valuator
- Art/collectibles >CHF 50K: Specialized appraiser
- Foreign assets: Local experts

**Costs**: CHF 2,000-10,000+ depending on complexity

**Purpose**: Support tax declarations, ensure fair distribution among heirs, defend against tax authority challenges

## Phase 3: Tax Assessment and Payment (Months 3-12)

### Step 8: File Inheritance Tax Declaration (Month 3-4)

**Submit to**: Administration cantonale des impôts, Canton de Vaud

**Required contents**:
- Completed tax declaration form (formulaire de déclaration de succession)
- Complete estate inventory with valuations
- Death certificate
- Will or inheritance certificate (certificat d'héritier)
- Proof of relationships (birth certificates, marriage certificate)
- Mortgage and debt documentation
- Professional appraisals (if applicable)

**Deadline**: Typically **3 months** from death (extensions available upon request)

**Online/paper**: Check current Vaud process (some cantons moving to online systems)

### Step 9: Tax Assessment Issued (Months 4-6)

**Process**:
- Tax authorities review declaration
- May request additional documentation or clarifications
- May challenge valuations (require higher values)
- Calculate tax based on Vaud rate tables and beneficiary relationships
- Issue official assessment (taxation)

**Timeline**: 2-4 months from declaration filing

**Assessment contents**:
- Taxable estate value (after deductions)
- Tax calculated per heir (based on their share and relationship)
- Payment deadline (typically 6 months from assessment)
- Payment instructions

### Step 10: Challenge or Accept Assessment (Month 6)

**If disagreement**:
- **Valuation dispute**: Provide additional appraisals, comparable sales data
- **Relationship classification error**: Provide correct documentation
- **Deduction omitted**: Submit proof of debt or expense

**Appeal process**:
- Written objection (réclamation) to Vaud tax authorities
- **Deadline**: 30 days from assessment receipt
- Tax authorities review and issue revised assessment or reject claim
- Further appeal: Administrative court (Tribunal cantonal) if necessary

**If accepted**:
- Proceed to payment (Step 11)

### Step 11: Pay Inheritance Tax (Months 6-12)

**Payment deadline**: Typically **6 months from assessment date**

**Payment methods**:
- Bank transfer to Vaud tax authority account
- Payment plan (if requested and approved - see below)

**Extension requests**:
- Submit written request if heirs lack liquidity
- **Justification**: Estate is primarily illiquid (real estate, business); sale in progress to generate funds
- **Typical extension**: Additional 6-12 months
- **Interest**: Accrues at ~4-5% annually from original deadline

**Installment plans**:
- Request payment over 2-5 years in installments
- Requires justification (illiquid estate) and security (mortgage on property)
- Interest applies

**Consequences of non-payment**:
- Interest accrues immediately after deadline
- Enforcement action (seizure of estate assets, liens on property)
- Estate cannot be distributed to heirs until tax paid

## Phase 4: Will Execution and Heir Determination (Months 2-6)

### Step 12: Will Validation (If Will Exists) (Months 2-4)

**Holographic will** (handwritten):
- Submit to Justice de Paix (Vaud district court)
- Court authenticates (verifies handwriting, date, signature)
- Court opens will publicly (lecture publique)
- **Timeline**: 1-3 months

**Public deed will** (notarized):
- Notary confirms validity
- No court authentication needed (already officially authenticated)
- **Timeline**: Immediate

**Foreign will** (if applicable):
- Requires authentication and potential translation
- Legal consultation needed
- **Timeline**: 3-6 months

**Outcome**: Court issues **certificate of inheritance** (certificat d'héritier) listing valid heirs and their shares

### Step 13: Intestate Succession (If No Will) (Months 2-4)

**If no will found**:
- Statutory heirs inherit per Swiss Civil Code hierarchy
- Justice de Paix issues certificate of inheritance based on family relationships
- Proof of relationships required (birth certificates, family tree, marriage certificate)

**Statutory shares** (see [Succession Fundamentals](succession-fundamentals.md) for detailed hierarchy):
- Spouse + children: 50/50 split
- Spouse + parents: 75% spouse, 25% parents
- Children only: 100% divided equally

**Timeline**: 2-4 months to obtain certificate

### Step 14: Resolve Disputes (If Any) (Months 3-12+)

**Common disputes**:
- Will validity challenge (forgery, undue influence, lack of capacity)
- Forced heirship violation (compulsory portions not respected)
- Asset valuation disagreements
- Executor conduct complaints
- Heir identification (unknown children, contested paternity)

**Resolution process**:
- **Mediation**: Family mediator facilitates agreement (2-6 months)
- **Court proceedings**: Canton court litigation (6-24+ months)

**Impact on timeline**: Disputes delay entire estate settlement until resolved

## Phase 5: Asset Distribution (Months 6-18)

### Step 15: Pay Debts and Final Expenses (Months 6-8)

**Priority order** (creditors paid before heirs):
1. Funeral costs
2. Estate administration costs (executor fees, appraisal costs, legal fees)
3. Secured debts (mortgages, property liens)
4. Taxes (income tax, inheritance tax)
5. Unsecured debts (credit cards, personal loans)

**Documentation**: Retain receipts for all payments (required for final accounting to heirs)

### Step 16: Distribute Specific Bequests (Months 7-9)

**If will specifies** particular items to particular heirs:
- "My Rolex watch to my son Pierre"
- "My piano to my niece Marie"

**Process**:
- Executor delivers items to designated heirs
- Obtain signed receipt from heir (proof of distribution)
- Value of specific bequests **counts toward heir's total share**

### Step 17: Liquidate Assets (If Needed) (Months 6-18)

**When liquidation required**:
- Heirs want cash instead of specific assets (real estate, business)
- Assets must be sold to pay taxes or debts
- Multiple heirs cannot agree on asset division

**Process**:
- Real estate: List for sale, find buyer, complete transaction (6-12 months)
- Business: Sell to third party or buyout by co-owners (6-18 months)
- Securities: Sell on market (immediate, but consider timing for tax optimization)

**Considerations**:
- Market timing (avoid forced sales in down markets if possible)
- Real estate gains tax (sale triggers gains tax on appreciation)
- Professional broker/advisor fees (3-5% of sale price)

### Step 18: Divide Remaining Estate Among Heirs (Month 12-18)

**Distribution methods**:

**Method 1: In-kind division** (each heir receives specific assets)
- **Example**: Child A receives Lausanne apartment (CHF 500K), Child B receives stock portfolio (CHF 500K)
- **Advantage**: No liquidation needed, lower transaction costs
- **Challenge**: Equalizing values, heirs may want same asset

**Method 2: Cash division** (all assets sold, proceeds divided)
- Sell all assets
- Divide cash per will or statutory shares
- **Advantage**: Perfectly equal, no valuation disputes
- **Disadvantage**: High transaction costs, tax on sales, heirs lose specific assets

**Method 3: Mixed** (some in-kind, some cash)
- Retain family home for specific heir, sell investment properties
- Divide remaining cash to equalize
- **Most common approach**

**Distribution execution**:
- Bank transfers to heirs (cash portions)
- Title transfers (real estate deeds, vehicle titles)
- Account transfers (securities accounts re-registered to heirs)

**Documentation**:
- Signed receipt from each heir acknowledging distribution
- Final accounting showing all assets, debts, expenses, distributions
- Executor's discharge (décharge) from heirs

### Step 19: Transfer Real Estate Titles (Months 12-18)

**Vaud Land Registry** (Registre foncier) process:

**Required documents**:
- Certificate of inheritance (certificat d'héritier)
- Proof of inheritance tax payment
- Notarized transfer deed (if sale or complex distribution)
- Identity documents of new owners

**Notary role**:
- Prepares ownership transfer deed
- Ensures all legal requirements met
- Registers new ownership at Land Registry

**Timeline**: 2-4 months from tax payment to registered ownership

**Costs**:
- Notary fees: 1-2% of property value
- Land registry fees: 0.5-1% of property value

**Outcome**: New owners officially registered, receive updated property certificate (extrait du registre foncier)

## Phase 6: Estate Closure (Month 18+)

### Step 20: Final Accounting and Executor Discharge (Month 18)

**Executor prepares**:
- Complete accounting: All assets, liabilities, income, expenses, distributions
- Supporting documentation: Receipts, bank statements, transfer confirmations
- Summary: Beginning estate value → Ending distributions

**Submit to heirs**: For review and approval

**Heir approval**:
- Heirs review accounting
- Sign discharge (décharge) releasing executor from liability
- Confirm satisfaction with estate administration

**If heirs dispute**: Mediation or court review of executor conduct

**Executor release**: Once discharge signed, executor is released from duties and liability

### Step 21: Final Tax Returns (Month 12-18)

**Income tax**:
- File final income tax return for deceased (year of death)
- Report income from January 1 to date of death
- **Deadline**: Same as normal tax filing (March-April of following year)
- Heirs are responsible for payment of deceased's final tax liability

**Estate income tax** (if estate generates income during administration):
- Interest on bank accounts, dividends, rental income during estate settlement
- File separate estate tax return
- Pay from estate assets before distribution

### Step 22: Archive Estate Documents (Month 18+)

**Retention requirements**: Keep estate documents for **10 years** (Swiss statute of limitations for tax and legal disputes)

**Documents to retain**:
- Original will and certificate of inheritance
- Complete estate inventory and valuations
- All tax declarations and assessments
- Payment receipts (taxes, debts, distributions)
- Final accounting and heir discharge
- Real estate transfer documents
- Correspondence with authorities

**Storage**: Secure location (executor's files, legal advisor, bank safe deposit)

## Timeline Summary

### Simple Estate (Direct Descendants, Minimal Assets, No Disputes)

**6-12 months total**

| Month | Phase | Key Actions |
|-------|-------|-------------|
| 0 | Death | Notification, secure assets, locate will |
| 1-2 | Inventory | Compile assets, obtain valuations |
| 3 | Tax Filing | Submit inheritance tax declaration |
| 4-6 | Assessment | Tax authorities assess and issue bill |
| 6 | Payment | Pay inheritance tax |
| 6-8 | Distribution | Distribute assets to heirs |
| 9-12 | Closure | Final accounting, executor discharge |

### Average Estate (Multiple Heirs, Real Estate, Some Complexity)

**12-18 months total**

| Month | Phase | Key Actions |
|-------|-------|-------------|
| 0 | Death | Notification, secure assets |
| 1-3 | Inventory | Compile assets, professional appraisals |
| 3-4 | Tax Filing | Submit declaration |
| 5-8 | Assessment | Review, possible challenge, revised assessment |
| 9 | Payment | Pay tax (or request extension) |
| 9-15 | Distribution | Sell some assets, divide estate |
| 15-18 | Closure | Real estate title transfers, final accounting |

### Complex Estate (Business, International Assets, Disputes, High Value)

**24-36+ months**

| Month | Phase | Key Actions |
|-------|-------|-------------|
| 0-1 | Death | Notification, secure complex assets, engage professionals |
| 1-6 | Inventory | Complex valuations (business, foreign assets, IP) |
| 6-8 | Tax Filing | Detailed declarations, multiple jurisdictions |
| 8-12 | Assessment | Negotiations with tax authorities, valuation disputes |
| 12-18 | Disputes | Will challenges, forced heirship litigation, heir disagreements |
| 18-24 | Resolution | Mediated settlement or court judgment |
| 24-30 | Distribution | Business succession, complex asset transfers |
| 30-36 | Closure | Final accounting, releases |

## Authorities and Contacts

### Vaud Cantonal Administration

**Vaud Tax Authority** (Administration cantonale des impôts - ACI)
- **Role**: Inheritance tax assessment and collection
- **Contact**: [Vaud Tax Website](https://www.vd.ch/themes/etat-droit-finances/impots/)
- **Office**: Various regional offices across Vaud
- **Phone**: 021 316 00 00 (main switchboard)

**Justice de Paix** (District Courts)
- **Role**: Will validation, inheritance certificates, executor appointment
- **Locations**: Each district (arrondissement) has a Justice de Paix
- **Contact**: [Vaud Justice Website](https://www.vd.ch/themes/etat-droit-finances/justice/)

**Land Registry** (Registre foncier)
- **Role**: Real estate title transfers, ownership registration
- **Locations**: Each district has land registry office
- **Contact**: [Vaud Land Registry](https://www.vd.ch/themes/territoire/registre-foncier/)

**Civil Registry** (Office de l'état civil)
- **Role**: Death certificates, vital records
- **Locations**: Each commune
- **Contact**: Local commune office

### Professional Advisors

**Notary** (Notaire)
- **Role**: Real estate transfers, will authentication, estate administration
- **Find**: Swiss Notary Association - Vaud section
- **Typical fees**: 1-3% of estate value for full estate administration

**Inheritance Lawyer** (Avocat spécialisé en droit successoral)
- **Role**: Complex estates, disputes, international elements
- **Find**: Vaud Bar Association (Ordre des avocats vaudois)
- **Typical fees**: CHF 250-500/hour

**Tax Advisor** (Conseiller fiscal)
- **Role**: Tax optimization, declarations, assessments
- **Find**: Swiss Tax Experts Association (AOTAX)
- **Typical fees**: CHF 200-400/hour

**Estate Appraiser** (Expert immobilier / Business valuator)
- **Role**: Property and business valuations
- **Find**: Swiss Appraisers Association
- **Typical fees**: CHF 2,000-10,000 per valuation

## Common Procedural Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Missing Tax Filing Deadline

**The mistake**: Failing to file inheritance tax declaration within 3 months of death.

**Consequences**:
- Automatic penalty (typically 10-20% of tax owed)
- Interest accrues from deadline date
- Delay in estate settlement (cannot distribute until tax resolved)

**The fix**: Request extension **before** deadline if more time needed. Extensions routinely granted with justification (complex estate, awaiting appraisals).

### Pitfall 2: Distributing Assets Before Paying Taxes

**The mistake**: Heirs take possession of assets before inheritance tax is paid.

**Consequences**:
- Heirs become **personally liable** for unpaid taxes
- Tax authorities can seize distributed assets from heirs
- If assets already sold/spent by heirs, heirs must pay from personal funds

**The fix**: **Never distribute assets before tax payment** (except with explicit tax authority approval). Executor must retain sufficient assets to cover tax.

### Pitfall 3: Not Securing Estate Assets Immediately

**The mistake**: Delaying securing property, allowing unauthorized access or removal.

**Consequences**:
- Assets disappear (cash, jewelry, valuables taken by non-heirs)
- Heirs dispute what was in estate ("missing" items)
- Reduced estate value affects distributions

**The fix**: **Immediate action** (day of death): Change locks, secure valuables, notify banks to freeze accounts, inventory accessible assets.

### Pitfall 4: Ignoring Creditor Claims

**The mistake**: Distributing estate to heirs without paying deceased's debts.

**Consequences**:
- Creditors can sue heirs to recover debts
- Heirs may be personally liable if they accepted distribution before debts paid
- Legal disputes and potential clawback of distributions

**The fix**: **Pay all debts before distribution**. Advertise estate (publication in official gazette) to invite creditor claims. Wait statutory period (typically 3 months) before distributing.

### Pitfall 5: Poor Communication Among Heirs

**The mistake**: Executor fails to keep heirs informed, heirs feel excluded.

**Consequences**:
- Heirs challenge executor conduct
- Distrust and family conflict
- Litigation delays estate settlement by years

**The fix**: **Proactive communication**:
- Monthly updates to heirs (status, timeline, issues)
- Transparent accounting (share financial statements)
- Consult heirs on major decisions (asset sales, valuation disputes)
- Document all communications (written updates, meeting minutes)

## Limitations

### What This Framework Cannot Do

**This is an educational tool, not legal advice**:
- Vaud procedures may vary by district or change over time
- Complex estates require professional legal counsel
- International elements add procedural complexity beyond this framework

### When Professional Consultation Is Mandatory

**You MUST consult professionals for**:
- Executor/administrator services (unless very simple estate)
- Will challenges or forced heirship disputes
- Business succession execution
- Real estate transfers (notary required)
- International assets or heirs
- Tax optimization and declaration preparation
- Estates >CHF 500K (professional ROI justified)

### Verify Current Vaud Procedures

**Before proceeding**:
- Confirm current deadlines with Vaud authorities
- Check latest tax declaration forms and requirements
- Verify Justice de Paix procedures for your district
- Consult Vaud-specialist professionals for guidance
