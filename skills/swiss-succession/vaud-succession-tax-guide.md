# Canton de Vaud Succession Tax Guide

Use this framework to understand and optimize inheritance and gift taxation in Canton de Vaud, one of Switzerland's highest-tax jurisdictions for non-direct heirs.

## Core Concept

The Vaud Succession Tax Framework provides jurisdiction-specific guidance for navigating Vaud's progressive inheritance tax system. Vaud applies **no tax to direct descendants and spouses** but has some of **Switzerland's highest rates** (up to 50%) for siblings, unmarried partners, and non-relatives. Understanding Vaud's specific brackets, exemptions, and planning strategies is critical for effective tax minimization.

### Key Characteristics of Vaud Succession Tax

1. **Progressive rates**: Tax increases with inheritance amount
2. **Relationship-based**: Tax rate depends on heir's relationship to deceased
3. **Direct line exempt**: Spouses, registered partners, and direct descendants pay **0% tax**
4. **High rates for others**: Siblings (up to 25%), nieces/nephews (up to 35%), non-relatives (up to 50%)
5. **Cumulative gift rules**: Gifts within 5 years are aggregated with inheritance

## Vaud Inheritance Tax Rates (2026)

### Tax Exemptions (0% Tax Rate)

**Completely exempt from Vaud inheritance tax:**
- Spouses (married couples)
- Registered partners (same-sex or opposite-sex)
- Direct descendants (children, grandchildren, great-grandchildren)
- Direct ascendants (parents, grandparents - for amounts up to CHF 50,000)

**Note**: This makes Vaud attractive for direct family succession but very expensive for others.

## Vaud Gift Tax Rules

### Gift Tax vs. Inheritance Tax

Vaud applies **identical rates** to lifetime gifts (donations) as to inheritances. However, timing and cumulation rules differ.

### Cumulation Period: 5 Years

**Critical rule**: Gifts made within **5 years before death** are added back to the estate for inheritance tax calculation.

**How it works:**
1. Lifetime gift is taxed at time of gift (using gift tax rates - same as inheritance rates)
2. If donor dies within 5 years, gift is **added to estate** for calculating total tax
3. Gift tax already paid is credited against inheritance tax
4. This prevents "deathbed gifting" to avoid taxes

#### Example: Gift Followed by Death Within 5 Years

**Year 1**: Father gifts CHF 200,000 to sister
- Gift tax: CHF 21,000 (calculated using sibling rates)
- Tax paid immediately

**Year 3**: Father dies, leaving CHF 800,000 estate to same sister
- **Cumulative taxable amount**: CHF 200,000 (gift) + CHF 800,000 (inheritance) = CHF 1,000,000
- Total tax on CHF 1M: CHF 140,000
- Already paid (gift tax): -CHF 21,000
- **Additional tax due at death**: CHF 119,000

**If father had lived 6+ years**: Gift and inheritance taxed separately (no cumulation).

### Strategic Gifting in Vaud

#### Strategy 1: Wait 5+ Years Between Major Gifts

**Avoid cumulation** by spacing gifts:
- Gift 1 (Year 1): CHF 300,000 → Tax ~CHF 36,000
- Wait 6 years
- Gift 2 (Year 7): CHF 300,000 → Tax ~CHF 36,000 (not cumulated)
- Remaining CHF 400,000 at death → Tax ~CHF 48,000
- **Total tax**: ~CHF 120,000

**vs. All at death**:
- CHF 1,000,000 inheritance → Tax ~CHF 140,000
- **Savings**: CHF 20,000

**Additional benefit**: Assets gifted early grow outside taxable estate.

#### Strategy 2: Small Annual Gifts (No Specific Exemption)

**Important**: Vaud has **no annual gift tax exemption** (unlike some countries). All gifts are taxable, regardless of size.

However, very small gifts (below CHF 1,000-5,000) are often **administratively ignored** if not reported. For formal planning, assume all gifts are taxable.

#### Strategy 3: Register Partnership for 0% Tax

**Most effective strategy for unmarried couples**:

**Before registration**: CHF 500,000 inheritance to unmarried partner
- Tax: ~CHF 150,000 (30% effective rate)
- Net: CHF 350,000

**After registered partnership (PACS)**:
- Tax: **CHF 0** (spouse equivalent)
- Net: **CHF 500,000**
- **Savings: CHF 150,000**

**Registration requirements**:
- Both partners 18+ years old
- Not married or in another registered partnership
- Not closely related (siblings, parent-child)
- Register at Civil Registry Office (Office de l'état civil)
- Immediate tax benefits

## Vaud Real Estate Taxation (Situs Rule)

### Real Estate Located in Vaud

**Key principle**: Real estate in Vaud is **always taxed by Vaud**, regardless of where the deceased lived.

#### Scenario: Non-Vaud Resident Owns Vaud Property

**Example**:
- Deceased: Resident of Schwyz (0% inheritance tax canton)
- Owns: Chalet in Vaud (value CHF 600,000)
- Heir: Sibling

**Taxation**:
- Schwyz movable assets: **0% tax** (Schwyz has no inheritance tax)
- **Vaud chalet: Taxed by Vaud at sibling rate** → CHF 73,500 tax

**Planning implication**: Living in tax-free canton does NOT protect Vaud real estate from Vaud tax.

### Real Estate Tax Optimization Strategies

#### Strategy 1: Sell Before Death and Reinvest

**Problem**: Vaud chalet worth CHF 800,000, heir (sibling) faces CHF 96,000 tax

**Solution**:
- Sell Vaud chalet while alive
- Reinvest CHF 800,000 in financial assets (stocks, bonds)
- Financial assets taxed at **residence canton** (e.g., Schwyz = 0%)
- **Savings**: CHF 96,000 (Vaud property tax avoided)

**Considerations**:
- Capital gains tax on sale (but Switzerland has no general capital gains tax for individuals on movable property)
- Real estate gains tax (Vaud has real estate gains tax on property sale - factor this in)
- Lifestyle (may want to keep specific property)

#### Strategy 2: Lifetime Gift of Real Estate

**Option**: Gift Vaud property to heir during life

**Taxation**:
- Gift tax applies (same rates as inheritance tax)
- **Advantage**: Removes future appreciation from estate
- **Advantage**: Heir can start using property immediately

**Example**:
- Gift Vaud property worth CHF 600,000 to sibling today
- Gift tax: CHF 73,500 (paid now)
- Property appreciates to CHF 900,000 over 10 years
- At death: That CHF 300,000 appreciation **not taxed** (already transferred)
- **Savings**: ~CHF 45,000 (tax on appreciation avoided)

**Downside**: Irrevocable transfer, donor loses control

#### Strategy 3: Register Heir as Registered Partner

**For unmarried couples inheriting Vaud real estate**:

**Before**: CHF 1,000,000 Vaud villa to unmarried partner
- Tax: ~CHF 370,000 (37% effective rate)

**After registered partnership**:
- Tax: **CHF 0**
- **Savings: CHF 370,000**

**Most effective strategy for couples** with significant Vaud real estate.

## Vaud-Specific Deductions and Reliefs

### Funeral and Succession Costs

**Deductible from taxable estate**:
- Funeral expenses (reasonable amounts)
- Succession administration costs
- Notary fees
- Estate executor fees
- Publication costs (death notice)

**Typical deduction**: CHF 5,000 - 15,000 depending on complexity

**Impact**: Reduces taxable base slightly (e.g., CHF 1,000,000 estate - CHF 10,000 costs = CHF 990,000 taxable)

### Debts and Liabilities

**Fully deductible**:
- Mortgages on inherited property
- Business debts
- Personal loans
- Medical debts
- Taxes owed by deceased

**Example**:
- Vaud property value: CHF 800,000
- Mortgage: -CHF 300,000
- **Taxable value**: CHF 500,000 (not CHF 800,000)
- Tax (sibling): CHF 55,500 (vs. CHF 96,000 without mortgage deduction)

### No Business Relief in Vaud

**Important limitation**: Unlike Zurich, Bern, and other cantons that offer **80% valuation relief** for qualifying business inheritances, **Vaud offers no special business succession relief**.

**Impact**:
- Family business worth CHF 1,000,000 inherited by sibling
- **Vaud**: Full taxation at CHF 140,000
- **Zurich** (with 80% relief): Tax base CHF 200,000 → Tax ~CHF 24,000
- **Difference**: CHF 116,000 more expensive in Vaud

**Planning implication**: Business owners in Vaud should strongly consider:
- Lifetime gifting to reduce taxable estate
- Corporate restructuring
- Relocation to canton with business relief (if business-driven lifestyle permits)

## Tax Payment and Administration

### Payment Deadline

**Standard deadline**: **6 months from death** (or from receipt of tax assessment)

**Extensions**: Available upon request if:
- Estate settlement is complex (international assets, disputes)
- Heirs lack immediate liquidity
- Real estate must be sold to pay tax

**Typical extension**: Additional 6-12 months

### Interest on Late Payment

**Interest rate**: ~4-5% per annum (varies by cantonal policy)

**Applied**: From deadline date until payment received

**Implication**: Delaying payment is expensive; better to negotiate payment plan than simply delay.

### Payment in Installments

**Available**: Vaud tax authorities generally allow installment payments if:
- Inheritance is primarily illiquid (real estate, business)
- Heir demonstrates inability to pay lump sum
- Request made in writing with supporting financial documentation

**Typical plan**: 2-5 years, with interest

**Security**: May require mortgage on inherited real estate as security for installment payments

### Tax Assessment Process

**Timeline**:

1. **Death** (Day 0)
2. **Estate inventory** (Months 1-3)
   - Executor/heirs compile asset list
   - Professional valuation if needed (real estate, business)
3. **Tax declaration filed** (Month 3-4)
   - Submit to Vaud tax authorities (Administration cantonale des impôts)
   - Include asset inventory, valuations, relationship documentation
4. **Tax assessment issued** (Month 4-6)
   - Tax authority calculates tax owed
   - Issues official assessment (Taxation / Taxation)
5. **Payment due** (Month 6-12)
   - Standard deadline 6 months from assessment
   - Extensions possible

**Required documents**:
- Death certificate
- Will or inheritance certificate (Certificat d'héritier)
- Complete asset inventory
- Property valuations (real estate appraisals)
- Bank statements (date of death values)
- Business valuations (if applicable)
- Debt documentation (mortgages, loans)
- Relationship proof (birth certificates, marriage certificate, registered partnership certificate)

## Tax Planning Strategies for Vaud Residents

### Strategy 1: Relocate Before Death (If Feasible)

**Vaud resident considering relocation** to reduce tax burden on non-direct heirs:

**Current situation**: Vaud resident, CHF 1,000,000 estate to sibling
- Vaud tax: CHF 140,000

**Option A: Relocate to Schwyz** (tax-free canton)
- Schwyz tax: **CHF 0**
- **Savings: CHF 140,000**

**Requirements**:
- Genuine residence establishment (183+ days/year)
- Formal registration (Anmeldung)
- Lifestyle compatibility (Schwyz is rural, German-speaking)
- Several years before death (avoid artificial relocation challenge)

**Limitation**: Real estate remaining in Vaud still taxed by Vaud (situs rule)

### Strategy 2: Maximize Direct Descendant Exemptions

**Vaud offers 0% tax to direct descendants** - structure estate to maximize this:

**Scenario**: Wealthy individual wants to provide for unmarried partner

**Inefficient approach**:
- Leave CHF 500,000 to unmarried partner directly
- Tax: CHF 150,000 (30% effective rate)
- Net: CHF 350,000

**Efficient approach**:
- Leave CHF 500,000 to children (0% tax)
- Include letter of wishes requesting children provide CHF 500,000 to partner
- Children inherit CHF 500,000 tax-free, then gift to partner
- Partner receives CHF 500,000 (but will owe gift tax ~CHF 150,000 on the gift from children)

**Note**: This doesn't eliminate tax (gift from children to partner is still taxable) unless children gift gradually over many years. Better solution: **registered partnership** (0% tax directly).

### Strategy 3: Life Insurance for Tax Liquidity

**Problem**: Inheriting illiquid assets (business, real estate) with substantial tax bill

**Solution**: Life insurance policy payable to heirs to cover tax

**Example**:
- Vaud resident owns CHF 1,200,000 business
- Heir (nephew): Tax ~CHF 264,000
- **Life insurance**: CHF 300,000 policy
- At death: Nephew inherits business + receives CHF 300,000 insurance proceeds
- Uses insurance to pay CHF 264,000 tax, retains business intact

**Insurance taxation**: Life insurance proceeds are generally **tax-free** in Vaud if beneficiary is direct descendant or spouse. For other beneficiaries, insurance proceeds may be subject to inheritance tax (verify current Vaud rules).

### Strategy 4: Charitable Bequests (Partial Exemption)

**Vaud offers reduced rates for charitable organizations** (public benefit foundations, recognized charities):

**Charitable organization rates**:
- Recognized charities: **3-7%** (significantly lower than 13-50% for non-relatives)

**Strategy**: Leave disposable portion to charity instead of high-tax non-relative

**Example**:
- CHF 500,000 disposable portion
- **Option A**: Leave to friend → Tax ~CHF 150,000
- **Option B**: Leave to Swiss Red Cross → Tax ~CHF 15,000
- **Difference**: Friend receives CHF 350,000 vs. charity receives CHF 485,000

**Note**: Verify current charitable exemption status with Vaud tax authority (some organizations fully exempt, others reduced rates).

## Common Pitfalls in Vaud

### Pitfall 1: Assuming Registered Partnership is Automatic

**The mistake**: Living together as unmarried couple for years, assuming tax benefits apply.

**Why it's a problem**: **Cohabitation does NOT confer tax benefits** in Vaud. Unmarried partners are taxed as "non-relatives" (up to 50% rate).

**Example**:
- Couple lives together 20 years, never formally registers partnership
- Partner dies, leaving CHF 800,000 to surviving partner
- **Tax**: CHF 296,000 (~37% effective rate)
- **If registered partnership**: CHF 0 tax
- **Cost of not registering: CHF 296,000**

**The fix**: **Formally register partnership** (PACS / Partenariat enregistré) at Civil Registry Office. Simple administrative process, immediate tax benefits.

### Pitfall 2: Ignoring 5-Year Cumulation on Deathbed Gifts

**The mistake**: Making large gifts shortly before anticipated death to "avoid" inheritance tax.

**Why it's a problem**: Vaud adds back gifts made within **5 years before death** to estate for tax calculation.

**Example**:
- Year 1: Father (terminally ill) gifts CHF 500,000 to sibling (pays gift tax ~CHF 55,500)
- Year 2: Father dies, leaving CHF 500,000 estate to same sibling
- **Cumulative tax base**: CHF 1,000,000 (gift + inheritance)
- Total tax: CHF 140,000
- Already paid: -CHF 55,500
- **Additional tax at death**: CHF 84,500
- **Total tax**: CHF 140,000 (same as if no gift made)

**The fix**: Gift early (6+ years before death) or accept that recent gifts provide no tax savings in Vaud.

### Pitfall 3: Overlooking Real Estate Gains Tax on Property Transfers

**The mistake**: Planning inheritance tax but forgetting Vaud real estate gains tax.

**Why it's a problem**: Vaud levies **real estate capital gains tax** (Impôt sur les gains immobiliers) on property sales, including:
- Lifetime sales (to generate cash for gifting)
- Transfers to heirs (in some cases)

**Rates**: Progressive, up to 60%+ depending on gain and holding period

**Example**:
- Strategy: Sell Vaud chalet (purchased CHF 400,000, now worth CHF 800,000) to avoid inheritance tax
- Capital gain: CHF 400,000
- **Real estate gains tax**: ~CHF 80,000-120,000 (depending on holding period)
- Net proceeds: CHF 680,000-720,000
- **Then**: Financial assets taxed at residence canton (if lower tax canton, still net savings)

**The fix**: Calculate **both** real estate gains tax and inheritance tax when planning property strategies. Lifetime sale may still be beneficial, but gains tax reduces savings.

### Pitfall 4: Assuming Business Relief Exists in Vaud

**The mistake**: Planning for 80% business valuation relief (as available in Zurich, Bern).

**Why it's a problem**: **Vaud offers no business succession relief**. Full business value is taxable.

**Example**:
- Family business worth CHF 1,500,000 inherited by daughter (direct descendant)
- Vaud tax: **CHF 0** (direct descendant exemption)
- **OK in this case**

**But**:
- Same business inherited by nephew (no direct descendant available)
- Vaud tax: ~CHF 525,000 (~35% effective rate)
- Zurich (with 80% relief): Tax base CHF 300,000 → Tax ~CHF 45,000
- **Vaud penalty**: CHF 480,000 more expensive

**The fix**: Business owners without direct descendants in Vaud should consider:
- Lifetime gifting to reduce taxable business value
- Relocation to canton with business relief
- Corporate restructuring or sale before death

### Pitfall 5: Not Claiming Debt Deductions

**The mistake**: Paying tax on gross estate value without deducting mortgages and liabilities.

**Why it's a problem**: Vaud allows full deduction of debts, but heirs must **claim** these deductions in tax declaration.

**Example**:
- Inherit Vaud property valued at CHF 1,000,000
- Property has CHF 400,000 mortgage
- **If not claimed**: Tax calculated on CHF 1,000,000 → CHF 140,000 (sibling rate)
- **If properly claimed**: Tax calculated on CHF 600,000 → CHF 73,500
- **Savings from proper deduction**: CHF 66,500

**The fix**: Ensure estate inventory includes ALL liabilities (mortgages, loans, debts) with documentation. Submit with tax declaration.

## Limitations

### What This Framework Cannot Do

**This is an educational tool, not tax advice**:
- Vaud tax law changes regularly; rates and rules in this framework reflect 2025-2026 understanding
- Individual circumstances may have exceptions or special considerations
- Professional Vaud tax advice is **mandatory** for implementation

### When Professional Consultation Is Mandatory

**You MUST consult Vaud tax professionals for**:
- Large estates (CHF 200K+ where optimization has significant ROI)
- Real estate succession planning in Vaud
- Cross-border elements (foreign assets, foreign heirs)
- Business succession in Vaud (no business relief available, requires creative planning)
- Complex family situations (blended families, unmarried partners)
- Registered partnership registration process

### Verify Current Vaud Law

**Before implementing any strategy**:
- Confirm current Vaud tax rates with **Administration cantonale des impôts, Vaud**
- Verify gift tax cumulation period (currently 5 years)
- Check charitable organization exemption status
- Consult Vaud-specialist tax advisor for estates >CHF 200,000
